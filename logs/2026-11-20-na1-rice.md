## What happened
- **#71-#74, the empire planner tuned.** An empty army's morale Give was refused by the cartridge and wasted turns at three sites; closing them was the first significant gain (57 → 76). Donors gated by MIRR, the strike season priced, hire-and-Move, and any SAFE fief funding the unsafe ones first: **92 of 340**.
- **#75, the stall audited:** the ready striker held its line and did not strike, blocked by the whole-empire gate, the prize left exposed, and its own Assign turn. Assigning ahead lost games: the split is also the fief's defence.
- **#76-#80, the double attack** (Chris: *result = unsafe might need a double attack*). Follow-up Moves that mend the prize lost at every gate. Past the header wall the problem is the win, not the hold. The battle lab measured two waves: a weak 1/99 wave feeds the defender, a commander-only first wave does not, and two waves cost more than one. The second waves timed out because **the defender bribed them with the first wave's lost gold**; the held-back commander bribing away the spare gold (Chris's dump) won 52 of 52. In the game, two waves are priced right but rare by geometry: the cap almost never binds in the stalled games.
- **#81, Uesugi's openings** (Chris read his 20 games): the fixed Echigo battle plan (#44) was never in the whole-game runs. Wired in: 7 → 11 of 20, year-1 invasions 8 → 1.
- **#82, rice** (Chris: *total econ is the better measure; we lose with the bigger economy, so we are not spending well*). 51 of 147 lost games out-earned every rival combined. **40 of 78 lost attacks went out with under 2 days of rice**, starved on day 1, and each army joined the defender whole. The last rival grew on our own men until it capped. Carrying 10 days of ration: **93 → 135 of 240** (z +5.7), games lost at 16 of 17 fiefs 13 → 1.
- **#83-#84, rice logistics and promotion.** Rice to the attack line and a maxed fief's rice feed cost hire turns, even men-first. The proven rules were promoted (`--preset best`, rice and the Echigo plan by default): 140.
- **#85-#86, revolts and the later invasions.** A capital revolts only under morale 45 (the roll cannot fail at 45+), apart from a 1-in-1,000 fluke at about 5% a game; a morale guard ends the rest. 8 of 21 later invasion deaths were the defender on 1 rice: the Summer gift checked the share line before the gift, but the AI's gate reads min(rice, men). Keeping just the rice the share line needs: **149 of 240**.
- **#87, the turn-2 invasions.** AI battle bribes cost about 140 men a game (none at morale above 200): not worth a turn. Hojo's turn 1: COMING, short of his line, and the ladder raised the tax; two attacks came that season. A threatened one-fief opening now hires first: **153**.
- **#88, the big-economy losses.** The Fall harvest clamps gold and rice at the header: the lost big empires threw away **10-22% of their gold and 42-63% of their rice** every year. Spending it on each fief's Summer turn (men first, rice to morale): **158**, wins two years sooner.
- **#89, Chris's 3 → 0 siege:** repeated waves that do not feed a target past the wall wear it to 15-35% a wave, but the wall is rare once we stop feeding the last rival; off.
- **#90, the last fief again:** the source had to keep rice >= all its men; the AI's gate needs only the share line. **162 of 240.**
- **#91, Chris's rice drain, in the lab:** the defender's field units hunt a keep-away force down; fighting the hunters back, three 600-man waves and a 1,200 finisher starve 19 of 21 capped garrisons, capitals too. A starving lord flees if he has another fief. AI fiefs keep 10+ days of rice, so it is an opportunist only after earlier battles. Not wired in.
- **#92, Uesugi and Hojo read game by game** (Chris's method): 9-fief empires lost an interior capital sitting at 0 men; a capital floor of 150: **173**.
- **#93-#94, risk priced, not gated.** The prize's income had been priced at the conquest's tax (45% low), offsetting the price's missing risk; fixed alone it lost. Weighting the attack's flows by outcome, with hazards measured from the games and calibrated in two rounds (chosen COMING prizes are lost 24-47% a year), replaces the SAFE-prize gate: **178**, the first conquest two years sooner. Chris: *when inevitability is not there, gamble on our outs.*
- **#95, the triage** (`na1sim.triage`) and Tokugawa's morale dead zone (a striker at morale 45-55 could neither hire nor lift it): **180**. Against the 92 player: saved 94, lost 6; big empires no longer invaded (10 → 0).
- **#96, the lord may Flee** (the ROM's human verb, $AAA7): lethal-only, with a gold burn only when losing. Correct and rarely reachable; off.
- **#97, Asakura's output trap and an econ lab.** Our player never develops the land; at output 0 the cartridge wipes loyalty and wealth and refuses gifts. `na1sim.econ_lab` (one fief, the real commands, a long horizon): gifts dominate and their timing is worth as much as their target; a wiped fief cannot recover alone while it carries an army. Next: a disaster-cliff recovery.
- **#98, disaster cliffs:** a recovery (Grow first, donors fund the Grow and any fief's coming upkeep) works, but only 3 fiefs in 240 games ever reached output 0; off, as insurance.
- **#99, the 180 run read by start:** the late revolts are all the 1-in-1,000 draw (morale and charisma 45+). Hojo's lord died in a 9-man commander (an attack's 1/99 split) with an owned fief to run to; Flee fixed to count units in reach and to use the ROM's own flight test for a lord: **183 of 240**.
- **#100, the defensive Assign** (100/0 for a capital not SAFE) costs more turns than it saves; Flee covers the case.
- **#101-#103, the early deaths traced turn by turn** (Chris: *we only have to play 3-4 years*): the ladder's tax rung crept two points a turn at the cliff (a 2-point raise drains 0); a minimum step of 5: **185 of 240**. Hiring first for an unpicked COMING fief collapsed the opening (88); jumping the tax at 70 changes nothing (the rung already does), at 59 it costs.
