# Plato's Ship of State — Charisma Over Competence
> Video essay: democracy systematically selects the confident amateur (*doxa*) over the cautious expert (*episteme*), repeats it every round, and ends by handing power to the ultimate sophist, the strongman. ## Argument Structure (what was actually said)

1. **Faces predict winners.** Todorov et al. (Princeton, 2005): 1-second exposure to unfamiliar foreign candidates → ~70% correct winner prediction. Antonakis & Dalgas (2009): children 5–13 picking a "boat captain" predicted French parliamentary winners at adult-equivalent rates. [0:18–1:41]
2. **Plato saw it live.** Athens: Socrates' execution, the Sicilian expedition sold by Alcibiades. [1:43–3:25]
3. **Ship of State.** The confident amateur beats the hedging navigator because "confidence feels better than competence"; the ship sinks. [3:31–4:57]
4. **Modern specimens.** Nixon–Kennedy 1960 (radio vs TV verdicts), Brexit ("Take back control" vs economists' reports). "Charismatic simplicity beats knowledgeable complexity every single time." [4:59–6:21]
5. **The qualification asymmetry.** Doctors, pilots and barbers are licensed; heads of state are not. [6:21–6:55]
6. **Sophists = the selected type.** Election skills (charisma, slogans, looking good on camera) ≠ governing skills (trade-offs, uncertainty, "no good solutions, only less bad ones"). *"Say any of that in a campaign and you lose."* → a systematic mismatch. [7:02–9:26]
7. **Doxa vs episteme ≈ Dunning–Kruger ≈ Kahneman System 1/2.** Availability bias and a preference for coherence over accuracy make scapegoating work. Height (taller candidate wins 58%, 67% with a 3+ inch gap), deeper voice, attractiveness. [9:33–14:25]
8. **The self-destruct sequence (Republic VIII).** Freedom → chaos → sophists escalate promises → exhaustion → strongman ("ultimate sophist") → "democracy dies with applause." Rome: Caesar → Augustus keeps the republican façade. [14:31–16:51]
9. **Why it recurs: every filter selects wrong.** Who runs (power-seekers), who succeeds (say-anything), who rises (persuaders). Experts won't lie, so they lose, *"until those amateurs destroy the very system that elevated them."* Earlier: populations end up unable to "tell the difference between demagoguery and leadership anymore." [16:54–17:33]
10. **Plato's cure:** philosopher-kings chosen by testing and education, not votes. The surgery analogy. "Elitist? Yes. Logical? Uncomfortably so." [17:37–19:12]
11. **Hedge:** Churchill's "worst except all the others", plus citizens' assemblies, ranked choice, sortition (Ireland, Athens), all called "marginal." Ends on evolutionary mismatch: tribal hardware, complex-policy game; "escaping the trap might be impossible." [19:14–23:24]

## Discussion seeds

1. **Which ratchet?** The video describes at least three one-way mechanisms and never names any of them: (a) **selection decay**, where each round erodes the electorate's ability to tell a leader from a demagogue (#9); (b) **promise escalation**, where sophists outbid each other (#8); (c) **the exhaustion door**, where chaos → strongman is irreversible (#8). Which one is yours, or is it a fourth?
2. **New, or the selection layer of the small-pain ratchet?** *"Say [trade-offs, no good solutions] in a campaign and you lose"* is intolerance of small pain showing up in who gets picked: the voter punishes the candidate who *names* the pain. [History Repeats](../../notes/history-repeats-small-pain-tolerance.md) already has "the enabler's incentive" and "masterly inaction earns no credit." Does this video add a mechanism, or is it the same variable with a selection filter bolted on?
3. **The cure is the disease.** The video's remedy (a competence filter, philosopher-kings) is the proposition you argued **con** in [rep-competence-tests-prep](./rep-competence-tests-prep.md): the filter moves power to whoever writes the filter. Peikoff's convergence argument ([12-plato-politics](../philosophy/tangents/history-of-philosophy/12-plato-politics.md) §VI) says Plato and the Sophists both end in tyranny. The video names the strongman as democracy's *failure* and then prescribes a guardian class as the *fix*.
4. **Scope, not selection?** Every one of the video's examples is about *who* holds the job. It never asks how big the job is. If the mismatch hurts in proportion to what the office decides, the lever may be [scope](../philosophy/morality/scope-confusion.md) (the SAVE-Act move: "restrict scope, not the vote").
5. **Grounding check on the evidence.** Todorov and Antonakis–Dalgas are real, replicated-ish results. The **Nixon–Kennedy radio/TV split is contested** (it rests on a small, unrepresentative radio sample; Vancil & Pendell 1987). **Dunning–Kruger** as stated ("least expertise → most confidence") is the pop version, disputed as partly a statistical artifact. The claim "every single time" is contradicted by the video's own counts (58%, 70%). *To verify before the page leans on any of these.*
6. **Symmetric grounding.** The examples line up one-sidedly: Brexit Leave = slogan, Remain = expertise. Remain also ran on a simple fear frame, and "experts" were on the losing side of real calls. Does the mechanism survive when it's applied to *expert-consensus* failures (the Sicilian-expedition equivalent run by credentialed people)?

## Discussion

### Round 1: Chris names the ratchet (2026-09-24)

**The video's own blind spot, Brexit.** Chris: the video treats Leave as plainly the wrong choice, assuming the economy would have done better under Remain, and never argues it. That counterfactual is contested. (It is also a specimen of the problem below: even afterward, nobody can observe the road not taken.)

**Rejects the cure.** Chris: *"I am not a fan of his (or Plato's!) solutions."* This is consistent with his con case in [rep-competence-tests-prep](./rep-competence-tests-prep.md).

**The ratchet, in Chris's words:**
> *"The ratchet is that we offload our governing in order to not have to fully understand the problems, but this requires the ability to pick properly to self-correct."*

So the claim is: **delegation is how we avoid understanding, and delegation only self-corrects if the delegator can still judge the delegate.** The video's charisma-over-competence story is the *symptom*. The mechanism is the delegation bargain itself.

**Complexity is the driver.** Chris: *"Complexity is probably the right answer here."* The electrician and lawyer contrast:
> *"When you hire an electrician or a lawyer the pass/fail criteria is fairly obvious. And tbh, you might not know immediately that the electrician gave poor service because the lights went on! And this is worse with a politician, as the effects of bad decisions might not be felt for a long time."*

- **Pass/fail visibility:** the electrician has an obvious test (the lights come on), yet even that test can pass a latent defect.
- **Feedback latency:** a politician's bad decision may not be felt for years, so the evaluation arrives after the next election, or never.

**The verdict:**
> *"I think the ratchet is still there. We get the government we 'deserve', and often this means a poor one, as we don't understand what is being asked."*

**Claude's extension (offered, then tested in Round 2):** delegation is one-way if it *manufactures* the complexity that makes the next round of delegation necessary. The electrician is gradeable on three axes the politician lacks: **repetition** (a track record across many customers), **counterfactual** (a second electrician can open the wall; nobody can rerun Brexit), and **latency**. A voter has no lower-level artifact to check the delegate against. The direction this points: make the voyages gradeable (federalism, exit, side-by-side jurisdictions) rather than pick better captains.

### Round 2: a mixture, and the misattribution click (2026-09-24)

**Can't vs won't: both.** Chris: *"I think it is a mixture. There are many that do try to understand and vote well, but many others don't."* The face-test result at the start of the video describes the second group.

**Federalism: yes, but the tide runs the other way.** Chris: federalism has real benefits for exactly this reason, *"though we have seen a great deal of effort to consolidate power instead of spreading it out."*

**One ratchet among several, and a big one.** Chris: *"I don't think this is the only ratchet, but it is a big one."*

**The lag is decades.** Chris: *"We are now just seeing the effects of programs made 20, 40, 60, 80 and even 100 years ago, if not more."*

**The misattribution click.** Chris:
> *"It seems easy for politicians to convince enough people it either wasn't their fault and/or they can fix it. Govt makes problem, now we need more govt to fix that problem!"*

**Chris's placement of the video:** *"I think that this was more just a large cause than actually answering the ratchet effect completely."* The video names a big **input** to the ratchet, not the whole mechanism.

**Specimen, the affordability crisis:** Chris: *"I see this a lot when I see many complaining about the 'affordability crisis', and yet not willing to solve the inflation issues caused by overspending on government programs."*

**Assembled loop (Claude, from vault parts; Chris accepted the framing in Round 3):** delegation → lag → **misattribution** → consolidation ([exit-cost ratchet](../philosophy/morality/legal-theory/government-formation.md)) → accumulated complexity (Tainter) → back to delegation. The one link the vault was missing is misattribution. The [small-pain note](../../notes/history-repeats-small-pain-tolerance.md) says the feedback loop *never closes* on the actor; here it closes **inverted**, so failure becomes the case for more authority.

## Vault Position (from the discussion)
- **The ratchet is delegation, not charisma.** We hand off governing so we don't have to understand it, and that only self-corrects while we can still grade the delegate (Chris). Charisma-over-competence is how grading fails, not the mechanism itself.
- **The one-way click is misattribution.** Lagged, counterfactual-free failure gets blamed elsewhere and becomes the case for more of the same. Promoted to the thesis page **[Failure as Mandate](../philosophy/dynamics/failure-as-mandate.md)**.
- **Reject the cure.** A competence filter moves power to whoever writes the filter ([rep-competence-tests-prep](./rep-competence-tests-prep.md); Peikoff's Plato–Sophist convergence). The better lever is making outcomes gradeable (federalism, exit, side-by-side jurisdictions), though consolidation runs the other way.
- **The video demonstrates its own problem:** it asserts Brexit was the wrong call on an unobservable counterfactual.
