# The Load-Bearing Word
> Every resolution has one word carrying the argumentative weight. Find it, pin it, and most of the debate is already decided — because the side that gets its reading on the record has quietly set the win condition for both.

**⭐ Fourth type — ASYMMETRIC LATITUDE (added 2026-09-03, two specimens).** Chris's formulation, from the [Iran-war round](./wordwar-r2-iran-war-summerhays-cruz.md): *"he says they can't be defined, and then defines 1 very narrow and the other very broad."*

> **Claim the resolution's terms cannot be fixed — then quietly fix each one in the direction that helps: the term you must satisfy narrowly, the term you get to exploit broadly.** The disclaimer is what makes it work: with nothing stated, there is no definition for an opponent to attack, and the asymmetry between the two readings never has to be justified as a joint principle.

Where **inflation** widens a term to vacuity, **severance** narrows it to dodge evidence, and **conclusion-loading** builds the verdict into it, this move does *both* widening and narrowing at once and hides the pair behind a refusal. It is the affirmative's definitional prerogative exercised while being disclaimed.

| Specimen | Broadened | Narrowed | The disclaimer |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Iran war](./wordwar-r2-iran-war-summerhays-cruz.md) — Summerhays | *"the Iran war"* — backdated to Oct 7 **and** extended into the future | *"best interest"* — long-term only, so every present cost is a sacrifice | *"it's intrinsically subjective"* |
| [Feminism rerun](./wordwar-feminism-rerun-david-tareyak.md) — David S. | *"helping"* — any instance whatsoever counts | *"feminism"* — held to Merriam-Webster | *"I've just said it's open to interpretation"* |

**The second specimen was already in the vault, named and homeless.** That page flagged **asymmetric latitude** — *maximum looseness on "helping," minimum on "feminism," both motivated the same way and not jointly principled* — as the round's strongest unstated charge, and filed the round's *burden* half under [The Negative's Easy Burden](../philosophy/tangents/the-negatives-easy-burden.md). That placement stands: burden-annexation and asymmetric latitude are **separate moves that co-occurred**. This entry gives the definitional half the home it lacked.

**The counter, and it is cheap in both cases:** demand that the latitude be **jointly principled**. Whatever standard licenses the broad reading must be applied to the narrow one — and an operative term declared undefinable is not load-bearing for its *proponent* either, so a burden left unspecified cannot have been met.

> **Chris:** "often topics hinge on degree type words.. even in my own debate, we both crystallized that we needed to nail down what **'sign'** meant, and when I got a concession, it made the rest of it much easier… 'America should build more data centers' … the point here is that **'should' is doing the heavy lifting**, and this is typical of debate topics."

## The claim

A resolution is a sentence, but it is not evenly weighted. Almost always **one term** decides what would count as winning — and it is usually not the topic word. *"Is masculinity in crisis?"* is not about masculinity; it is about **crisis**. *"Is the algorithm fair?"* is not about algorithms; it is about **fair**. *"Poverty in America is a sign of exploitation"* is not about poverty or exploitation; it is about **sign**.

Two consequences:

1. **Whoever pins that word sets the win condition.** Not by trickery — by supplying the only thing that makes the proposition checkable.
2. **Rounds that never pin it cannot be won on the merits**, only on delivery. This is the mechanism underneath the Word War bracket's [layer-1 deaths](./word-war-debate-series.md#cross-round-findings): a layer-1 failure just *is* a failure to locate and fix the load-bearing word.

## Part 1 — The four families, and the test each one needs

### Degree words — *too, enough, in crisis, works, fair*

The most common family and the most slippery, because they smuggle in a threshold nobody states. *Too* sensitive compared to what? *Works* by what measure?

**Failure mode:** a standard with **no failure condition**. Both sides bid a threshold that flatters their case and neither can be refuted, because nothing was specified that could turn out false.

**The repair — operationalise against function.** Name the purpose the thing exists to serve, then ask whether it still serves it. That converts an unwinnable vibes question into a checkable one, because it supplies the missing failure condition.

| Resolution | Term | Operationalisation |
|---|---|---|
| Is masculinity in crisis? | *crisis* | a system no longer functions for the purpose it was built |
| Are we too sensitive? | *too much* | past the point where it destroys what it exists to serve |
| Is the algorithm fair? | *fair* | the tool meets the goals of its owner |
| Does therapy work for men? | *works* | achieves its stated goal — not *exceeds* it, not *produced some learning* |

The therapy round shows both ways to get this wrong at once: the Aff set the bar at **passive return** (*"money works for you"* — the patient should have to do nothing), the Neg at **any learning whatsoever**. One standard nothing could meet; one nothing could fail.

### Quantifiers — *impossible, all, never, always, any*

**Failure mode:** the burdens are set by the word and both sides argue as if they were symmetric.

**The repair — read the burden off the quantifier.** A universal claim is refuted by **one counterexample**; the affirmative owes necessity across every case. In the [wealth round](./wordwar-rich-without-luck-constable-aftermath.md) the resolution was *"it is **impossible** to get rich without luck"* — so the Neg needed exactly one clean counterexample and never claimed the entitlement, while the Aff invoked a sample-of-one principle that does nothing for a universal.

### Evidential words — *sign, evidence of, indicates, reflects*

**Failure mode:** an evidential claim gets argued as a causal one. *"X is a sign of Y"* is much weaker than *"Y causes X"* — and much easier to break, because signs have structural requirements that causes don't.

**The repair — a sign must (a) vary with what it signals and (b) mark a departure from a baseline.** From [Chris's own poverty prep](./poverty-exploitation-prep.md): the official poverty rate moved 1.5pp across four decades while consumption poverty collapsed, so the metric is insensitive *by construction*; and poverty is the universal human baseline, so wealth is the thing requiring explanation.

**This is the page's positive specimen.** It is the one case where the pinning was done deliberately and early, and it worked exactly as advertised:

> **Chris:** "we both crystallized that we needed to nail down what 'sign' meant, and **when I got a concession, it made the rest of it much easier**."

### Modal words — *should, ought, must*

**Failure mode:** a practical question is argued as a moral one, when the modal has no room to range.

**The repair — ask whether the thing is a live choice at all, and if not, find the real decision variable.** Chris's affirmative prep on *"America should build more data centers"*:

> **Chris:** "this is not a moral claim.. building data centers is a market force, **they are going to get built anyway**, all you can do is control ***where*** they get built."

If the outcome is going to occur regardless of the vote, *should* cannot range over **whether**; it can only range over **where, by whom, and under what constraints**. The resolution names a decision variable that isn't the one actually in play — and locating the real one is the whole affirmative case. Same instrument, entirely different family from a degree word.

## Part 2 — The two ways the word gets manipulated

Once you know which term is load-bearing, both dirty moves become legible. They are the **same operation in opposite directions**: adjust the term's *extension* until the resolution can't be lost.

| Move | Direction | Effect | Specimens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inflation** | Widen until no counterexample survives | The resolution becomes **trivially true** and stops meaning anything — you win the sentence and lose the argument | *luck* → "essentially anything that happens" ([wealth](./wordwar-rich-without-luck-constable-aftermath.md)); *boomer* → a disposition, "spiritually boomer" ([boomers](./wordwar-boomers-economy-jones-meyers.md)) |
| **Severance** | Narrow until the opponent's evidence falls outside | You **concede the substance** and win on scope — every example is granted as damning and ruled off-topic | *masculinity* severed from biological sex ([masculinity](./wordwar-masculinity-crisis-owlish-garcia.md)); *algorithm* severed from human moderation ([algorithm](./wordwar-algorithm-fair-cruz-hamm.md)); *culture* severed from circumstances ([fragility](./wordwar-fragile-culture-turner-bourdeau.md)); *therapy culture* severed from CBT ([therapy culture](./wordwar-therapy-culture-ouedrago-smith.md)) |
| **Loading** | Leave the extension alone; build the **conclusion into the definition** | Accepting the term concedes the resolution — the proposition becomes true by stipulation rather than by argument | *therapy culture := the* ***over****use of therapy* ([therapy culture](./wordwar-therapy-culture-ouedrago-smith.md)) — *"over"* already asserts excess, which is the whole question |

**The tell for inflation:** substitute the offered definition back into the resolution and read it aloud. *"It is impossible to get rich without anything happening"* is unfalsifiable and worthless — and its rhetorical force is being borrowed from a narrower reading the speaker is no longer defending.

**The tell for loading:** the proposed definition contains an evaluative word the resolution was supposed to *establish* — *over*use, *excessive*, *proper*, *genuine*, *real*. Ouedrago's refusal is the model counter, and it takes one sentence: *"He's loading a lot of normative language there. **Overuse** implies that it's over — means it's not good… so obviously I won't accept the definition **because it by definition has a negative connotation**."* Then supply a descriptive substitute rather than merely objecting; he offered *"the generalisation of therapeutic ideas in society,"* and it was accepted. Note this is the **cheapest** of the three moves to defeat and the easiest to miss, because a loaded definition sounds like a definition rather than an argument.

**The tell for severance:** the opponent starts *agreeing* with your evidence while denying its relevance. *"I would agree that that's unfair, but that's not part of the idea of an algorithm."* The counter is never to accept the boundary and argue inside it — it is to attack the boundary directly.

**In both cases the fix is the same:** get the definition on the record **before** the evidence is presented, so the boundary can't be redrawn once it becomes inconvenient.

## Part 3 — Operating rules

1. **Identify the load-bearing word before you prepare anything else.** It is rarely the topic noun. Ask: *which single word, if my opponent's reading is adopted, do I lose regardless of my evidence?*
2. **Pin it early and get the concession on the record** — but **pinning late still beats not pinning at all.** The [fragility round](./wordwar-fragile-culture-turner-bourdeau.md) defines its term in the *second half* and still organises everything after it, because the opponent keeps contesting the definition rather than accepting and moving on; its twin, the [sensitivity round](./wordwar-too-sensitive-guptill-tejada.md), never pinned and produced no proposition to argue about at all. The cost of delay is the time already spent — a third of the fragility round went to a hypothetical that led nowhere, and the Neg named the omission in his own closing. This is a prep task, not an in-round one; see the caveat below.
3. **Quote the concession back verbatim.** Once the load-bearing word is fixed, opponents concede its components without noticing — *"they're going to be a product of whatever that environment is"*, *"I agree that internal locus of control breeds more resilience."* Restating your own thesis invites a rebuttal; **replaying their sentence forecloses the retreat**, because they must now either affirm it or explain the reversal on the record. The fragility round's Aff had two such concessions in hand and used neither, which is the difference between winning the argument and being seen to win it.
4. **Match the test to the family.** Degree → supply a failure condition. Quantifier → read the burden. Evidential → variation and baseline. Modal → is this even a choice?
5. **Settle only to the precision the resolution requires, then stop.** The [wealth round](./wordwar-rich-without-luck-constable-aftermath.md) burned twenty minutes on how much money counts as "rich" when the resolution asked about the *mechanism of acquisition* and no outcome depended on the threshold. Over-definition is its own failure.
6. **Watch the extension in both directions** (Part 2).
7. **An adopted definition that isn't enforced is worse than none** — it manufactures the appearance of settled ground. In the [sensitivity round](./wordwar-too-sensitive-guptill-tejada.md) a definition was agreed, immediately argued past, and silently replaced mid-round.

**The caveat that keeps this honest:**

> **Chris:** "often it is hard to know what the 'winning argument' really is during the debate, and the better debaters come in with a **strategy** based on this."

Identifying the load-bearing word looks obvious from a transcript and is genuinely hard in the room. That is precisely why this is **preparation work**. The contenders who look sharp are not thinking faster live; they arrived having already decided which hill they were fighting on. Two rounds in the bracket were lost by debaters who *named the winning frame in their first few minutes* and then abandoned it — not from carelessness, but because nothing had told them in advance that it was the one that mattered.

## Open Questions

1. **Are there load-bearing words outside these four families?** Comparatives (*better than*), counterfactuals (*would have*), and collective nouns (*we*, *society*) are candidates — the sensitivity round spent real time on *society* without it mattering.
2. **Does a resolution ever have two?** *"Impossible to get rich without luck"* carries both a quantifier and a degree word, and they were attacked separately. Whether that is one term with two aspects or genuinely two load-bearing words is unresolved.
3. **Is pinning always to the pinner's advantage?** The assumption here is yes. But a definition precise enough to be checkable can also be checkable *against you* — worth finding a specimen where pinning backfired.
