# Formal Debate Concepts — A Portable Inventory
> Competitive debate spent a century naming the moves. Informal debate uses none of the vocabulary and re-derives the moves badly, or misses them entirely. This is the inventory: each concept, what it means, why it **globalizes** past the format that produced it, and the specimen where the vault watched someone need it and not have it.

**Links:** [Online Debates](./README.md), [The Load-Bearing Word](./the-load-bearing-word.md), [The Negative's Easy Burden](../philosophy/tangents/the-negatives-easy-burden.md), [Easy to Critique, Hard to Build](../philosophy/tangents/easy-to-critique-hard-to-build.md), [Word War series hub](./word-war-debate-series.md)

## Why import it

> **Chris:** *"it is obvious this format doesn't follow policy debate rules, but I do think most of the concepts can globalize to most debates, and having definitions and inventory for these is a good thing."*

The occasion was the [women's-suffrage round](./wordwar-r2-womens-suffrage-tejeda-aftermath.md), where the decisive move was **free, available all night, and unknown to both debaters.** The affirmative's entire case was an implementation objection — *repeal would be a steep hill, you still have to explain how you'd take the right away* — and one word disposes of it. The negative sensed it, said *"that's why it doesn't fit the topic"* four different ways, and never landed it, because he was reaching for a concept he did not have a name for.

**Checked, not assumed:** a search of both round-2 transcripts for *fiat*, *topicality*, *inherency*, *counterplan*, *presumption* and *non-unique* returns **zero occurrences**. The vocabulary is entirely absent from the bracket.

## ⚠ The caution that keeps this page honest

**Import these as tools of *analysis*, not of *prediction*.** They tell you what a debater should have said. They do **not** tell you who wins, because the scorer here is an audience ballot rather than a judge flowing arguments — and [finding 6](./word-war-debate-series.md#cross-round-findings) established that technique tuned for a flowing judge **inverts** under an audience vote: dropped points don't score as conceded, density reads as noise, and a technically-won round can lose by forty points.

So: use the inventory to say *what was true of the argument*. Keep [finding 17](./word-war-debate-series.md#cross-round-findings) for *what the room will do*. Conflating the two is how this page would go wrong.

## The inventory

### 1. Fiat

**Definition.** The convention that an affirmative plan is *stipulated* to be enacted. The question is whether it **should** be adopted, not whether it **would** pass. So *"Congress would never do this"* is not a disadvantage.

**Why it globalizes.** It separates two questions that ordinary argument constantly welds together: **is this right** and **could this happen**. An implementation objection is not a truth objection, and **irreversibility is not justification** — if *"we can't take it back"* counted as an argument *for* an arrangement, every entrenched arrangement would be self-justifying.

**Specimen — [women's suffrage](./wordwar-r2-womens-suffrage-tejeda-aftermath.md).** The affirmative's whole case is that repeal is socially impossible. One sentence answers it: *if I win the resolution, the franchise changes by fiat; how it would be accomplished is not on the table.* Chris's call, and the read of the round.

### 2. Topicality

**Definition.** Whether the case actually falls within the resolution. A brilliant case for a proposition nobody proposed loses.

**Why it globalizes.** It is the precise, checkable version of *"that's not what we're arguing about"* — and it is a **prior** question, resolved before the merits.

**Specimens.** [Suffrage](./wordwar-r2-womens-suffrage-tejeda-aftermath.md) — the affirmative argued feasibility, not entitlement, and the negative kept gesturing at it without the word. [Data centers](./wordwar-r2-data-centers-medeiros-horseradish.md) — the moderator's crossfire question (*would "the powers that be" be less successful if only men voted*) was itself off-resolution, and a debater said so on air. Compare [finding 4](./word-war-debate-series.md#cross-round-findings)'s *topic-talk vs. resolution-work*, which is this concept discovered from scratch and named worse.

### 3. Presumption and burden of proof

**Definition.** Presumption sits with the status quo; the affirmative must overcome it and therefore carries the burden. Formal systems **compensate** the heavier burden with structure — the affirmative speaks first *and* last, as a prosecutor does.

**Why it globalizes.** It explains why the sceptic's posture is cheap, and why informal debate hands him an unearned edge by removing the compensation without removing the asymmetry.

**Store:** fully developed in [The Negative's Easy Burden](../philosophy/tangents/the-negatives-easy-burden.md) — not duplicated here.

### 4. Stock issues

**Definition.** The checklist a policy affirmative must satisfy: **harm** (is there a problem?), **inherency** (is it built into the status quo, or would the status quo fix it?), **solvency** (does the plan actually fix it?), **topicality**, and — for the negative — **disadvantages**.

**Why it globalizes.** It is a completeness test. Most informal cases satisfy *harm* loudly and never touch *inherency* or *solvency*, which is where they are weakest.

**Specimen — [data centers](./wordwar-r2-data-centers-medeiros-horseradish.md).** The negative's case was almost entirely **harm**, with nothing on inherency or solvency, which is why it kept dissolving under the affirmative's questioning.

### 5. Inherency

**Definition.** Is the harm *intrinsic* to the status quo, or contingent on something adjustable?

**Why it globalizes.** It is the sharp form of *"is that a reason against the thing, or a reason to do the thing differently?"*

**Specimen — [data centers](./wordwar-r2-data-centers-medeiros-horseradish.md).** The affirmative's pre-registered filter — *is this a reason not to build, or a reason to build differently?* — **is an inherency test**, invented on the spot and run four-plus times. He had the concept and not the word, and it still carried the round. Best evidence on this page that the inventory is worth having: an untrained debater reconstructing a named move under time pressure.

### 6. Non-uniqueness

**Definition.** A disadvantage that obtains regardless of the plan does not differentiate, and so does not argue against it.

**Why it globalizes.** It is the test for whether an objection is *about this* or *about everything*.

**Specimen — [data centers](./wordwar-r2-data-centers-medeiros-horseradish.md).** *"Then we shouldn't build hospitals or factories either."* Correctly run. The negative eventually found the right answer — those have larger benefits per acre, so it is a **ratio** claim, not a harms claim — but only in his closing.

### 7. Counterplan

**Definition.** The negative advocates a *different* action rather than defending the status quo. It **concedes the harm** and contests the agent or the method.

**Why it globalizes.** It clarifies what you have taken on. Proposing an alternative is not free: you now own its solvency, and you have granted the problem.

**Specimen — [data centers](./wordwar-r2-data-centers-medeiros-horseradish.md).** *Build manufacturing instead*, and *the government/DARPA should hold strategic AI, not private firms*, are both counterplans. Neither was framed as one, so the negative never noticed he had conceded the harm and never defended his alternative's solvency.

### 8. Kritik

**Definition.** An attack on the framing, assumptions or actors rather than on the plan. To function it needs a **link** (this case specifically), an **impact**, and an **alternative**.

**Why it globalizes.** It names the most common failure in online debate: the motive/character argument fired with no link and no alternative.

**Specimen — [data centers](./wordwar-r2-data-centers-medeiros-horseradish.md).** The negative opens the floor with Thiel, Yarvin and *"they want to undo America"* — a kritik of the actors, missing both components. The affirmative's repeated *"even if true, why does that mean we shouldn't build more?"* is exactly the standard demand for a **link**, and it went unanswered three times. **This is why the motive case produced nothing**, and it is a better diagnosis than "conspiracy talk doesn't play."

### 9. Turns — link turn and impact turn

**Definition.** Taking the opponent's argument and running it as your own: the plan *avoids* the harm (link turn), or the supposed harm is actually **good** (impact turn).

**Why it globalizes.** The strongest available reply to a harm list, and almost never used outside formal rooms.

**Specimen.** Chris on the water disadvantage: *"pushing our society to invent more and better water and energy resources is a bonus to this wave, not a hindrance"* — a clean **impact turn**, and the seed of [Easy to Critique, Hard to Build](../philosophy/tangents/easy-to-critique-hard-to-build.md). Note **closed-loop cooling is the turn made physical**, and it surfaced inside the round without either debater claiming it.

### 10. Severance and definitional narrowing

**Definition.** Shrinking the operative term mid-round so the evidence no longer reaches it.

**Store:** catalogued with its siblings (inflation, conclusion-loading) in [The Load-Bearing Word](./the-load-bearing-word.md).

**Specimen — [suffrage](./wordwar-r2-womens-suffrage-tejeda-aftermath.md).** The negative severs *should* into *must*, demanding proof that suffrage is **necessary**. A burden-play offered in place of the topicality objection he actually wanted.

### 11. Dropped arguments

**Definition.** Before a judge flowing the round, an unanswered argument is **conceded**. That is what makes speed and density rational.

**Why it globalizes — mostly as a warning.** The rule is **local to the scorer.** Under an audience ballot nothing is dropped, because nobody is keeping a list.

**Specimen — [Charsky vs. Bunn](./wordwar-ai-making-people-dumber-charsky-bunn.md).** [Finding 6](./word-war-debate-series.md#cross-round-findings)'s technique/format mismatch is precisely a debater still playing by this rule in a room that isn't. *(He won anyway, by 58 — which is the bracket's best evidence that argument quality can beat legibility.)*

### 12. Switch-side debating

**Definition.** Debaters are assigned sides and expected to argue either. Standard, and defended as the core pedagogical value of the activity.

**Why it globalizes — as a dependency, not a technique.** Switch-side only works **because the rest of the scaffolding carries it.** Fiat, presumption, stock issues and an allocated burden mean a debater argues *the case*, and the judge scores the case rather than the man. Remove all of it and hand the decision to an audience, and nothing distinguishes **arguing** a position from **holding** one.

> **Chris:** *"I would argue this format is harder than policy debate in being able to swap sides of the argument."*

**Specimen — [suffrage](./wordwar-r2-womens-suffrage-tejeda-aftermath.md).** A declared devil's advocate, scored by a room, on a sincerity he was never supposed to have.

## Using it — the four questions worth asking of any round

1. **Is the case topical?** Does it argue *the resolution*, or a neighbouring proposition?
2. **Is the objection inherent and unique?** Or does it apply to every member of the class — and is it a reason to do the thing *differently*?
3. **Who holds the burden, and was it earned or annexed?** Both sides claiming the easy burden is a collapsed round, not a hard one.
4. **Is the plan being attacked, or its implementation?** If implementation — **fiat**, and move on.

## Open Questions

1. Does an **inventory** change how the vault reads rounds, or only how it names what it already saw? Two of twelve concepts here (**inherency**, **non-uniqueness**) were reconstructed unaided by a debater with no training, which cuts both ways.
2. Is there a formal concept whose import would *hurt* — one whose validity really is local to the judge? **Dropped arguments** is the clear case; are there others?
3. Debate theory has more inventory than this (perms, framework, theory shells, the *should/would* distinction). Is the remainder worth importing, or is this the useful 80%?
