# Online Debates
> Analysis of online "bloodsports" debates — extracting signal from spectacle.

## Purpose

Online debates (often labeled "bloodsports") are semi-structured, entertainment-driven exchanges between participants who range from informed amateurs to semi-experts. The format rewards interruptions, rhetoric, and crowd performance over rigor.

But they have value. The participants often touch on topics explored elsewhere in this vault — economics, philosophy, legal theory, civilizational patterns — even if only tangentially. The goal here is to:

1. **Summarize** what was actually said (not what the audience thought was said)
2. **Analyze** the claims against our existing frameworks
3. **Filter** signal from noise — flag where participants are onto something vs. performing
4. **Cross-link** to vault research where the topic intersects with work we've already done

## Approach

Focus on **ideas, not individuals**. Participants are listed for attribution but the analysis is about what was said, not who said it or how they said it. Personal attacks, rhetorical style, and character critiques are ignored — the goal is to extract anything useful regardless of how it was delivered.

## Entry Format

Each debate gets its own file. Structure:

```
# [Debate Title / Topic]
> One-line summary of the core question

**Date:** YYYY-MM-DD
**Source:** [link or platform]
**Participants:** [names — for attribution only]
**Duration:** approximate

## Key Claims & Analysis

- **Claim:** what was said (attributed)
- **Assessment:** correct / partially correct / wrong / unfalsifiable
- **Notes:** why, with references to vault frameworks where applicable

## Extracted Ideas
```

## Method pages
Not transcript reviews and not doctrinal spin-offs — reusable debate craft, extracted from many specimens at once:

- **[Worked Examples — The Impossibility Floor, With Arithmetic](./voting-paradox-worked-examples.md)** — an **evidence bank**, built for the planned [CGP Grey STV](./cgp-grey-stv-too-good.md) refutation: eight ballot profiles that make the social-choice pathologies concrete, every number computed by [`tools/voting-paradoxes.py`](../../tools/voting-paradoxes.py) and re-derived on every `--selftest` run rather than asserted. **Two attacks kept deliberately separate:** *impossibility* (no method satisfies every desideratum, so "the fair system" names a forbidden corner) and *underdetermination* (**"STV" is not one rule** — the implementation choices the cartoon skips *decide the winner*). **Headline:** identical ballots, four ordinary rule-sets (Droop/Hare × Gregory/whole-ballot), **three different three-member councils** — plus isolations proving the **quota alone** and the **surplus rule alone** each flip the result; where the surplus is drawn by lot, *a recount of the same ballots can seat a different person*. Second-best: **one electorate, six methods, five different winners**, with a Condorcet winner present so "there was no majority will" is unavailable. Also carries a worked Gibbard–Satterthwaite manipulation (6 voters bury their own favourite and win), non-monotonicity (raising the winner on 12 ballots defeats them), an IIA-violation-plus-Condorcet-failure in a single profile (the center-squeeze shape), a tie-break-free no-show paradox, and a majority-criterion failure under Borda that the selftest found *as a failed sanity check*. **The discipline the page enforces — don't fight looseness with looseness:** only the IIA case is literally **Arrow**; manipulation is **Gibbard–Satterthwaite**, participation is **Moulin**, and monotonicity/Condorcet/majority are *criteria*, not theorems. Deployment order is theory-**last**

- **[The Load-Bearing Word](./the-load-bearing-word.md)** — every resolution has one term carrying the argumentative weight, and it is rarely the topic noun (*"is masculinity in crisis?"* is about **crisis**; *"poverty is a sign of exploitation"* is about **sign**). Whoever pins that word sets the win condition for both sides, and a round that never pins it can only be won on delivery — which is the mechanism underneath every layer-1 death. Sorts load-bearing terms into **four families with a different test each**: *degree* words (*too, works, fair, crisis*) need a **failure condition**, supplied by operationalising against the function the thing exists to serve; *quantifiers* (*impossible, all, never*) set the burdens asymmetrically, so one counterexample kills a universal; *evidential* words (*sign, indicates*) require the sign to **vary with what it signals** and mark a **departure from a baseline**; *modal* words (*should, ought*) require asking whether the outcome is a live choice at all — if data centers get built regardless, *should* can only range over **where**, not whether. Part 2 unifies the two dirty moves as one operation in opposite directions: **inflation** (widen the term until nothing can refute it, and the resolution goes vacuous — *luck* → "anything that happens") and **severance** (narrow it until the opponent's evidence falls outside, conceding the substance to win on scope — *algorithm* severed from human moderation). Nine dated specimens, including Chris's own poverty debate as the positive case, where the concession on *"sign"* was won early and *"made the rest of it much easier."*
