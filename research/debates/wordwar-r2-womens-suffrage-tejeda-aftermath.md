## ⚠ The round collapses in the first sentence, and the affirmative says so himself

> **Aftermath, opening line:** *"I'm going to be arguing a position in this debate that I don't actually agree with. Because my opponent and I basically have identical worldviews, I agreed to play devil's advocate and argue that women should be allowed to vote."*

**Chris caught it live:** *"the aff has implicitly stated he was being a devil's advocate, and his opening is almost conceding the topic from the get-go."* It is not even implicit — it is the first thing said, and the concession is structural rather than rhetorical.

**Neither man defends the resolution.**

- **The Aff's case is irreversibility, not entitlement.** His argument is that repeal is socially impossible — the reaction from women would be unmanageable — so the franchise stays. He then spends the balance of his opening *restricting* it: a female voter should be **25 or older**, **married with at least two children**, and able to **define the word "woman"**; and the voting patterns he objects to should be corrected over decades by *"mass and consistent propaganda… based on Christian values and ethics."* He closes by handing the burden back: even granting the Neg's arguments, *"he still has the task of walking us through how we would go about taking the rights away."*
- **The Neg's case is non-necessity, with the actual question bracketed.** Tejeda severs **"should"** from **"must"**: the prompt says *should*, which he reads as leaving *"wiggle room,"* so the Aff must show that without women voting *"everything crumbles, society breaks down."* And he explicitly sets the repeal question aside — *"when it comes to taking away the right to vote… that's a whole separate topic."*

So the Aff argues *we cannot take it back*, the Neg argues *it is not strictly necessary*, and **both propositions are true simultaneously.** There is no proposition in contention.

**The moderator could not identify the Neg's side after his five-minute opening** and had to ask outright: *"Are you arguing that women shouldn't have the right to vote?"* — following up with *"I literally couldn't tell if you were arguing if women should or shouldn't vote."*

## Two burden-plays pointed at each other

Both men spend their openings trying to hand the *other* an impossible burden while defending nothing themselves:

| | Move | Vault name |
|---|---|---|
| **Neg** | Sever *should* into *must*; demand proof that suffrage is **necessary** | **Severance** — narrowing a term to dodge the evidence ([The Load-Bearing Word](./the-load-bearing-word.md)) |
| **Aff** | Demand a **repeal mechanism** — walk us through how you would actually take it away | Burden-shift; the [easy burden](../philosophy/tangents/the-negatives-easy-burden.md) claimed by the *affirmative* |

This is the [feminism rerun](./wordwar-feminism-rerun-david-tareyak.md)'s pathology **run by both sides at once**. There, one affirmative refused to build and annexed the negative's presumption. Here each man does it to the other, and the resolution is left unattended in the middle.

## ⚠ What this does to the pre-registered test — handled without moving the goalposts

This round was [filed this morning](./word-war-debate-series.md#round-2-pre-registration--predictions-filed-before-any-tape-2026-09-01), before any tape, as **the best test in the bracket**: clause (a) of [finding 17](./word-war-debate-series.md#cross-round-findings) says the ballot tracks the resolution's near-unanimous prior → **Aftermath**; clause (b) says affiliation intrudes and **Tejeda carries the bracket's largest mobilised bloc** (+89.1) drawn onto the indefensible side. The registered condition was blunt: *"a Tejeda win kills finding 17 outright — no re-coding of the prior saves it."*

**That condition stands exactly as written.** The model is fed the resolution and the side assignment, which is all it had; it could not see a devil's-advocate declaration, and weakening a registered test after seeing the tape is the error [finding 15](./word-war-debate-series.md#cross-round-findings) died of.

**But the collapse changes what the round can measure, and that is worth more than the original test.** A declared devil's advocate who concedes his own side is the **largest execution gap the bracket can produce.** So the two forms of the model now split:

| Model | Predicts | Filed |
|---|---|---|
| **Prior-only** (clause a alone) | **The Aftermath** | ✅ **pre-tape, 2026-09-01**, before the transcript existed |
| **Two-factor** (prior, overridden by a large execution gap) | **Jose Tejeda** | ⚠ **post-opening, 2026-09-01** — registered *after* reading the openings and labelled as such |

**One of the two dies whichever way the ballot goes**, and that is a cleaner discrimination than the round was designed to give. The honesty condition is that the second call is **not** claimable as a pre-tape prediction and is recorded here as post-opening.

## Discussion

### Chris's encapsulation

> **Chris:** *"the debate was 'women should vote because we can't remove the right' vs 'I don't think women should vote.'"*

That is the round in one line, and it names the defect: **those are not contradictories.** One is a claim about feasibility, the other about desirability, and both can hold at once.

### The centrepiece: this is a **topicality** failure, and *fiat* was the missing word

> **Chris:** *"If I had to judge this based on formal debate ideas, the affirmative's plan was interesting — practicality. I think the problem was topicality; Neg was right that 'should' is the operative word. **'By fiat' is the phrase that the neg should have come up with** — 'if I win, women can't vote by fiat' — as execution arguments tend to be weak in policy debate formats."*

**This is the read of the round, and it comes from outside the vault's existing toolkit.** In policy debate the affirmative is granted **fiat**: the plan is *stipulated* enacted, so *"it would never pass"* is not a disadvantage. Chris inverts it and hands it to the negative — one sentence disposes of the affirmative's **entire** case, because the affirmative had nothing else:

> *If I win the resolution, the franchise changes by fiat. How it would be accomplished is not the question on the table.*

Everything Aftermath ran — repeal is a *"steep hill,"* the divorces and social backlash, *"you still need to answer how we would even go about taking the right away"* (repeated at least twice) — is an **implementation objection**. Under debate convention that is not a reason the resolution is false. **An implementation objection is not a truth objection, and irreversibility is not justification** — if *"we can't take it back"* counted as an argument *for* a proposition, every entrenched arrangement would be self-justifying.

Tejeda had the right instinct and the wrong instrument: he sensed the affirmative was off-resolution and said so repeatedly (*"that's why it doesn't fit the topic… you're just conflating topics right now"*), but he attacked it with the **should/must severance** — demanding proof that suffrage is *necessary* — which is a burden-play of his own rather than a topicality objection. **Fiat was available and free, and neither man knew the word.**

### The affirmative never crossed his own principles — and the tape shows why

> **Chris:** *"even in this case, the affirmative didn't really go against his principles. 'Sure women shouldn't vote, but how are you gonna make that happen?' It wasn't really a position, but more of an implementation question."*

Confirmed on the tape, and the mechanism is visible: Aftermath's actual position is **opposition to universal suffrage as such** — *"I would have a strict criteria for men as well… I don't believe in universal suffrage"* — which is **compatible with both sides of the resolution as posed.** He does eventually say the words (*"I do believe that women should have the right to vote"*), but bundled with age, marriage, childbearing and definition requirements. So the devil's-advocate assignment never actually required him to hold a view he rejects. **The resolution was loose enough to accommodate his real position on either side of it** — the same underspecification defect as the [data-centers round](./wordwar-r2-data-centers-medeiros-horseradish.md), arriving by a different route.

### Switch-side debating needs burden conventions to be viable

> **Chris:** *"I would argue this format is harder than policy debate in being able to swap sides of the argument."*

A genuine structural point. Policy debate makes side-swapping routine **because the scaffolding carries it**: fiat, presumption, stock issues and an allocated burden of proof mean a debater argues *the case*, and the judge scores the case rather than the man. Strip all of that out — as this format does — and hand the decision to an audience ballot, and there is nothing left to distinguish **arguing a position** from **holding one**. A devil's advocate is then scored on a sincerity he is not supposed to have. This is [finding 6](./word-war-debate-series.md#cross-round-findings)'s technique/format mismatch generalised: the missing conventions are not decoration, they are what makes the exercise possible at all.

### The moderator — Chris's read, checked against the tape

> **Chris:** *"at first I liked the moderator, but then halfway into the moderator questions it felt she had her own agenda… makes sense, 2 men talking about women's suffrage and a woman moderator. Still, she wanted to debate :)"*

**Grounded.** In a segment introduced as ten minutes to *"clarify your positions,"* Monica Perez's first question asks whether *"the powers that be"* would be less successful *"if only men voted"*; asked to restate it, she supplies her own frame — that elections may not be real, that media propaganda makes choice impossible, that the options are *"the welfare warfare superstate or the warfare welfare superstate."* Aftermath answers, *"with all due respect it's a little bit off topic."*

Notably this does **not** fit [finding 1](./word-war-debate-series.md#cross-round-findings)'s existing charge. The rule there is *supply the distinction, own it as yours, decline to adjudicate* — and she **does** own it, in the first person. The defect is a different one and needs its own name: **the question was off-resolution.** Whether voting is causally efficacious is orthogonal to whether women should hold the franchise, and it consumed a share of the only segment designed to pin positions down.

**Chris reconsidered this and then sustained it — by checking the base rate rather than his impression.**

> **Chris:** *"I was about to retract my harshness on the judge segment for this, as it is *possible* that segment was designed for the judge to use as they saw fit — but it seems most other judges use it to 'clarify positions', and this is probably the correct read."*

Worth recording as a *method* note as much as a moderator one: the charge was tested against **the bracket's own convention across six moderators** rather than against how the segment felt, and it survived. The segment has a settled use; this one departed from it.

**Round 2 has now produced two novel moderator behaviours in two tapes** — [Dylan Ren's live fact-check](./wordwar-r2-data-centers-medeiros-horseradish.md) settling a contested empirical claim, and an off-resolution frame here. Six moderators, and the behaviour is still not converging.

### ⚠ Correction: the post-opening call rests on a category error

Chris's verdict: *"I don't know who wins, this is probably a popularity contest as there wasn't much engagement and neither one said much to stand out"* — **an abstention**, and the ledger has precedent for abstentions being the right move ([Summerhays/Gilkison](./wordwar-western-therapy-men-summerhays-gilkison.md), where the substance read and the ballot diverged by 43 points).

That verdict undercuts the post-opening call filed above. It reasoned that a declared devil's advocate is *"the largest execution gap the bracket can produce"* and therefore that the two-factor model predicts Tejeda. **But what happened is a topicality failure, not an execution failure** — the affirmative did not argue *badly*, he argued a **different question**, competently. Chris's read that neither man stood out is exactly what that looks like from the audience seat.

**So [finding 17](./word-war-debate-series.md#cross-round-findings) has a gap this round exposes: the two-factor model has no term for *the affirmative answered a different question*.** Its override clause is keyed to execution *quality*, and off-resolution argument is invisible to it.

**Both calls stay filed as written.** The prior-only call (Aftermath) and the post-opening call (Tejeda) stand, with this objection recorded against the second — shopping for a third call after the discussion would be exactly the goalpost-moving the pre-registration exists to prevent. Score them as filed, and note the objection when the ballot lands.

### Chris's closing verdict — the lesson is topic selection, not the debaters

> **Chris:** *"An interesting round from the perspective of trying to find a good topic between 2 similar debaters, and they tried very hard to engage with the right spirit, but the lesson here is a more contentious topic."*

Notably generous where the page has been harsh, and it relocates the fault correctly: **both men behaved well.** The affirmative took an assignment he disagreed with and disclosed it; the negative kept trying to steer back to the resolution. What failed was upstream of both — **the resolution was not chosen to split this pairing.**

That is a fixable process defect and it now sits in the [hub's defect list](./word-war-debate-series.md#format-defects-and-their-cheap-fixes) as defect 8: a tournament that assigns topics without regard to *who is arguing* will keep producing rounds where the sides agree. The fix is cheap — ask the pair, before assigning, whether they disagree about the proposition.
