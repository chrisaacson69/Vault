## Argument Structures
### Keen (clips)
1. **How he got there:** the Sydney building boom made him ask why the cranes add no value. Marx's first seven chapters give a reason why they *do*, and Keen says Marx retreated from it to keep "scientific socialism" intact. (0:00–0:46)
2. **The classical setup:** Ricardo held that cost of production, not supply and demand, regulates price. If everything sells at cost, where does profit come from? Marx's answer: labour's exchange value is the cost of subsistence (6 hrs), its use value is a 12-hr day, and the gap is surplus. (2:44–4:29)
3. **Falling rate of profit:** competition forces more machinery. If machines add no surplus, profit ÷ (depreciation + wages) falls → wage suppression → revolution. "Obviously completely wrong in how things worked out." (4:29–5:22)
4. **The core argument:** Marx says use value and exchange value are *incommensurable* (the tribes trading leather and pottery). If that gap is what lets labour yield surplus, **the same gap applies to a machine**: you buy it at its cost but value it for what it produces. So machines can add value, a rising capital/labour ratio need not lower the profit rate, and socialism is not inevitable. (6:47–8:51)
5. **Marx against finance:** "roving cavaliers of credit"; banks "know nothing about production". The financial sector should serve industry. Today there is too much private debt and speculation. (11:49–13:03)
6. **Globalization was a farce:** the services that grew were mostly finance. "Manufacturing is where true value is added." (16:21–16:44)
7. **America needs a public/private balance:** the public sector is better at some goods "because it's not trying to make a profit". (20:25–21:15)

### Marxist commentator
1. **History hasn't ended:** early capitalism collapsed before it won, and so did early socialism. "The future is unwritten." (5:22–6:18)
2. **Skip the LTV technicals and jump to full automation:** machines produce everything in abundance, so no exchange value is needed and prices fall to zero (TVs, phones, software). Capitalism implodes into communism; "even Musk" says money will be obsolete. (8:51–11:22)
3. **Financialization is a *countervailing tendency* to the falling rate of profit,** not a policy error. Pushing that capital back into production *hastens* the crash. (13:03–15:53)
4. **Deindustrialization is the falling rate of profit:** mature capital can't reproduce profitably at home, so it is exported. It isn't a policy mistake. (17:14–19:58)
5. **Privatization is the system's necessity, not ideology.** Keen's balanced mixed economy is an *equilibrium* claim, which contradicts his own attack on equilibrium economics. "To prevent any privatization, you have to prevent all of it." (22:40–28:14)

## Discussion seeds
1. **Value vs wealth: the load-bearing word.** The commentator's automation argument *concedes* machines produce use values (wealth) and only denies they produce **exchange value**. Under the LTV "value" means labour-time, so "machines create no value" is true *by definition*. Is the whole fight a dispute over one word?
2. **Falling prices as proof of collapse.** He reads cheaper TVs as capitalism losing profitability. Marginalism reads them as the system doing its job: more wealth per hour of work. Who is measuring the right thing?
3. **"History hasn't ended."** Every failure is temporary and every countertrend is a "countervailing tendency." Is anything allowed to count against the theory? (Compare Chris's Keen-is-ideological verdict, which applies here with the parties swapped.)
4. **Keen's own residue:** "manufacturing is where *true* value is added" and "public is better because it's not trying to make a profit" are productive/unproductive-labour leftovers. Did Keen escape the LTV, or just move its boundary?
5. **The commentator's best shot:** Keen attacks equilibrium economics, then proposes a stable public/private balance. Is that a fair catch?

## Discussion
**Chris (2026-10-05):**
- **They talk past each other.** "Keen is just criticizing LTV and pointing out that it in itself underlies socialism/communism, while the commentator is advocating that Marx still wants Marxism :)"
- **"History hasn't ended" is a poor move.** It makes the claim unfalsifiable. The usual counter, "how many times does something have to fail before you understand it doesn't work?", **falls flat** against it, because unfalsifiability absorbs any number of failures. **The better attack is to show the theory is self-contradictory, and therefore impossible**, rather than to count failures.
- **Use value vs exchange value is "a bit silly."** In plain terms, *exchange value is cost* and *use value is demand*. Prices do fall, but **not to zero**; we've discussed this effect (see [Jevons and software demand](../economics/jevons-software-demand.md): cheaper output draws more demand rather than vanishing). **"His whole argument relies on an impossible zero cost."**
- **He agrees with Claude's proof-by-definition point** (seed 1): "Marxists like to do this all the time."
- **Seeds 4 and 5 are where we already disagree with Keen**, consistent with [Keen × PBD](./keen-pbd-postkeynesian-capitalism.md).
- **Marx as an overextended trendline** (portable; see below):
  > "Maybe at best we can see Marx = what goes wrong when one tries to take a trendline too far :)"

  Falling prices are a real *direction*. "Prices go to zero, so exchange value disappears, so capitalism implodes" is the *destination* that direction can't reach. → filed as a specimen in **[Directional Truth and the Discrete Trap](../../notes/directional-truth-and-the-discrete-trap.md)**.
