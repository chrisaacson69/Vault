The position is that **no one may dictate whom you date.** He spends the round narrating his "trap" instead of running it: *tool* 114 times, a secret list of predicted objections, and an hour arguing against a clip of himself. ## Context

He answered *"Blind chick? Hell yeah,"* then gave five reasons: *"the hypergamy thing goes away,"* less materialism, more modesty, *"less chance she's going to be doing sex work,"* and *"you get to be the hero… She has to depend on you for everything. You are Superman."* AVG challenged the segment, and he says he avoided the debate *"as long as possible."* The clip is played on air from 61:54 to 68:09.

**Chronology:** this June round is the **earliest Ruelas specimen on file**. Both of his Word War rounds came later (August 2026). ## Argument Structures (what was actually said)

### Martae Ruelas — Affirmative

1. **The definition, from the coiner.** The blind pill is *"Martae Ruelas's invention,"* dated July 15, 2025 on Rollo Tomassi's podcast. It is *"a sharp rhetorical debate tool… not a worldview… not a prescription… not a data packet,"* and it is *"pure rhetoric… a paradox generator"* (2:54–5:28). The definition he reads says it is *"the provocative thought experiment of **deliberately** choosing to date or **pursue** a blind woman… as **superior** high-virtue alternative to modern feminist-influenced dating"* (3:20). He repeats the same wording in his closing (94:52).
2. **The dilemma.** Ask a feminist what would be bad about a man *"opening his dating market"* to include blind women. **If she accepts it**, she *"admit[s] that feminism has produced women so toxic and unreliable that disability beats liberation… complementarianism wins."* **If she rejects it**, she *"defend[s] the strong independent woman as superior, revealing… not equality but female supremacy and male disposability,"* i.e. *"men can't have preferences"* (20:24, 35:14–36:19, 88:11–88:40).
3. **The model is "the Muslim dilemma."** He compares it to the Christian apologetic argument (the Quran affirms the earlier scriptures, which contradict it) and to TAG. His version is *"the feminist dilemma"* (3:36, 22:07, 36:39, 104:12).
4. **An objection counts as confirmation.** *"Arguing against the tool itself instantly outs you as defending the feminist position by default. It can't be critiqued neutrally without stepping into the frame"* (39:17). You never supply a motive; *"you wait for the feminist"* to supply *"you must be a predator"* (14:08).
5. **A list of about 20 predicted objections ("axioms"), withheld.** Examples: *"you're fetishizing them"* means male desire is predatory by default; *"savior complex"* means male initiative is suspect; *"what about what they want?"* means male preference needs female endorsement (33:57, 81:51–82:57). *"I'm not going to put the trap online so that way people can realize that they're falling into it"* (34:16). *"If I tell you a trap before you walk into it, does that make the trap better or worse?"* (27:40).
6. **No "should."** He never said men should date blind women. *"Opening my dating market"* is not *"targeting"* (18:48, 24:14, 41:22, 88:02). He says the May 31 answer was a personal preference, given when he was asked *"would you?"*, and that it comes from his deaf mother and a childhood among blind people (23:06, 43:40, 86:05).
7. **The descriptors are observation.** He says blind women are less prone to *"propagandization"* and materialism: *"I've literally witnessed this with my eyes"* (45:16, 55:02). Dependence is good: *"We should need women and men need women… Want means that you're optional. That's what the blind pill exposes in feminists"* (29:36, 72:24).
8. **Evidence that it works.** He cites a year of use in X Spaces, Tomassi calling it *"genius"* in private, and Mo Rebuttal, Alex Medeiros and "Uncle Tom" being unable to find an exit (14:54, 78:36, 95:48).
9. **Collecting concessions.** AVG agrees a thought experiment is a rhetorical tool (*"Sure"*), and he answers *"you just contradicted yourself"* (48:03–48:37). She says *"anything could exist in a hypothetical,"* and he calls it *"concession after concession"* (54:01).
10. **The final form of his claim.** *"The rhetorical tool is a net positive for men"* (32:19), not the dating (85:07–85:16). And at 83:06: ***"I would agree with her that if men specifically go for blind women, they're bad."***

### Average Female Debater — Negative

1. **She opens and closes by naming a "definist fallacy":** *"defining a term in a biased way that… makes the opponent's position impossible to defend"* (15:12, 97:14, 103:14).
2. **The record.** On May 31 he called blind women *"an untapped market"* and said they were less hypergamous, more modest and moved by the liturgy (15:39–16:30). No one watching took it as a thought experiment, and the tool framing never appears in that stream (25:53, 43:21, 46:54, 48:38). She concedes he never literally said "should": *"No. Not in strict words"* (47:46).
3. **Implication without "should": the Chanel-bag analogy.** Suppose she told her viewers *"I need a rich man to buy me Chanel bags."* Is that advice to them? *"Is it possible to imply something by use of terminology?"* (50:44–51:25). Public figures are responsible for how they will be heard (47:31, 72:47).
4. **Her own position, stated so the trap can be tested against it.** Falling in love with a blind woman is fine, and blind women are not inferior spouses. But she would not recommend deliberately seeking a blind wife, for two reasons: (a) it is a hard life, and she can't fill the traditional role; (b) courting her for her blindness and then leaving when the projection fails would devastate her (37:15–38:32, 79:52–80:58, 101:39).
5. **Dependency is the objectionable part.** Preferring a partner for her dependence is *"not a preference, that's a psychopathic instinct"* (28:18). *"Do they want a hero because… without that man [they] could not do basic functional physical tasks?"* (71:53). *"Blind them so they need men?"* (72:32).
6. **Swapping out the disability.** *"Is a fully visually competent woman not capable of having virtue, of having a low body count or a zero body count?"* (56:15).
7. **The instrument as evidence against itself.** He reads from a script instead of answering (39:41). Nobody else can see the list of traps: *"It's almost like it's impossible to not fall into them"* (34:24). The Tomassi endorsement can't be checked (76:27).
8. **Force doctrine as the exit.** *"Men do that anyway. I don't allow men to do anything. **Force doctrine, baby**"* (91:14).
9. **Appeal to emotion.** She counts the deaf-mother references (*"five times,"* 45:06) and calls his claims *"anecdotal"* (55:13).
10. **The concession she banks.** Asked directly, he says men should not be told to date blind women. He agrees it is a hard life that most men aren't virtuous enough for (*"Yeah, for sure"*). And he agrees that the net positive he is claiming is the tool, not the dating (84:12–85:16).

## Discussion seeds

1. **Who fixes what "the blind pill" means: the coiner or the broadcast?** Ruelas's whole case rests on the coiner's prerogative: he invented it, so it is what he says it is. AVG's case rests on public meaning: what people actually saw on May 31. The vault has already ruled on this fight once. Peirce lost *pragmatism* to James, and self-designation did not fix the reference ([Load-Bearing Word § Capture](./the-load-bearing-word.md#-fifth-type--capture-the-shift-arrives-already-done-added-2026-09-23)). This case differs from capture, though: **the coiner broadcast both senses himself.** And his own definition (item 1, read twice) contains *deliberately*, *pursue* and *superior*, the three words he spends an hour denying he ever used. AVG never quoted it back. Does that settle the reference question on his own terms, with no need for the May 31 clip?
2. **Is it a dilemma at all?** The Islamic dilemma gets whatever force it has from the opponent's *own* premises: the Quran's own affirmation of the earlier scriptures. The blind pill assigns each horn its consequence by stipulation. Take "nothing's wrong with dating a blind woman, so you admit feminism ruined dating" (90:25): what does the "yes" actually entail? Then at 83:06 he endorses AVG's substantive position, and her "escape" at 101:39 is that same position. Is the trap's author standing in its escape hatch? And is AVG's force-doctrine line (91:14) a third exit? The dilemma assumes her accepting or rejecting has some permission-granting authority.
3. **Can the trap lose?** Objection counts as confirmation (item 4), and the list of forbidden moves is secret. Two threads. First, **disclosure**: he says telling you the trap weakens it. A logical argument survives being fully explained to its target; the Islamic dilemma is published, answered and still argued. Is *"survives being explained to the person it's aimed at"* a clean line between an argument and an ambush? That would be a portable candidate. Second, **symmetric grounding**: "your objection proves my thesis" is the Kafka-trap shape the right usually charges the left with. Here it was aimed at an Orthodox anti-feminist (*"Feminism is evil… I completely agree,"* 73:13), and it still classified her as "defending a feminist position."
4. **Can you recommend something without saying "should"?** His defense is purely literal: find the "should." Her counter is the Chanel bag. The May 31 answer is *"here's why I would,"* plus five evaluative reasons (*"you get to be the hero"*) given in reply to a public question. How does *"it was only a thought experiment"* compare to *"just asking questions"* or *"just joking"*? Is cancelling an implication after the audience has reacted a legitimate move?
5. **What does the thought experiment actually vary?** Its conclusion is about male preference in general (*"men can't have preferences"*). But the thing it changes is disability, and with it dependency. AVG ran the substitution at 56:15: keep the same virtues, drop the disability, and nobody objects. So is the unease it produces about men having preferences, or about preferring dependency? The steelman runs the other way. Is there a real asymmetry buried under this vehicle, the one he argued in the [Word War feminism round](./wordwar-feminism-relationships-ruelas-anton.md)? That asymmetry: women's preferences for height and income are socially licensed, while men's preferences for modesty get pathologized.

## Discussion

### 1. The verdict, and why it isn't the interesting part

> **Chris:** *"I am less interested in determining a winner, I think AVG won because Marte could not explain himself well. This continued even into the after-party as others tried to steelman him and could not."*

⚠ The after-party is **not in this capture**, since the upload ends at 104:47. The steelman attempts there are recorded as Chris's report.

### 2. Right position, wrong delivery: he narrated the move instead of making it

> **Chris:** *"What I found interesting is that Marte actually has the correct position here, but he is just very bad trying to explain it. **Too often he discussed what he was trying to do instead of just doing it.**"*

The transcript bears this out. It says *tool* 114 times, *dilemma* 50, *prescription* 36, *axiom(s)* 27, *trap* 23 and *designed* 17. There are also two dozen or more moments where he announces that the trap is working: *"you're falling into the tool that I created"* (19:06), *"this is actually working the way that I designed it"* (27:00), *"it's supposed to be built like that"* (27:24), *"it's f—ing brilliant"* (90:37). The narration costs him three ways:

- **It discloses the structure.** By his own account, a trap you describe in advance is a weaker trap (27:40).
- **"You're trapped" is a claim about an argument, not the argument itself.** The audience has no way to check it, which makes it the [fallacy fallacy](../philosophy/tangents/the-fallacy-fallacy.md)'s cousin: naming the move in place of making it.
- **It spends his concessions instead of banking them.** At 90:18 AVG granted, under stipulated good intentions, *"there's nothing wrong, Marte, with dating a blind person."* He answered by describing the mechanism instead of quoting her back ([Load-Bearing Word, rule 3](./the-load-bearing-word.md#part-3--operating-rules)).

**A contrast to check.** Two months later, in the [Word War feminism round](./wordwar-feminism-relationships-ruelas-anton.md), he ran the *emasculation* binary without narrating it. He asked the question, got the answer "humiliation," and stopped: *"That's all I had to ask."* It is the same method, but executed rather than described.

### 3. The steelman *(proposed by Claude; Chris redirected it in §4)*

Chris asked for the best way to interpret and defend the position. The permission claim is the part worth defending: a man may include, or even favor, blind women, and the reflexive charge of "predatory" is a presumption about male motive, not an argument. The defense:

- **The capability dilemma: the horn he named and then dropped.** His opening named two targets, *"the feminist **or ableist**"* (4:30), and then argued only the feminist one. The ableist horn is the version that works the way the Islamic dilemma does, because it runs on the opponent's own premises:
  - either blind women are capable partners, and then choosing one exploits nothing;
  - or they are not, and then the objection rests on an ableist premise and makes her father's classmates *rational* for passing over the blind girl.

  AVG held both in one answer: *"I don't think that blind women are inferior spouses… necessarily"* and *"she's not going to be able to fulfill the traditional roles"* (37:30–37:49).
- **That pushes the charge onto conduct, and conduct has a test.** Wanting to be needed is the provider role both debaters endorse (*"most women do want a hero,"* 71:49). What separates a provider from a predator is what he does with her need: build her independence, or cultivate her helplessness. That difference is observable, and it is the same test a woman seeking a provider would face. This is the [Parity Move](../philosophy/epistemology/the-parity-move.md): it turns "uniquely predatory" into a draw, and a draw sends the argument back to conduct. Under stipulated good intentions, AVG already conceded (90:18).
- **"Untapped market" is Becker's economics of discrimination, and AVG supplied the evidence.** Her father's classmate was beautiful and nobody would date her *because she was blind* (80:09). That is a pool excluded for a reason that doesn't track her worth as a partner. In Becker's model, the entrant who stops discriminating raises the excluded group's options. He corrects the bias; he doesn't exploit it.
- **Search vs. selection: the distinction he kept asserting and never explained.** *"Opening my dating market"* versus *"targeting"* is the right line. Category-level reasons (this pool is overlooked) govern where you *look*; person-level reasons govern whom you *commit to*. AVG's objection, going for her *because* she's blind, attacks category-level reasons for *choosing*, and the steelman never needs those. It also squares with his own concession at 83:06.
- **Where the steelman has to give ground:**
  - Drop the superiority claims (less hypergamous, more modest, no OnlyFans). They are anecdotal, and the permission claim doesn't need them.
  - Cut *"they're not incentivized to push people away who can be their caretakers"* (9:21). It is the one reason about her *outside options* rather than her virtues. That is the [coconut-island](./coconut-island-and-manufactured-option-space.md) BATNA logic, and it is the case the predatory charge actually fits.
  - State the parity claim narrowly. Suspicion of one-sided selection isn't reserved for men (*gold digger*), so parity defeats the *reflexive* charge, not every suspicion.

### 4. Chris's correction: the thesis is about jurisdiction, not the market

> **Chris:** *"I think talking about that there is a bigger market for blind girls has truth, but is not the main issue here. The point behind all of this, the 'trap' is that **someone's dating preferences are not for outsider review.**"*

That demotes §3's Becker bullet to a side note: true, but not the point.

**The substitution test: "the church pill."**

> **Chris:** *"Let's say he would have changed this and instead talked about 'the church pill', as in you should try to date girls that you meet at church.. This actually lands well with the conservative audience, but it is the same argument.. same as 'you should pick up girls at the supermarket'. At best, they offer people another way to meet others and reduces any stigma, at worst it is just bad dating advice.. but it is [not] 'horrendous' like the negative tried to claim."* (The bracketed *not* is the evident sense; the typed message omits it.)

The swap keeps the argument's form (a suggestion about where to look) and removes the disability, and the outrage disappears with it. AVG ran the same test from the other side at 56:15: keep the virtues, drop the blindness. **Both substitutions agree that the disability is the only thing producing "horrendous."** They disagree on whether it should. Chris's answer is that it shouldn't, because the preference isn't the outsider's to review.

**Where the preference comes from.**

> **Chris:** *"Some background that might be in the debate is that Marte had to take care of his disabled mother. His 'love language' is taking care of people, and a blind girl would fit this need. It is also possible that a person could have dated a blind girl in their youth and grown an attachment for that community."*

⚠ This capture establishes a deaf mother who has since died (44:12) and a childhood *"in a deaf and blind household"* (5:17). The caregiving specifics are Chris's background knowledge and are not on this tape.

**He made this argument himself, once, and then buried it.** At 23:06–23:38: *"young men when they grow up, they'll look for their mother, especially if she's a good woman… My mom was disabled and I grew up around blind people and deaf people… I don't think that that would be bad for me to prefer this."* His very next sentence is *"Now you're trapped in the dilemma still."* The best argument of his night comes wrapped in trap narration (§2).

**AVG excluded the one piece of evidence relevant to her own charge.** *"Psychopathic instinct"* (28:18) is a claim about motive, and the mother is evidence about motive: where the preference came from. AVG ruled it out as an *appeal to emotion* (44:12–45:09). That label was [triggered by surface features](../philosophy/tangents/the-fallacy-fallacy.md#3-not-all-fallacy-accusations-hold-up), a dead mother and an emotional subject, rather than by the argument's structure. When motive is on trial, biography doesn't substitute for the argument. It *is* the argument.

**The trap, reread as a question of jurisdiction.**

> **Chris:** *"So the point he was trying to make is to not let others dictate who you should or should not date/marry.. and that was the 'trap' he saw everyone fell into (which they did)."*

On this reading the dilemma is valid, and the horns no longer need consequences assigned by stipulation. **"Accept" means permitting and "reject" means forbidding, and both presume the standing to review.** The only clean exit is to decline that standing. AVG actually found it at 91:14: *"Men do that anyway. I don't allow men to do anything. Force doctrine, baby."* Marte filed it under the accept horn (*"you have no push back, which means… you're choosing the accept portion"*). The one answer that conceded his real thesis was the one his script couldn't recognize.

**The thesis was already in his own list of objections.** He read it aloud at 82:24 and 82:51: *"male attraction requires feminist approval to be legitimate"* and *"male preference is only valid when women endorse it."* That is Chris's point, stated as the objection it diagnoses. It never got promoted from the list to the case.

**He fought the wrong charge.** The church-pill frame shows what an honest defense concedes: yes, it's advice, and at worst it's bad advice. AVG's two substantive reasons (a hard life; a projection that fails and hurts her, 37:15–38:32) are critiques of the *advice's quality*, and fair as such. Escalating them to *predatory* and *psychopathic* is where she overreached. Marte did the reverse. He denied it was advice at all (*"never said should"*), which is false on the tape and would have been harmless to admit. And he never contested *predatory*, which is false and was the damaging charge.

**The symmetric principle.**

> **Chris:** *"If we try to make the claim that others cannot dictate that you can't discriminate and should date those outside your preference, they are basically making the same claim the negative makes here."*

As read here, and confirmed by Chris in §5: the demand *"you can't discriminate in dating; date outside your preference"* is the same outsider-review claim as AVG's, pointed the other way. One compels inclusion and the other compels exclusion. A principle that rejects only one of them is a preference about outcomes, not a principle about who has standing. This also explains why the "feminist" label misfired and the trap still worked. The reflex it catches is not feminist; it is the reviewing reflex, and an Orthodox anti-feminist has it too.

**Where the principle needs a boundary** *(Claude, for Chris)*:
- **Preferences are private; conduct is public.** "Not for outsider review" can't cover conduct, such as isolating a partner or cultivating her helplessness, and it doesn't need to. §3's conduct test is exactly where review belongs.
- **Broadcast advice is reviewable as advice** (does it work, and is it good for the people who follow it?), but not as a verdict on the adviser's own preference. That is the line between AVG's fair critique and her overreach.
- **"Dictate" is stronger than "review."** As a claim about authority, the principle is at its strongest: no one may dictate. As a gag on opinion it is weak, because people will judge regardless. The point is that their judgment carries no standing.

### 5. Chris confirms advice, conduct, and "no one may dictate", and defends the absurd example

> **Chris:** *"yes he should have admitted it is only 'advice'. and good point on conduct, Marte could have dismissed much of the criticism with this. and sure.. people will always judge, **'no one may dictate' is the main point here**.. it is wrong to let society dictate your preferences, again, the 'trap' he was trying to illustrate."*

> **Chris:** *"Objecting to a preference to blind women is no different to objecting not dating outside your race or any other 'not politically correct' preference, and his point was that **making it absurd like this illustrates the key point in a way that 'a church girl' would not.**"*

This settles §4's reading of the symmetric sentence. It also answers discussion seed 5 from the other side: the blind case is not a confounded thought experiment but a *deliberately* hard one. The church pill would pass every listener's review, so only a case that trips the reflex can show whether the listener holds the principle or simply approves of the preference. Conduct is held virtuous by stipulation (89:55–90:02), and the objection that survives is an objection to the choice itself.

It also closes discussion seed 3. The jurisdiction version passes the disclosure test that the "feminist dilemma" failed: *"May you decide whom he dates? If not, 'predatory' was never yours to say."* Said out loud in full, it still binds anyone who holds the reviewing reflex. The version Ruelas actually ran needed a secret list.

**Promoted.** The principle now has its own thesis page, [No One May Dictate](../philosophy/morality/no-one-may-dictate.md), with this debate as its first specimen. That page grounds it twice in existing vault instruments: [Scope Confusion's enforceability gradient](../philosophy/morality/scope-confusion.md#the-enforceability-gradient) (who may enforce) and [Force Doctrine's voluntary layer](../philosophy/morality/force-doctrine.md) (compelled love isn't love).

### 6. Wrap-up: demonstrate; AVG's likely read; pressure, harm and broadcasting

**Labeling and narrating have the same cure.**

> **Chris:** *"this is usually a mistake made often.. This has ties to 'the fallacy fallacy' where someone just makes a statement instead of explaining themselves, though here, they are over-explaining when they should just demonstrate.. but that is why I linked them, **the answer to both is to just demonstrate.**"*

This round has one failure per side: AVG *labels* (*"definist fallacy," "appeal to emotion," "appeal to common sense fallacy"*) and Ruelas *narrates*. **Promoted** to [The Fallacy Fallacy § Label or narrate](../philosophy/tangents/the-fallacy-fallacy.md#label-or-narrate-the-answer-to-both-is-to-demonstrate), where this debate is the paired specimen and his Word War *"That's all I had to ask"* is the contrast.

**AVG's likely read.**

> **Chris:** *"I might try to run the short version by AVG to see what she thinks.. there is still a reflexive disgust because the assumption is abuse, but tbh, I think she understands the underlying point is about dictating preference."*

**Pressure, harm, and broadcasting**, all folded into the thesis page:

> **Chris:** *"I think much of the point of the discussion is that even 'pressure' is misguided, and one should not let society dictate your preferences. What this leaves open is the age-old question of preferences vs anti-social behavior.. killing someone should not be a 'preference'. But no one has shown any real harm in mate-selection. As for the broadcasting bit.. yeah, that opens you up to judgement, but otoh, this is the line advice lies on.. just because someone says something works for them, doesn't mean it works for everyone.. ## Toolkit / Vault Position *(as developed with Chris)*

1. **Rebuild the dilemma as a question about jurisdiction.** *"May you decide whom he dates? If not, 'predatory' was never yours to say."* Both "accept" and "reject" presume the standing to judge, and declining that standing concedes the thesis. Unlike the "feminist dilemma," it survives being fully explained to its target. → [No One May Dictate](../philosophy/morality/no-one-may-dictate.md)
2. **Concede the advice, contest the predation.** At worst it is bad advice. A moral verdict needs harm shown in conduct.
3. **Use the conduct test.** A provider builds her independence; a predator cultivates her helplessness. The preference is private, and the conduct is reviewable.
4. **When motive is on trial, biography is evidence, not an appeal to emotion.** → [The Fallacy Fallacy](../philosophy/tangents/the-fallacy-fallacy.md#3-not-all-fallacy-accusations-hold-up)
5. **Demonstrate. Don't label, and don't narrate.** → [The Fallacy Fallacy § Label or narrate](../philosophy/tangents/the-fallacy-fallacy.md#label-or-narrate-the-answer-to-both-is-to-demonstrate)
6. **Pick the case that trips the reflex.** A church girl passes everyone's review and so tests nothing. The absurd case shows whether a listener holds the principle or merely approves of the preference.
