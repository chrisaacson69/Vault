## Argument Structures

### Aff — Rex Jones

1. **An age-based ruling class.** Not racial, not gendered — age. Boomers (1946–64) took the Senate majority in 2008 and have held it 18 straight years, versus the Greatest Generation's 15-year peak; House majority from 1998. Currently "60 out of 99 senators." They have been "the decisive median voting block for the past 30 years."
2. **The transfer.** Social Security and Medicare are "the largest wealth transfer from the poorest segment of the population, the young people, to the richest segment, the old people." Personal anchor, returned to four times: withholding taken from his first paycheck at 15, for a program he will never collect.
3. **The debt.** ~$10T when boomers took the Senate in 2008 → "over 38 trillion" today (he later says $40T). Plus ~$10T on Iraq/Afghanistan/Ukraine/Iran.
4. **War hawks who don't fight.** The boomer cohort largely *avoided* Vietnam while inheriting its parents' martial self-image — "raised off the stolen valor of what their parents did in World War II." So they vote for wars others must fight. Named across party: Graham, Schumer, Trump, Cheney, Rumsfeld; plus the Massie/Ed Gowin primary as a live example.
5. **The cost-of-living asymmetry.** "$200 a month rent" wagging a finger at "$2,000 a month rent" — pre-empting the you-kids-don't-save counter.
6. **The origin story.** Postwar America as sole surviving manufacturing power, gold standard then petrodollar: "truly like being a Roman citizen." Hence "the most spoiled people on earth," "the most sheltered, coddled, and protected generation to ever exist."
7. **"Spiritual boomer."** Pressed on the definition, he extends it past the cohort: "a person could spiritually be a boomer if they support neocon foreign policy" — an attitude ("overconfidence in the American empire"), not a birth date.

### Neg — Mariah Meyers

1. **Nobody builds the track they're born onto.** Every generation inherits an economy shaped by central banks, institutional frameworks and capital far larger than any cohort controls. Blaming boomers for a multi-trillion-dollar system is like blaming smartphone owners for surveillance.
2. **Punch up, not across.** The generational frame is "a manufactured illusion… designed to keep the 99% divided." The real axis is class: boomers in office are "puppets of yet a larger game."
3. **Boomers as co-victims.** They lost pensions and retirement accounts in 2008–09; they paid into the same programs; they are dying and their programs are being dismantled around them.
4. **The counter-transfer.** Much of the "$84–104 trillion" wealth transfer is being routed *down* deliberately — boomers funding grandchildren's houses and student loans, specifically to bypass the banks and the war fund.
5. **Exit, not repair.** "Why are you hanging on to an old system that's breaking?" She carries no health insurance by choice; the answer to a captured system is to build outside it, not to petition it.
6. **Cyclical determinism.** Strauss–Howe's fourth turning (artist / hero / prophet / nomad), fused with astrology as the *causal* substrate: Uranus's 84-year orbit sets the civilizational period, Pluto's ~20-year sign transit "stamps a generation," retrograde splits each cohort into status-quo and opposition halves. Asked to assign it a weight, she says **"almost always 100%."**

## Analysis

### 1. Rex builds Mariah's case for her

The affirmative's most vivid material is §6 above: boomers were *formed* by postwar abundance — sheltered, propagandized by war films, handed a hegemon. That is a **causal-formation story**, and a causal-formation story is precisely the defeater for culpability. If their dispositions were manufactured by conditions they didn't choose, "responsible" in the moral sense is exactly what they aren't.

This isn't a subtle tension, because **Pisco made him choose**. Asked directly whether the prompt was about moral culpability or mere causality, Rex took culpability without hesitation — *"It's like somebody's criminal record, but it's a voting record."* Having claimed the moral reading, he then spends the rest of the hour explaining why the defendant couldn't have turned out otherwise.

Mariah needed exactly one thing: a **deeper cause than generational choice**. Two were on the table — the material/structural one Rex was handing her, and the astrological one. She took the second. The strongest negative case in the room was being read aloud by the affirmative, and it went unclaimed.

### 2. The round fails at a layer below the stack — and that answers an open question

The series' [layer model](./word-war-debate-series.md#cross-round-findings) is **definitional → mechanism → empirical**, and open question 3 asked whether the layer-1 and layer-3 failure modes exhaust the space. They don't. This round never reaches layer 1, because the two sides never share a standard for **what counts as evidence at all**:

> **Rex:** "Is astrology an established science? Is it provable?"
> **Mariah:** "Yes, it is. Because all the planets move in a predictable manner just like generations have a predictable manner."
> **Rex:** "hocus pocus bongus tokus."

Call it **layer 0 — epistemic non-overlap**. It is diagnostically distinct from the other two: a layer-1 failure is two people who *could* adjudicate a definition and don't; a layer-0 failure has no adjudication procedure to reach. Nothing either side says can move the other, and neither is being unreasonable *given their own standard*. Rex is right that he can't be asked to grant it; Mariah is right that he never engages it. Both are correct and the round is still dead.

The tell is the exchange that should have settled it. Pisco asks Mariah the single best moderator question in the bracket so far — **"What would it take for you to be convinced?"** — the falsifiability question, put neutrally. Her answer never supplies a condition. A position that cannot name its own defeater has left the space where debate operates.

### 3. Term inflation — and it is the second round in a row

Cornered on whether *every* boomer fits, Rex converts the term: a **"spiritual boomer"** is anyone with "overconfidence in the American empire," of any age. This rescues the thesis from counterexamples and destroys the resolution doing it. Under the cohort reading, *"boomers are responsible"* is a checkable empirical claim about a voting bloc. Under the disposition reading, it decays to *"the people who supported these policies are responsible for these policies"* — circular, unfalsifiable, and no longer about generations. He was ahead on the version he traded away.

**This is the same move Calvin Constable makes with "luck"** in [the wealth round](./wordwar-rich-without-luck-constable-aftermath.md) — widen the load-bearing term until no counterexample can survive, at the cost of the resolution meaning anything. Two rounds, two resolutions, one disease. Flagged as a **candidate cross-round finding** for the hub rather than promoted, because its second specimen is a round Chris is reviewing himself and the promotion call is his.

### 4. Grading Rex against the thesis page's own tests

[Generational Attribution §1](../philosophy/dynamics/generational-attribution.md) says a generational resolution needs a **mechanism**, a **differential**, and a **counterfactual**. Running the actual tape against it — which is what a specimen is for:

| Test | Rex's case | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Median voting bloc → specific enacted policy (entitlements, war authorizations, deficit spending), with dates and seat counts | **Supplied** — better than the generic form of this argument. He is not merely finger-pointing |
| **Differential** | Never argued. Would a similarly-sized cohort with different values have voted otherwise? | **Absent — and actively undercut** by his own §6 formation story |
| **Counterfactual** | Never attempted. Same policies, smaller cohort? Same cohort, different era? | **Absent** |

So the round is a **partial** counterexample to §4's "finger-pointing is the natural attractor": Rex clears the bar that finding predicts he'd fail (mechanism) and dies on the one the thesis page calls "the killer" (differential). That refines the thesis rather than confirming it — the attractor isn't *no mechanism*, it's *a mechanism substituting for a differential*, which is more seductive because it feels like rigour.

### 5. Pisco confirmed — with the closest call in the bracket

Full-tape read supports the hub's spot-check. Pisco's technique, in order of quality:

- **Forces the culpability/causality split** on Rex, and makes him own the answer. This is the round's structural spine and it exists only because the moderator installed it.
- **Asks for the falsifier** ("what would it take for you to be convinced?").
- **Demands a weight** on the astrology claim ("can you assign a percentage — 20%, 75%?"), converting a vibe into a stated commitment. Her "almost always 100%" is then contradicted by her own "no, that's just a tool," and Rex gets to say so.
- **Labels his own supplied content** — "here's a response that I'm kind of channeling" before offering the demographic-collapse counter. This is the behaviour the attribution rule was built to name.
- **Isolates existing agreement** ("I think you guys actually agreed on that") before letting them keep fighting over it.

The **closest call**: when Mariah runs an etymological move — *casting* a vote as *casting* a spell — Pisco intervenes to block it ("casting a vote is just another way to say submitting a ballot"; "the etymology of the word isn't really…"). That is a moderator adjudicating a substantive point mid-round. It stays on the right side of the hub's line only because it is **stated as his own** and put to her face rather than laundered into her opponent's mouth. Worth logging as the boundary case: the attribution rule permits it, and a stricter rule would not.

### 6. One empirical claim that needs flagging

Rex: *"Bill Clinton… was the first president, the only president in modern history to get the debt down from 4 trillion to 3.2."* These are two different series. **Gross federal debt rose every year of the Clinton presidency** (roughly $4.4T in 1993 to $5.6T in 2001); **debt held by the public** fell in the surplus years (roughly $3.8T to $3.3T). His figures are the publicly-held series while the rest of his argument — "$10 trillion in 2008," "$38 trillion today" — runs on gross debt. Switching series mid-argument is exactly diagnostic 1 of [Reading Outcome Statistics](../philosophy/epistemology/reading-outcome-statistics.md): *specify the measure*. The directional point (fiscal position improved under Clinton, deteriorated after) survives; the specific numbers as stated do not. *Figures from general knowledge — worth a source check before they're cited anywhere downstream.*

His other headline numbers (1946–64 cohort definition; 91% top marginal rate in the 1950s; millennials having overtaken boomers as the largest US cohort — conceded to him by Mariah) are consistent with the standard figures.
