## Discussion

### Seed 1 — the action axiom and the smoker (2026-10-12)

**Chris's position (his words):** the performative/action move is *"the thing I felt the strongest about, but I could not grasp properly"* — and it gets an **instant rejection every time**: a face-to-face debater, Mullally, the host in the original debate, and Claude `[12, 62]`.

> *"People act in a way they think is best at a given time. Why the smoker still smokes is that he values something about smoking that prevents him from accepting the 'logical' reasons not to… so the reality is he doesn't really believe that it is bad. He knows there are some bad things about it, but he thinks the good outweighs… You[r] actions cannot lie about how you truly feel about something. They are not just 'preferences' as people want to casually dismiss."*

**Lineage — this is not a crank position.** Denying weakness of will (akrasia) is **Socrates** (*Protagoras*: no one knowingly does what they judge worse) and, in modern form, **R.M. Hare** (sincerely accepting an ought for yourself *entails* acting on it; if you don't act, you didn't sincerely accept it). Davidson's *"How Is Weakness of Will Possible?"* (1970) is the standard opponent.

**Analysis (Claude, for discussion) — where the nuance is:**
1. **"Believes smoking is bad" is two beliefs.** (a) *factual*: smoking causes harm; (b) *all-things-considered*: smoking now is not worth it. Chris's own description grants (a) and denies (b). Stated that way, the smoker counterexample stops working: nobody claims the smoker doesn't know the facts. The defensible claim is **action reveals the all-things-considered valuation at the moment of acting.**
2. **But actions can disagree with each other.** Nicotine patches, throwing out the pack, asking a spouse to hide the cigarettes: these are *actions* too, and they reveal valuing *not* smoking. "Actions can't lie" holds, but across time they show a **divided agent**: Frankfurt's *second-order desires* (wanting not to want), and Ainslie's preference reversal (the nearer reward wins at the moment, and the agent knows it and pre-commits against it). So "he doesn't really believe it's bad" overshoots. What's revealed is *what won at t*, not what he values overall.
3. **Why the instant rejection.** Listeners hear the move as *is → ought*: "you're alive, so you ought to live" or "so you contradict yourself." Revealed valuation can only ever give **"you *do* value X,"** never **"you *ought to* value X."** The murderer reveals he values murder `[14]`. Pitched as a contradiction, it fails; the breath counterexample is already on the vault (is-ought L83).
4. **What it *can* do is load-bearing: it detects the antecedent.** The vault's oughts are hypothetical/agent-conditional (*if* you pursue life/agency, *then*…). The action axiom is **evidence that the antecedent is satisfied *for this person*.** That is Gewirth's *dialectically necessary* method (lineage L176–194), and it is the vault's **Tier 2 — "Strong"** row (constitutive-middle-dialectics L90). The reframe: *"I'm not claiming you ought to live. Your acting shows you've already taken your continued agency as an end. I'm arguing from your end, not mine."* That moves the argument from "proof of an ought" (rejected) to "identifying the opponent's own premise" (hard to reject).
5. **⚠ Tension with the vault's own text.** [course-06](../philosophy/liquidzulu/course-06-defensive-force.md) §1–2 *rejects* LiquidZulu's smoker argument ("acting is believing you should act… holding the contrary is cognitive dissonance"): *"an act reveals a preference… not a norm… the smoker shows that choosing X is compatible with holding ¬X-ought."* Chris's position today is close to LZ's. One of them has to move. Point 2 above suggests a middle: the act reveals an all-things-considered valuation at t (more than "mere preference," as Chris says), but not a universalizable norm (as the vault says).

**Chris's reply — accepts the compromise:** *"your compromise is fair… I am now starting to see the nuance, and why people are rejecting it… just as I went after Claude, the particular doesn't mean the rule."* → **Resolved:** the act reveals the agent's **all-things-considered judgment at t** (more than a casual "preference"), **not a universalizable norm**. course-06's verdict on LZ stands. Chris's correction to it: "preference" undersells what's revealed. *(Pending: apply that refinement to course-06 §1–2.)*

### Seed 1b — the action base vs. argumentation ethics on the NAP

**Chris's move:** the point of the action move was to **ground Rand's NAP**, which *"she accepts at face value."* Claude's Rand ("moral actors need to act freely") is still a *premise*. Settle for *"you value life,"* then argue **inside the opponent's framework**:

> *"I think this does better than the objections to justifying the NAP through argumentation ethics, as they use the same trick and say this only works if one argues! One who just goes straight to violence without any discussion is showing their preference that they don't value non-aggression!"*

**Analysis (Claude, for discussion):**
1. **The scope advantage is real, and it's Chris's own objection turned into a strength.** AE binds only while arguing; that is Chris's own *bad-faith-arguer* objection (lineage L143: *"nothing… at t forbids the club at t+1"*). An action base doesn't care whether anyone argued: the club at t+1 is *also an action*. **Action base: wide scope, thin content. AE: narrow scope, thicker content.**
2. **But the last sentence concedes the content gap.** If the aggressor's act *reveals he doesn't value non-aggression*, then on an agent-conditional framework the NAP **doesn't bind him by his own ends**. The action axiom reports that honestly, and that's the problem. Revealed value gets you **Tier 2** ("you value *your* agency"; constitutive-middle-dialectics L90, rated *Strong*). It does not get you **Tier 3** ("so respect *mine*"), which is the vault's own *contested joint* (L91).
3. **Where the action move *can* reach Tier 3: universalize the reason, not the act.** The aggressor's action shows he values agency (he's using his). Ask *why* he values it: because he's a purposive agent. That ground **"has no name on it"** (Gewirth, L91). So aggression is valuing agency and destroying agency **on the same ground at the same moment**. That's an inconsistency in *his own valuations*, not a clash between his values and yours. And it doesn't require him to have argued: **Gewirth's universalization, run on action instead of discourse.**
4. **The remaining bullet:** the egoist's indexical, *"I value mine because it's **mine**."* The vault's backup is relational, not logical: *"betrayal isolates the betrayer"* (L91, a category-3 fact about agent-interaction).

**Portable?** Point 3, *action-based universalization as the repair of AE's scope problem*, generalizes beyond this chat. Candidate home: [Performative Grounding Lineage](../philosophy/morality/performative-grounding-lineage.md) (after the bad-faith-arguer entry) and/or the *action-base vs life-base* open question at [Objectivism Boundary](../philosophy/morality/objectivism-boundary.md) L142.

**Chris — adopts it:** *"yeah, I buy the universal part… I think Gewirth will be key here, as my first objection has always been 'I can value MY actions, but that doesn't mean I have to value yours.'"* → **Promoted** to [Lineage §5 Gewirth](../philosophy/morality/performative-grounding-lineage.md#5-alan-gewirth--the-principle-of-generic-consistency) (⭐ entry) and annotated its Open Qs #1 and #3.

**What he'd said to Claude, and why it bounced.** At `[11]` Chris had: *"if you value your life, you should assume others value theirs for the same reason."* Claude `[12]` replied that this is *"true but descriptive. The egoist grants it and still asks why their valuing gives me a reason."* The fix is one word of focus. Not *others value theirs* (a fact about them) but *the reason **I** value mine is a reason that applies to theirs* (a fact about **my** reasons). The first invites "so what?"; the second makes aggression an inconsistency inside the aggressor's own valuations.

**Chris — meta-observation (worth keeping):** *"What I am learning is that my understanding of the framework is mostly intuition, and the reason for the vault is to help put it into rational steps."* This exchange is a clean specimen: the intuition was right in `[11]`, but the step was stated in the descriptive form that gets rejected.

**Chris on the egoist's "mine because it's mine":** it's *"the same universalization applied to property,"* and the *"what's mine is mine, what's yours is mine"* instinct is common in human nature, but *"it does not survive contact"*: whoever values killing others *"will be met by the same response and this will be short lived."*

**Analysis (Claude, for discussion):**
1. **The property parallel is right, and it's stronger than "survives contact."** *Why* is it yours? Because you made it, homesteaded it, traded for it. That ground has no name on it either, so "what's yours is mine" asserts a property ground and denies it in the same breath. It's the same Gewirth move, one level down: an inconsistency in the claimant's own reasons, not a prediction about his fate.
2. **"Doesn't survive contact" is the detector, not the definition, and you already ruled that.** [Sitch–Mullally](./sitch-mullally-birthright-citizenship.md) L81: *"Durability is probably the detector; the given structure is the definition."* Here the structure (definition) is the universalization inconsistency; *failing on contact* is the evidence that the inconsistency is real. Keep them in that order.
3. **Why the order matters: contact fails exactly where morality is needed most.** `[42]`: harvesting from prisoners and the enslaved was **stable for decades**, because the victims couldn't retaliate. "Met by the same response" assumes the victim *can* respond. Against the powerless, the egoist survives contact fine. So if contact is the definition, the powerless fall outside morality. If contact is only the detector, the universalization step still covers them, and the detector is simply slow or blind there.

**Chris — agrees on the order** (universalization = definition, contact = detector). Seed 1 closed. Three additions:

- **"Got away with it" defies reality.** *"There is always evidence left behind, and it is not repeatable. The strong evolution claim."* "Doesn't survive contact" is *"shorthand for this concept that probably I am the only person who understands it this way."*
- **The rape-farm debate (unrecorded; this is the only vault record).** Chris *"had to go to great lengths to show that the farms would not last indefinitely, using the concept that captive beings always resist… like it had troubles explaining the length of slavery, it had troubles here."* The opponent left, *"probably because he thought I kept moving the goalpost… not really, I was just showing that for every example he brought up, I showed why eventually it fails."* Chris: *"this was not presented well."*
- **Contact is the wrong operative word: dependence, not retaliation.** *"The weak don't survive contact, but they can be necessary for continuation. Slaughtering all of your farmers might seem like a power move to the military minded, but come harvest time, their loss is now felt."*

**Analysis (Claude, for discussion):**
1. **The dependence detector is better than the retaliation detector.** It doesn't need the victim to fight back, only to be *needed*. The exploiter consumes the productive agency he lives on. That is Rand's own picture in *Atlas Shrugged* (the looter depends on the producer). It also fits [Force Doctrine](../philosophy/morality/force-doctrine.md) L69's "structure selects who holds the force across rounds" (reproduction, coordination). It explains slavery's long run better too: slavery persisted *because* it kept the farmers alive, and it decayed where it suppressed their agency.
2. **But the dependence detector is blind in exactly the original case.** It covers the victim who *produces something the aggressor needs*. It's blind to the **surplus** victim: the prisoner harvested for organs (who yields value by being consumed), and the **low-IQ, depressed, disabled person the opening debate was about**, whose loss "won't be felt at harvest." Every detector so far (retaliation, evidence, dependence) is weakest on the people who are powerless *and* unneeded. That is why the definition has to be the universalization step: *the reason you value your own agency doesn't check whether the other agent is useful to you.*
3. **The goalpost diagnosis.** It probably wasn't goalpost-moving, but it **looked like it for a structural reason**. Each answer was a *detector* story ("this variant eventually fails because…"), and detector stories are contingent, so the opponent can always build a new variant with the failure mode stipulated away. That forces a fresh ad hoc answer every round. **Leading with the definition answers every variant once:** *"the farm-owner values his own agency on a ground the captives share; that's the wrong, whatever happens next."* The failure stories then become corroboration ("and here's the evidence it doesn't last"), not the load. **Definition first, detector second** is a debate toolkit move. *Portable?* Candidate home: [Formal Debate Concepts](./formal-debate-concepts.md) or [Debate FAQ](../philosophy/morality/debate-faq.md).

### Seed 1c — does universalization freeze an adaptive system?

**Chris** (on the goalposts: *"it only looked like I was moving, but the reality was that he was the one moving"*):
- **Descartes:** *"'I think therefore I am'… doesn't universalism make it 'they think, therefore they are too'?"*
- **The worry behind the "structural hole":** *"Evolutionary pressure will remove those unfit. It seems nice to argue that everyone is 'fit', but I don't think this matches reality. If we put universalism over everything it means that once a concept is universalized, it cannot be taken down… but the whole point is that the system adapts to circumstances that can change."*
- **Repeatability and probability:** repeatability *"seemed to close most of the examples given… it also answered that morality is about probability, as my turn left = car crash example showed. A perfectly moral being can run into bad luck."*

**Analysis (Claude, for discussion):**
1. **Descartes: yes for the *reason*, with a caveat about access.** The cogito proves existence only to the thinker; you can't run *their* cogito for them (the other-minds problem). What universalizes is the *ground*: "if thinking grounds my existence-claim, it grounds theirs." That's the same Gewirth shape. The open part is *detecting* that another being thinks or acts, which is an inference by analogy, not a proof. That's the bridge to **seed 5 (animals)**.
2. **"Fit": the norm, not the person.** Selection acts on **norms and dispositions**. "Evolutionary pressure will remove those unfit" reads as *people*, and that is the opening debate's premise (low-IQ). The vault's guard against exactly this slip is [Objectivism Boundary](../philosophy/morality/objectivism-boundary.md) L62: *"The explanatory level is population-scale; the normative beneficiary stays individual."* "Not everyone is fit" can be true of norms without making any *person* a legitimate target.
3. **Universalization fixes the ground, not the content, so the system still adapts.** What's universalized is *"agency counts because it's agency."* What adapts is everything downstream: property rules, institutions, social-media norms `[46]`. That's the Foot + Hayek split Chris already accepted (fixed foundation, evolved applications), and the vault's triple: **"conditional in form, objective in content, universal in scope"** ([is-ought](../philosophy/morality/is-ought-and-performative-grounding.md) L23). It also stays revisable as a *theory*: if the creatures changed, the conditional's antecedent would change (`[38]`, "different creatures").
4. **Repeatability doesn't close the surplus-victim case.** Repeatability turns acts into **rules**, and the vault's disposition-level justification is correct. But "harvest the unneeded" is a repeatable rule, and it was stable for decades `[42]`. Repeatability catches rules that self-destruct, not rules whose victims are disposable.
5. **⭐ The moral-luck point is the strongest card in the hand, and it cuts *against* "they'd get caught."** Chris's turn-left case says the act is judged by the **choice under foreseeable knowledge**, not by how the dice land ([Sitch–Mullally](./sitch-mullally-birthright-citizenship.md) L87: *"a limit on our ability to predict… not a flaw in morality"*). **Apply it symmetrically.** If *bad* luck can't make a good choice wrong, *good* luck (not getting caught) can't make a bad choice right. So "what if he got away with it?" is answered **by Chris's own principle**, with no need for "evidence is always left behind." Getting away with it is moral luck; the act was wrong when chosen. That's [Chris–Mullally](./chris-mullally-callin.md) L296 again: *"his getting away with it changes his outcomes, not the entailment."*

**Chris — accepts the symmetry; seed 1 CLOSED.** He clarifies what "evidence left behind" was for: *"the 'got away with it' isn't as clean as the hypo wants it to be."*
- **Dexter:** a serial killer who always gets away with it *"is what makes it a good show… the suspension of disbelief. In reality he might be able to do it for a long time, but eventually he gets caught… the same pattern I was trying to explain in the rape farm. Our imaginations can think up many wonderful things, but reality determines how that works."*
- **His own hypo, a world without death** (*Groundhog Day*, *Edge of Tomorrow*): *"Now is murder such a crime? It seems in that reality the harm is much less. So maybe it is not!… It was only when the main character acted in a way that reached the wanted end did they escape the traps. Death was a lesson."*
- **On communication:** *"I need to get better at explaining all of this and dealing with objections… the downside to my unique thought patterns is that I have a hard time relating to the audience, as the words presuppose meanings that not many share."*

**Analysis (Claude, for discussion):**
1. **Two jobs, keep them separate.** *Why murder is wrong* = universalization, with moral-luck symmetry handling "got away with it." *Why the clean hypo misleads* = the Dexter point, a **methodological** claim about which thought experiments are informative. The second is legitimate (trolley-skeptics make it), but it shouldn't carry the first. Mixed together, they produced the "wrong only if caught" reading every opponent heard.
2. **The no-death world is a demonstration *for* the framework.** Change the creatures and the content changes: if killing doesn't destroy agency, murder's specific wrong shrinks, and what remains wrong is whatever still violates agency (coercion, pain, stolen time). That's the conditional antecedent at work (`[38]`; seed 1c point 3), and the ground ("agency counts") never moved. Expect the consistency jab, though: *"you rejected my hypothetical for breaking reality, then used one."* The honest answer: both are legitimate probes. The moral-luck symmetry means Chris no longer *needs* to reject Claude's hypo; he can answer it.
3. **⭐ *Groundhog Day* is the Ring of Gyges, run as fiction, and it comes out Chris's way.** Phil has total impunity (no consequence survives the reset), which is the purest "got away with it" world possible. His manipulative strategies still **fail**, because his end (Rita, a genuine relationship) can only be reached by treating others as agents. That's Plato's own answer to Gyges (the unjust soul defeats itself; [Plato Ethics](../philosophy/tangents/history-of-philosophy/11-plato-ethics.md) L110), told through the agent's ends rather than through detection. *Edge of Tomorrow* is the other half: death as **feedback** in an iterated trial is Chris's selection epistemology in miniature, with learning by repeated contact with reality.

### Seed 4 — Foot needs a human nature; the vault says "no essence of man"

**The collision.** Claude's landing `[38, 46]` (*"human nature is fixed, so the condition is always met for us"*) and the low-IQ rescue `[68]` (*worth set by the kind, not ranked by individual capacity*) both need a **species-level nature**. The vault has three positions that pull against each other:
- [Objectivism Boundary](../philosophy/morality/objectivism-boundary.md) §2 (L44–48): *"There is no essence of chair; there is no essence of man either"*; *"'Flourishing' is the word where the smuggling happens."*
- [Wilson stance-dependence](./wilson-objectivism-stance-dependence.md) L199: the **kind vs. group** distinction is *"the Aristotelian essence position, and it's the correct response to 'but babies can't reason.'"*
- [Human Constancy](../philosophy/human-constancy.md) §5: Trunk 1 *"has a standing dependency"* on human nature, downgraded to **"ends drift far more slowly than means."**

**Candidate repair (Claude, for discussion): generics, not essences.** Foot's "natural goodness" (via Michael Thompson's *Life and Action*) runs on **natural-history generics**: *"wolves hunt in packs," "the oak has deep roots."* These are true of the *kind* even though some wolves hunt alone and some oaks are shallow. A generic is not an essence. It's a fact about the **dense centre** of a population, which is exactly what [Where Concepts Live](../philosophy/epistemology/where-concepts-live.md) says concepts track: *"agree in the dense centre… disagree at the edges."* So:
1. **"Fixed human nature" downgrades to "stable generics,"** which Human Constancy already supplies ("ends drift slowly"). That's enough for the conditional's antecedent to be met on every timescale anyone can act on ([symmetry-breaking](../philosophy/dynamics/symmetry-breaking.md): *functionally absolute, metaphysically conditional*).
2. **The kind-level threshold survives without an essence.** "Humans are rational agents" is a true *generic*. The low-IQ or disabled individual falls under the kind's generic as a three-legged wolf still falls under wolf-norms. That's what *protects* them: they're judged as members of the kind, not ranked by individual capacity.
3. **Wilson L199's "essence" would be relabelled "kind-level generic"**, making it consistent with Objectivism Boundary §2.

**What it leaves open, and it's the fork into seed 5:** if standing tracks the *kind's* generic, a brilliant dog (Chaser, `[66]`) has less standing than a severely impaired human, because the dog's kind is generically not a human-level agent. That's the speciesism charge (Singer). Chris's "it's about degree" `[67]` pulls toward *individual* capacity, which hands back the low-IQ premise `[68]`. **Three options:** (a) standing by kind-generic (protects every human; speciesist edge); (b) by individual capacity (Singer; reopens the opening debate); (c) **split**: *agency* (responsibility) by degree and individual, *patienthood* (standing) by the kind's generic. Option (c) is Claude's `[68]` and is consistent with all three vault pages above.

**Chris: there IS an essence, the way a chair has one.** *"At the very basic a chair is where you can sit… can it be used for other things? Sure, but it has to be able to allow you to sit. A human has a heartbeat, they breathe, they are bipedal apes… I think we can find many common things about lifeforms in general that fit."*

**Chris: the soft rape farm, the mythos version.** *"What if instead of being so overt, we develop a mythos where women are taught that it is best for them to be mothers and caregivers, and this is what gives them fulfilment… you don't really need coercion here… this also sounds a lot like certain cultures and religions."* Tying women up *"goes against what makes humans happy and fulfilled,"* but under the mythos *"many women would adapt… and find meaning in it… it could potentially last for a long time."* He judges it like war vs. trade: *"repeatability and maximization of benefits… once random effects create alternate possibilities that are better than the previous system, evolution adapts to this and we never go back."* On political churn: *"everyone wants to believe the next new thing is what is going to save humanity… because we have had previous success… what is not seen is how many failures came before… change is hard."* He wants to **dig into Foot** to fill gaps.

**Analysis (Claude, for discussion):**
1. **Chris's "essence" behaves like a generic, which may make this a vocabulary dispute.** Each feature has members of the kind who lack it: amputees and wheelchair users aren't bipedal, a patient on heart-lung bypass has no heartbeat of their own, and a beanbag seats you but a broken chair doesn't. Nobody stops counting them as human. So "essence" here means *the necessary-in-the-typical-case core*, which is the dense centre. **What [Objectivism Boundary](../philosophy/morality/objectivism-boundary.md) §2 rejects is narrower: a *normative* essence** (man *qua* man prescribing what you must become). A *descriptive* functional core (chair = sittable, human = breathing bipedal rational ape) is compatible with it. Chris's chair-by-function is also exactly **Aristotle's *ergon***, the bridge Foot uses: a good chair seats well. Proposed wording: **"functional core," descriptive, generic-strength**. Chris has already accepted "flourishing" if narrowed (§2: *"if the definition is this narrow, I can yield"*).
2. **The soft farm is a better test than the overt one, because the framework's answer changes.** With no coercion, there's no aggression, so universalization has nothing to catch. It's an **elective construction**, judged on fitness ([Constructed ≠ Arbitrary](../philosophy/constructed-not-arbitrary.md)). The line that brings morality back is **exit**: can she leave, own property, refuse? A mythos *enforced* by blocked exit (no property, no education, honor violence) is coercion in costume, and the universalization step applies again. The vault already has the tool: **exit + voice** (Hirschman), [The Represented Unit](../philosophy/morality/legal-theory/represented-unit.md) / [17th-Amendment review](./jake-lawyer-17th-amendment.md): *"neither-exit-nor-voice = no leverage."* **Proposed test: open exit → elective (judge by fitness); blocked exit → coercive (judge by universalization).**
3. **"We never go back" is contradicted by the vault itself.** [Human Constancy](../philosophy/human-constancy.md) L140: *"family and religion return under modern complexity."* History adds Iran in 1979 (women's roles went back within one generation) and Rome's collapse into the medieval period. The ratchet is **probabilistic, not one-way**. Survival picks winners locally, and environments can regress.
4. **The churn point is a correct use of survivorship bias**, and it cuts both ways: reformers see only the reforms that worked, and traditionalists see only the traditions that survived. That's Chesterton's fence and its mirror. Worth its own line.
5. **Foot leads (from memory; verify before promoting).** Foot's 1967 *"The Problem of Abortion and the Doctrine of the Double Effect"* introduced the **trolley problem** and the **magistrate-and-mob case**, the ancestor of the vault's [Sheriff Problem](./utilitarianism-sheriff-counterexample.md). Her resolution, **negative duties (not to harm) outweigh positive duties (to aid)**, is the vault's [action/inaction asymmetry](../philosophy/morality/action-inaction-asymmetry.md) by another route. *Natural Goodness* (2001) supplies the generics (Thompson), "natural defect," and a chapter on whether the wicked can be happy (the Gyges question again). So **the transplant/sheriff case, seed 3, may be Foot's own, answered in a way the vault already agrees with.**

**Analysis (Claude):**
1. **"The need, not the organ" sharpens the functional core.** The core is the **life-process requirement** (circulation, oxygenation), not the mechanism that meets it. An artificial heart meets the need by other means, the way a three-legged chair still seats you. That's Aristotle's *ergon* exactly: the function defines the kind, and any mechanism that performs it counts. **Settled wording: "functional core: the life-process needs of the kind," descriptive and generic-strength.**
2. **The margins are where the moral disputes live** (symmetric-grounding note). The core settles the easy cases. The contested cases (the low-IQ person in the opening debate, animals, the fetus) are all marginal *with respect to capacity*, which is why the kind-vs-capacity fork matters. On the fetus specifically: "alive" and "human" are not where the serious disagreement is; both sides largely grant the biology. **The crux is whether standing tracks the kind (options a/c) or present capacity (option b)**, which is this seed's fork. So seed 4's fork *is* the abortion crux, stated without the politics. (Prior vault work: [Abortion prep](./abortion-readily-available-prep.md), whose working definition already uses *"the living embryo or fetus."*) Either side must be grounded to equal depth before the vault takes a position.
3. **Logan's Run is the cleanest exit specimen:** a pleasant mythos (renewal at Carousel) with **exit enforced by Sandmen**, so it's coercive however happy the residents are. The harder sci-fi case is **Brave New World**: exit nominally open, but desire engineered so no one wants it. That's **[manufactured option space](./coconut-island-and-manufactured-option-space.md)** turned inward (constraining the *wanting* rather than the options), and it's where the exit test needs a supplement. *(Open thread.)*
