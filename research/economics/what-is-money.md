# What Is Money?
> Seed page. Is money mainly a **store of value** or a **medium of exchange**? Chris leans toward store of value: *if it were only a medium of exchange, we could all share the same dollar.* That puts him against Mises on the definition, and on the free-banking side of an internal Austrian split on supply. Not resolved; parked on purpose.

## Scaffolding (Claude, to test when the discussion resumes)

- **The shared-dollar argument has a formal version.** In the Cambridge cash-balance equation (Marshall, Pigou), M = k·P·Y, where *k* is the share of income people choose to *hold*, and k = 1/V. A pure medium of exchange would need only a tiny money stock circulating at very high velocity. **Holding balances is the store-of-value function, and it is the V term.** So "velocity is unstable" ([Equation of Exchange](./equation-of-exchange.md)) and "the desire to store value changes" are the same fact.
- **Orthodox Austrians disagree with thought 1.** Menger and Mises treat medium of exchange as money's *defining* function: money emerges from whatever good is easiest to sell, and storing value follows from that. This is a real disagreement, not a strawman.
- **Thought 2 is inside the Austrian school, not against it.** It is the **free-banking** position (Selgin, White, Horwitz): the money supply should expand and contract with the demand to hold money ("monetary equilibrium"). **Rothbardians** (100% reserves, a fixed commodity base) are on the other side.
- **Thought 3 may not be fence-sitting.** Skeptical of fiat + accepts fractional reserves is close to the free-banking package exactly: a commodity base with competitive fractional-reserve issue on top. Chris's two "fences" may be one coherent, named position. *To test: does he accept its other commitments (no central bank, competitive note issue)?*

## Open Questions

1. **Store of value vs medium of exchange:** which is primary, or is the question malformed (one function per timescale: exchange at a moment, storage across time)?
2. **The fixed-currency test (Chris):** does a hard currency with supply shocks and changing technology show a stable "general" level while relative prices move? *Candidate lab:* the classical gold standard, where prices fell from the 1870s to the 1890s with real growth (Selgin's "productivity norm"), but gold discoveries (1849, the 1850s, 1886 onward) were money-supply shocks *inside* the "fixed" system. *Needs grounding.*
3. **Is fractional reserve compatible with anti-fiat?** And is the free-banking position the resolution of Chris's fence, or a third fence?
4. **"Floating is attractive but abusive":** is the abuse inherent in floating money, or in a monopoly issuer? (Compare [Failure as Mandate](../philosophy/dynamics/failure-as-mandate.md): the discipline missing may be competition in issue, not the float itself.)
