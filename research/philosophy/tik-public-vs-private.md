# TIK — Public vs Private
> Public = hierarchy, private = the individual at its base — an etymological history run forward into a political taxonomy. The thesis is coherent; it runs on two criteria that disagree, and the best line in it undercuts the framing.

**Date:** 2026-09-23 (reviewed)
**Source:** [YouTube — TIK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ksAqr4lLA_Y) — [Transcript](../../raw/debates/transcript-ksAqr4lLA_Y.txt) (906 cues, 102:17)
**Speaker:** TIK (TIKhistory), answering a Patron's question about whether Hitler "privatised the state"
**Why it's here:** the video TIK points at in [the ancap video](./tik-anarcho-capitalism.md) (8:02), **and the one [LiquidZulu attacks by name](./liquidzulu/responding-to-the-critics.md)** — *"TIK's public = large hierarchy is a package deal (bigness + collectivism)… his appeal to historical usage is nominalism, traced through Hobbes."* First time the vault has both sides of that collision in hand.
**Sources he leans on:** Fustel de Coulanges, *The Ancient City* · Larry Siedentop, *Inventing the Individual* (named at the end) · Mises · Menger · Higgs, *Depression, War and Cold War* · Aly, *Hitler's Beneficiaries* · Folsom · Temin

## The chain of argument

1. **Etymology → history.** *Privus* = single/individual; *publicus* = of the people. Ancient society is built family → *gens* → *phratria*/*curia* → tribe → city, each tier lighting a new hearth. **The "public" just is that hierarchy**; "private" is the household at its base.
2. **Paul invents the individual.** Judgement becomes personal rather than familial, so *private* gains its modern individual sense on top of its older small-group sense.
3. **The taxonomy:** public = hierarchy/collective; private = individual/small. Capitalism = *private control of the means of production*; socialism = *social control of the same*.
4. ⭐ **Publicly-traded companies are "public" entities — mini-states.** *"A public entity may not be the central state, but it may be a mini-state in itself, in competition with the central hierarchy."* Alphabet is *"an internet public state."*
5. ⭐ **The funding test:** *"if it's paid for by the state, it is the state"* — NHS, Academy schools, BBC.
6. **Calculation.** Prices require private exchange (Menger's subjective value; Mises's calculation problem). No prices → no demand signal → shortage and famine (Lenin's grain, Stalin's collectivisation).
7. **Capitalism is anti-corporation.** Firms *"cannot grow large naturally"* because internal calculation fails; monopoly requires the state; corporations are state creatures. Vanderbilt vs Fulton as the specimen.
8. **Therefore fascism and National Socialism are left-wing** — public-sector control, whoever owns the paper. *State capitalism* and *anarcho-socialism* are both oxymorons.
9. ⭐ **The Engels gotcha.** If the state withers away, what remains is *"the private sector without a state. Anarcho-capitalism."* So *"Marx and Engels were anarcho-capitalists! Or they're lying."*
10. **Exploitation** requires the absence of choice; a wage agreed to is not exploitation because *"you can always leave."*
11. **Answer to the question:** Hitler and his goons benefited personally, but the state is public whoever eats from it — control, not who gets the spoils.

## Discussion

### Chris: the opening is where the errors are seeded

The origin story is not decoration — it is the evidence base for every definition that follows, and it is **one lineage presented as the lineage**.

> *"He imagined just one version of how society formed, and it was geared towards the Roman, or at least euro-centric, development. His etymology even went to Latin while he was speaking in terms pre-civ. This isn't a mistake per se, but it shows the bias."*

⭐ **This is a method problem, not a sourcing quibble, and it is fatal to his own framing.** TIK's method *is* historical usage — he is claiming to recover **the historic definitions**, not to propose good ones. A method that reads meaning off history needs the history to be **general**, and he sampled a single civilisational line while narrating it as the dawn of society as such. The Latin-etymology-for-pre-literate-society tell is the seam showing. *(Note where this lands: it feeds [LZ's nominalism charge](./liquidzulu/responding-to-the-critics.md) from an unexpected direction — not "usage cannot fix meaning" but "you sampled one usage and called it the essence.")*

**Three specific overreaches:**

1. **Christianity → individualism is drawn too thick.** *"I am sure it helped, but this dismisses the Jewish religion entirely, as this had elements of individuality pre-Christ."* Siedentop's thesis is a real one, but TIK runs it as an invention event with a single author. Personal responsibility and individual judgement have Hebrew antecedents that the video does not mention.
2. **Ancestry was not the only early model.** *"This assumes that extended families were the only source of collectivism, and that the 'father' was the only head of state. We have examples of **village elders**, showing that communities shared the workload of government."* A council of elders is collective authority that is **not** an extended hearth-family — so the family→gens→tribe→city ladder is one route, not the route, and "public = the hierarchy that unites private families" loses its claim to be what *public* fundamentally means.
3. **Corollary:** if the ladder is contingent, then his central definition is **an etymology of one civilisation, not a discovery about the concept** — which is exactly the charge he levels at his opponents for muddying terms.

### Chris: both criteria are wrong — it is access, not ownership

> *"LZ's 'group ownership is a contradiction' is an error the other way, as you see. Otoh, I don't buy TIK's idea that 'public' is about hierarchy and # of owners… **I think they are both wrong — it is about access, not ownership.** Even in a family unit, something like a toy can be given to an individual and that is his private property, it does not have to be shared. And that is where the libertarian definition comes in: **who is excluded from control?** A public park, a public road, does not exclude who uses it; a corporate building does."*

⭐ **Promoted to its own thesis:** [The Public/Private Boundary — It Is Drawn by Exclusion, Not Ownership](./morality/legal-theory/public-private-boundary.md), with TIK and LZ as the two failed criteria and the comparison table.

Two things make it more than a third opinion:

- **The toy breaks TIK's rule from the inside.** Privateness does not require being small or low in a hierarchy — it requires an **exclusion boundary**, and one can exist inside the most collective unit there is. TIK's ladder cannot produce that result, because on his account *private* just means *near the bottom*.
- **It is continuous with the vault's existing property theory rather than imported for the occasion.** [Self-ownership](./morality/legal-theory/self-ownership-and-property-rights.md) is already *"the right of exclusive control… the right to exclude others"*, and **LZ's own ch. 6** says *"a property right in X is the right to exclude others from X."* So the public/private boundary and the property boundary turn out to be **the same boundary** — a unification neither disputant gets from his own criterion, and one LZ could have reached from his own course.

### ⭐ Chris: TIK refutes himself in an aside

> *"**YouTube is not its own state!** He even says one cannot put their feet on the CEO's desk!"*

The line is at 18:10 — Alphabet is *"'private' in the sense that you or I can't just put our feet up on the CEO's desk, **but** it's still a 'public' entity because it is a public hierarchy."* On the access criterion that clause **is the test**, and it returns *private*. TIK produces the decisive fact himself and files it as a figure of speech, because his criterion has already told him where the answer must land. **This is the cleanest evidence in the video for the package-deal charge** — the word *public* is being carried by the hierarchy sense while the exclusion sense, sitting right there in the same sentence, is waved off.

## Discussion Seeds

1. ⭐ **The package-deal charge is testable from inside the video, and it lands.** TIK runs **two criteria** for *public* and they disagree on the same case:
   - **Hierarchy/size** → Alphabet is public (society at large holds the shares) — *"an internet public state."*
   - **Funding** → *"if it's paid for by the state, it is the state"* → Alphabet, paid by advertisers on the market, is **private**.
   He never reconciles them. That is exactly what a package deal produces: two properties welded into one word, so the word can be applied by whichever criterion suits the case. **LZ's charge survives without importing a single Objectivist premise.**

2. **But LZ's replacement criterion is worse.** *Collective vs individual*, because *"group ownership is a contradiction in terms"* — which makes every partnership, joint-stock company and joint bank account a contradiction. TIK's instinct that shareholder ownership differs in kind from a sole proprietorship isn't crazy; he just picks the wrong marker. **Is there a third criterion?** Candidate on the table: *is the entity's revenue obtained by consent or by compulsion* — TIK's own funding test, generalised. It makes Alphabet private, the NHS public, and needs no claim about group ownership.

3. ⭐ **His best line undercuts his own framing** — quoting Temin, and he flags it himself: ***"State control does not require ownership."*** If **control** is what matters, then the public/private line should be drawn on control, not on ownership *or* size — and the whole ownership fight both he and his opponents are having is the wrong fight. He states it and walks past it.

4. **The Engels gotcha is a real burden badly aimed.** It works only if *non-state ⇒ private*, which is the identity under dispute: Marx's withered state leaves **collective** ownership without a coercive apparatus, not individual ownership. As a joke it equivocates. As a question — *what enforces collective ownership with no state?* — it is a genuine burden the Marxist owes.

5. **"Exploitation cannot happen in a free market… you can always leave."** The weakest passage, and the vault has the machinery: [the exit problem](./morality/legal-theory/government-formation.md) — free association without free dissociation. **He argues that the state forecloses exit** (correctly) **and then assumes the labour market never does.** The asymmetry is unexamined.

6. **"Firms cannot grow large naturally" overstates a real result.** The internal-calculation argument gives a **bound** on firm size, not an impossibility — see [Theory of the Firm](../economics/theory-of-the-firm.md) (Coase: firms grow until internal costs exceed market transaction costs). The true claim is *there is a ceiling*; the claim made is *large firms are always artificial*.

7. ⚠ **Two checkable historical claims, flagged not adjudicated.** (a) *"The 1929 recession lasted 17 years, until 1946"* rests on Higgs's regime-uncertainty thesis and is contested. (b) **Nazi privatisation** — TIK says it never happened; there is a literature arguing it did (Bel, 2006), and interestingly **his own answer concedes its mechanism**: ownership transferred, control retained. Per the vault's symmetric-grounding standard these are marked as open, not settled.
