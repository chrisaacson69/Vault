# Philippa Foot — Natural Goodness, Read Against the Vault
> Foot is the vault's nearest ancestor, but the parts the vault took from her were mostly from memory. Read from the sources, she does four things: she supplies the **functional core** without an essence, she abandoned the **hypothetical-imperative** view the vault still holds, she **separated** the Sheriff case from the trolley case, and she says outright that moral goodness is **not** "what survives."

## 1. She changed her mind twice — know which Foot you're quoting

| Period | Her view on *why be moral* | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Early** — "Moral Beliefs" (1959) | The virtues are always in your interest, the way intact limbs are. Takes on Thrasymachus directly. | Morality = rational self-interest |
| **Middle** — "Morality as a System of Hypothetical Imperatives" (1972) | Moral reasons bind only if you have the (optional) desire, like etiquette. *"The man who rejects morality… can be convicted of villainy but not of inconsistency."* | Desire-conditional |
| **Late** — *Natural Goodness* (2001), RG (2004) | Rejects her 1972 view as instrumentalism. Practical rationality is **species-relative**: reasoning that ignores what humans need doesn't count as good reasoning. | Kind-conditional |

What moved her off 1972 was **Warren Quinn's "rational shamelessness"** objection: if rationality just means serving whatever you want, a cruel person is *rationally required* to be cruel efficiently. Her late test case is a Sudetenland farm boy who chose execution over joining the SS. If a theory of rationality can't call that choice rational, *"so much the worse… for the theory"* (RG 6).

Two things she never gave up, even in 1972: morality has a **fixed content** grounded in facts about human life, and self-interest always gives reasons.

## 2. The vault holds her middle view, and she left it for a reason

[Objectivism Boundary](./objectivism-boundary.md) records the agreement *"the oughts are hypothetical, not categorical"*. That's 1972 Foot almost word for word, and her own reason for leaving it is the objection the vault has to answer too. The vault's existing answer is *"hypothetical ≠ non-universal"*: the condition is being an agent, which every agent meets. That is close to where late Foot landed. **Her condition is the kind, not a desire.** So the vault's position is better described as **kind-conditional (late Foot) than desire-conditional (1972 Foot)**. This also settles the review's `[13–20]` exchange: Chris cited Foot for "all morality is conditional", and the Foot who said that took it back.

Late Foot also gives a third answer to the amoralist, between Kant ("you contradict yourself") and 1972 ("you're a villain, not inconsistent"): **you're defective**, in the same sense that any organism can be defective for its kind, except that a defect of *will* is the agent's own and can be blamed (SEP §6). That's the same correction the review made in seed 1 (practical inconsistency, not logical contradiction), with a name for it: **natural defect** of the will.

## 3. The functional core — essence without essentialism

Foot (via her student Michael Thompson) argues that you can't even see something as alive without placing it against its **life form**. "Eating" only means something against what the kind normally eats. Judgments about the kind are **natural-history generics** ("the oak has deep roots"), true of the kind even when this oak is shallow. In plain steps:

1. Calling something an organism already puts it in a kind.
2. The kind sets what its vital operations are for.
3. A feature is a **defect** only if it gets in the way of those operations. A blue tit missing its blue head patch is *not* defective, because it hunts, mates and lives just fine (NG 30).
4. So the norm tracks **function, not a list of traits**. That's Chris's *"the need, not the organ"* and Aristotle's *ergon*.

This is the **"functional core: the life-process needs of the kind, descriptive and generic-strength"** that seed 4 settled on, now with a source. It is **not** the normative *man qua man* essence that [Objectivism Boundary §2](./objectivism-boundary.md) rejects. Foot keeps a "sea change" for humans: *"animals go for the good (thing) that they see, human beings go for what they see as good"* (NG 56). So for humans, nature governs **how we reason**, not each act directly.

## 4. Not "what survives" — Foot says so explicitly

SEP calls the survival-and-reproduction reading of Foot a **misreading** (Andreou 2006). Her own line (NG 42):

> *"Lack of capacity to reproduce is a defect in a human being. But choice of childlessness and even celibacy is not thereby shown to be defective choice, because human good is not the same as plant or animal good."*

This confirms the vault's rule *"durability is the detector; the given structure is the definition"* ([Force Doctrine](./force-doctrine.md), review seed 2). It also bears on Chris's **soft-farm mythos** (seed 4): a culture that reads women's reproductive *capacity* as the norm for their *choices* makes exactly the slide Foot rules out. Capacity is a fact about the kind; choice is judged as reasoning. That pairs with the **exit test**: open exit means the choice really is hers.

## 5. The Sheriff is Foot's own case, and she separates it from the trolley

**Verified (1967):** Foot introduced *both* cases on the same page. One is a judge facing rioters who will kill unless he frames and executes an innocent man. The other is the **driver** of a runaway tram who can steer toward one worker instead of five. Her question is why we turn the tram *"without hesitation"* yet are *"appalled"* at the framing.

**Her answer is not aggregation.** The driver weighs **negative duty against negative duty** (killing one vs. killing five, so minimize). The judge would break a **negative duty** (don't kill) to meet a **positive** one (save the five from the mob), and *"it takes more to justify an interference than to justify the withholding of goods and services"* (1984, MD 83). Her later Rescue I/II pair makes the same point: you may steer the jeep to save five instead of one, but not run over one to reach the five.

**For the vault:**
- [Sheriff page](../../debates/utilitarianism-sheriff-counterexample.md) Extracted Idea 2 (*"just a trolley problem for law enforcement"*) is **wrong as history**. The case was built to show the two are *different*. Corrected in place.
- Her answer **reinforces** the vault's arguments rather than replacing them. It's the [action/inaction asymmetry](./action-inaction-asymmetry.md) reached by the doing/allowing route, and it rejects the trade without needing the vault's "no group utility" argument. A fifth, independent argument.
- **Counter-evidence, recorded symmetrically:** Thomson (1986) moved the choice to a **bystander**, who *is* choosing between killing and letting die, and most people still say turn the trolley. That damages Foot's doing/allowing explanation. (Thomson herself reversed in 2008.) The vault's version needs to survive the bystander case too. *(Open.)*
- Foot's euthanasia essay (1977) gives a doctor a duty to sustain life from **contract**, not general charity. That's the vault's [Contracted Duty Exception](./action-inaction-asymmetry.md#the-contracted-duty-exception) in her words.

## 6. Abortion — what Foot actually held (the seed-5 crux; record both sides)

- **Against Thomson's violinist:** unplugging the violinist lets an existing fatal condition run (Rescue I). Abortion **starts** a fatal sequence (Rescue II). *"The fetus is not in jeopardy because it is in its mother's womb; it is merely dependent on her in the way children are dependent on their parents for food"* (MD 87).
- **But she wouldn't settle the fetus's standing.** She called it intractable, and in 1970 said whether the fetus counts as a human being is, in a sense, **for communities to decide**. SEP calls this a surprising retreat from her own objectivity.
- So Foot hands the vault the **structure** (doing vs. allowing applies to abortion) and leaves the **crux** (does standing track the kind or present capacity?) exactly where seed 4 left it. Her late natural-history view pushes toward *kind*; her 1970 remark doesn't. Neither side gets her as an authority on the crux.
