# Human Constancy
> **The question is a ratio, not a binary** — and stating it that way is most of the work, because both poles are strawmen nobody defends. Chris's claim: *"we are more the same than we are different."* Not that nothing changed (physiology has changed; what we eat changes things; measured intelligence has moved), but that **ends drift far more slowly than means.** The page's central instrument is a checkable asymmetry rather than an intuition: across a 3,000–4,000 year gap, the **ends**-content of ancient texts is transparent and the **means**-content is opaque. If ends drifted at the rate means do, both would be equally alien. They are not. That asymmetry is what turns "human nature is basically constant" from an assumption into a claim with evidence — and it is also what lets the claim be *weakened* to the much more defensible form the rest of the vault actually needs.

## 1. The ratio, not the binary

Almost every public version of this argument is fought between two positions nobody holds:

- **Strong blank slate** — humans are indefinitely plastic; apparent nature is socialization all the way down.
- **Strong essentialism** — human nature is fixed and environment is decoration.

Both are refuted by ordinary observation, which is why the fight is unproductive. The useful question is quantitative:

> **How much of what a human is stays fixed across time and environment, how much moves, and how would we tell?**

Chris's answer, and this page's thesis:

> **Chris:** *"Are we the same as always before? Can we adapt? No, there are differences — we do adapt. How much has stayed the same? I think more than people give credit… different environments producing different results, and yet we still can empathise with those of yester-year. It seems to me that we are more the same than we are different."*

Note what this concedes up front, and why conceding is what makes it strong: **physiology has changed**, diet changes outcomes, measured intelligence has moved, and *"can I say with certainty that I think and feel like a caveman would in their circumstances? I might even say we wouldn't."* The claim is a **ratio with an admitted numerator**, not a constancy claim. That is a far smaller target and a far more defensible one.

## 2. The layered model — where change actually lives

Sorting by how fast each layer moves is what makes the ratio tractable:

| Layer | Example | Rate of drift |
|---|---|---|
| **Physiology** | eating, breathing, heartbeat, reproduction | essentially fixed on historical timescales; real but slow on evolutionary ones |
| **Means of satisfying it** | *what* we eat, *how* we reproduce, how we keep warm | **fast** — this is where nearly all visible change is |
| **Neurology / affect** | grief, ambition, disgust, pair-bonding, status-seeking | slow; the contested layer, and the one this page is about |
| **Expression / norms** | what counts as an insult, what is shameful, marriage form | fast — a *means* layer wearing psychological clothes |

**The load-bearing move is that the middle two are routinely confused.** Change in expression is read as change in affect. That marriage forms vary wildly across cultures is a fact about the *means* of pair-bonding, not evidence that the drive is constructed — and the inference from one to the other is made constantly in both directions.

Chris flags the honest complication: *"I won't even dismiss that **what** we eat changes things."* Right — the layers are not sealed. Nutrition affects development affects cognition; the means layer feeds back into the substrate. The model is a gradient of rates, not a stack of independent shelves.

## 3. The intelligibility asymmetry — the instrument

This is the page's contribution, and it is an *empirical test* rather than a sentiment.

> **Across a 3,000–4,000 year gap, the ends-content of ancient texts is transparent while the means-content is opaque.**

**Transparent** — you read these as a participant, and the feeling lands without mediation:

- **Gilgamesh** on the death of Enkidu — grief, and the terror of one's own mortality arriving through someone else's.
- **Ecclesiastes** — everything is vapor; the work will be forgotten; do it anyway.
- **Marcus Aurelius** talking himself out of bed in the morning because the work is waiting.
- **Sappho** on wanting someone across a room.
- The **Satire of the Trades** — an Egyptian scribe explaining that every other profession is miserable and his own is superior.
- The **complaint tablet to Ea-nāṣir** (~1750 BCE) — Nanni is furious about the grade of copper delivered and about being treated with contempt by a merchant's messenger. It reads exactly like a modern one-star review, because it *is* one.

**Opaque** — you can only read these as a historian, never as a participant: ancient physics, humoral medicine, celestial mechanics, metallurgy-as-theory. The technical content is genuinely alien. You do not *feel* the four humours; you study them.

**The inference:** both bodies of text crossed the same gap, in the same languages, through the same translators, with the same survival filter. **If ends drifted at the rate means drift, both would be equally opaque.** The ends-content is not. That is the asymmetry, and it discriminates — it could have come out the other way, and for the technical content it does.

### The objection, which is real

**Projection and survivorship.** We may be reading *our* emotions into these texts; translation is itself an interpretive act, and the works that survived, got copied, and got translated are disproportionately the ones that resonated with later readers — a selection effect pointed straight at the conclusion.

**The partial answer:** translation and survivorship apply *equally* to the technical corpus, which was also copied, also translated, and also selected. If projection explained the transparency of the ends-content, it should also make ancient cosmology feel familiar. It does not. So the objection weakens the evidence without dissolving the asymmetry.

**What would actually settle it:** the transparency should hold for texts *not* selected for literary merit — administrative records, receipts, legal complaints, graffiti. The Ea-nāṣir tablet is exactly that kind of document, which is why it is the strongest single specimen here and not merely the most entertaining. **Systematically checking the mundane corpus is the real test, and it has not been done.**

## 4. Specimens

### 4a. Reimer and Money — and the second lesson

The **David Reimer** case is the standard citation against strong blank-slatism, and correctly so: a boy whose circumcision was botched in infancy, reassigned and raised as a girl on John Money's advice, who never identified as female, reverted at 14, and died by suicide in 2004. It is unusually informative for an *n* of 1 because there was an **identical twin control**.

**Bound the claim properly.** It refutes the *strong* blank-slate position on gender identity. It does not establish strong nativism about anything, and a single case — even a controlled one — cannot carry a general thesis about human nature.

**The second lesson is the more useful one for this vault.** Money **reported the case as a success for over a decade**, and it circulated in textbooks as evidence *for* social construction until Milton Diamond and Keith Sigmundson published the follow-up in 1997 and Reimer went public. So the most-cited proof of one position was, on examination, evidence for the opposite — and it took thirty years because nobody checked the primary case. That is the [grounding discipline](../../CLAUDE.md) in the wild, and it belongs on this page as a caution about *this page*: the specimen that everyone cites is the one least likely to have been verified by the person citing it.

### 4b. The Flynn effect — means-side, and it fits

Measured IQ rose roughly **3 points per decade** through the 20th century. Flynn's own reading was **"scientific spectacles"** — a trained facility with abstract classification and hypothetical reasoning, not an increase in raw processing power.

Chris's version of the question: *"if IQ was measured on a full-historical scale, would Aristotle, Newton and DaVinci still stand out? A bit — but Aristotle might be 180 relative IQ in his day and only 160 today."* That is roughly the mainstream reading, and it is **means-side**: the ratchet running on cognitive tooling while the substrate sits still.

Two things make this support constancy rather than threaten it:

1. **The reverse Flynn effect.** Scores have declined in Norway, Denmark and Finland since the mid-1990s, and the Norwegian work found the change *within families* — so it is environmental, not genetic. A genetic change cannot reverse in twenty years.
2. **Chris already held this position, from a different direction.** From the [AI-making-people-dumber round](../debates/wordwar-ai-making-people-dumber-charsky-bunn.md): *"human intelligence will likely remain the 2+ standard deviations from 100 it always has been… what increases IQ are mostly **health and genetic improvements**."* Two independent derivations, and the earlier one was about tools rather than history.

### 4c. Pair-bonding — where the evidence is genuinely two-sided

Chris: *"pair-bonding still has much of embedded code in it — women still select for strength, size and competency and the like."*

**The nativist evidence:** Buss's 37-culture study (1989) found consistent sex differences in stated mate preferences across wildly different societies — the kind of cross-cultural invariance that is hard to get from socialization alone.

**The counter-evidence, which belongs here by the [symmetric-grounding standard](../../CLAUDE.md):** Eagly and Wood's re-analysis found those differences *shrink as gender equality rises*, which is what a social-role account predicts and a purely innate one does not.

**And the synthesis is this page's own model.** Both results are compatible with **a fixed end pursued through variable means**: if the underlying end is secure provisioning and competent partnership, then who is expected to supply it will track who controls resources — so the *preference expression* moves with social structure while the *end* does not. That reading is not a dodge; it is a prediction, and it is testable — it says the invariant should be the **end** (competence, security, reliability) and the variant should be **which traits proxy for it**.

## 5. What this does for the rest of the vault

**It repairs an assumption I overstated.** [Subsistence vs. Participation §6c](../economics/subsistence-vs-participation.md) rests on the ratchet running on *means* while *ends* stay fixed, and that section was flagged as depending on "human nature is constant" — its weakest load-bearing claim. **That was an overstatement of the requirement.** The ratchet argument does not need constancy. It needs only:

> **Ends drift far more slowly than means.**

Which is weaker, is what Chris actually claims, and is what §3 supplies evidence for. The assumption should be **downgraded, not defended** — and the argument gets stronger for resting on less.

**It supplies Trunk 1's input parameter.** [Constitutive realism](./the-four-trunks.md#trunk-1--normative-ontology) holds that normative facts are entailed by *the structure of the relata* — *given* agents with certain properties, the norms follow. That formulation is only as determinate as the property list. If the relata were indefinitely plastic, the entailed norms would be too, and the trunk would collapse into the constructivism it explicitly contrasts with. **So Trunk 1 has a standing dependency on some version of this page**, and it has been running on an unstated one.

**It bounds [Constructed ≠ Arbitrary](./constructed-not-arbitrary.md).** That page argues constructions are constrained at design time and run time, and therefore evaluable. This page names *what* they are constrained against at run time: a human whose ends did not move. A construction that stops serving those ends fails, however entrenched it is — which is the mechanism behind §6c's observation that family and religion return under modern complexity.

## 6. The counter-pressure — and it is not resolved

The strongest candidates for genuine **ends**-drift are exactly the ones this framework owes an explanation for and has not paid:

- **Fertility collapse.** Reproduction is the paradigm fixed end, and across every developed society people are choosing dramatically less of it. Either the end changed, or the means-environment now defeats an unchanged end.
- **Declining religiosity.** §6c uses religion's *return* as evidence of a stable underlying need. That argument cuts both ways: the long decline is at least as good evidence that the need was contingent.
- **Changed family structure.** Household form has moved further in eighty years than in the preceding several thousand.

The means-substitution reading is available for each — contraception decoupling sex from reproduction while opportunity costs rose; institutional substitutes absorbing what religion supplied; households restructuring around wage labor rather than production. **But "available" is not "established," and this page does not establish it.** Each is a genuine test the thesis could fail, and treating them as obviously means-side would be the exact error §2 warns about, run in reverse.

**The honest status: the ratio claim is well-supported for affect and poorly tested for motivation.** Grief and irritation are demonstrably stable across four millennia. Whether *what people want* is equally stable is the open half, and fertility is the case that will decide it.

## 7. Open questions

- **Run the mundane-corpus test.** §3's asymmetry rests mostly on literary texts, which are selection-biased toward resonance. Administrative records, receipts, legal complaints, and graffiti are the unbiased sample. If the transparency holds there, the objection largely dies; if it does not, the instrument does.
- **Is fertility collapse ends-drift or means-defeat?** The single most consequential open question on this page, and the vault has nothing on it.
- **Does the layered model (§2) have a principled boundary, or only a gradient?** §2 concedes the layers are not sealed. If it is a gradient all the way, "means vs. ends" is a matter of degree — which weakens every argument built on the distinction, including the ratchet.
- **What is the actual drift rate of an end?** The page asserts "far more slowly" without a number. Even an order of magnitude — centuries? millennia? — would convert this from a qualitative claim into something that could be checked against the historical record.
- **Does this generalize past humans?** Trunk 1 is about *agents*, not humans specifically. If the constitutive story is right, an artificial agent's "nature" would be its own fixed property-set — which makes this page's question the one to ask about [LLMs as actors](../economics/llm-praxeology.md), and a different answer there would be informative about the framework rather than about the machines.
