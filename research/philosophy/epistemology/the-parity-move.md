# The Parity Move
> When someone says *"you can't account for X, therefore your position is unjustified — only mine can"*, apply the demand to their own terminus. It never refutes their conclusion. It converts a claim of **uniqueness** into a **draw** — and a draw is the whole prize, because the argument then has to be won on the structure instead of at the foundation. The leveler is the door, not the room.

The move was already in the vault in three places and portable from none of them: as a tactics list inside [Force Doctrine](../morality/force-doctrine.md) (a *morality* thesis, where it reads as a Wilson-specific counter), as a warning inside [The Regress Problem](./the-regress-problem.md) ("weaponizing it into *your beliefs are arbitrary* is an overreach"), and as a boomerang inside the [TAG debate review](../../debates/god-precondition-knowledge-nicks-allen.md). It kept getting re-derived because it had no home.

Chris, on promoting it: *"this type of argument actually comes up a lot."* It does — in TAG, in moral-grounding debates, in political-legitimacy arguments, and anywhere someone claims a privileged exemption from the regress.

## The Structure

The move answers one argument shape, which is worth being able to recognise on sight:

> 1. Your position P depends on foundation F
> 2. You cannot account for F
> 3. Therefore P is unjustified
> 4. My position Q accounts for F
> 5. Therefore only Q is justified

Steps 1–3 are usually correct and should be conceded immediately. **The move targets step 4**, by asking the question step 4 exempted itself from: *and what accounts for your terminus?*

There are only two answers, and this is the general form of the dilemma:

| Answer | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Nothing — it's brute / self-attesting** | Then Q rests on an unaccounted foundation too. **Parity.** Step 5 fails; the uniqueness claim is gone |
| **Something else** | Then that something is the real ground, not Q. **Regress or relocation.** Step 4 was false as stated |

Both exits kill step 5. Neither touches the truth of Q.

## Euthyphro Is The Moral Instance Of Exactly This

Chris's read — *"it is similar to 'is it good because God is good or is it good because God says it is'"* — is right, and the relationship is tighter than an analogy. **Euthyphro is this dilemma with F = moral obligation.**

- **Horn 1:** X is good *because God commands it.* Then God could have commanded cruelty, and goodness is divine preference backed by ultimate force. That is **arbitrariness relocated one floor up** — the very charge being levelled at the secularist. *Parity.*
- **Horn 2:** God commands X *because X is good.* Then the truth-maker is whatever makes X good, and God is not it. *Uniqueness fails.*

Same two exits, same target. So Euthyphro is not a separate weapon to learn — it is the parity move in ethics, and the [regress page](./the-regress-problem.md) already grouped it correctly as a *diagnostic* alongside Agrippa and the halting problem.

The generalisation is worth stating plainly, because it is what makes the move portable:

> **Any claim of the form "only my terminus grounds F" faces a two-horned parity dilemma, whatever F is** — morality, logic, universals, predication, political legitimacy, the reliability of reason. Substitute F and the two horns write themselves.

## Scope Discipline — What It Does Not Do

This is the load-bearing section, and skipping it is how the move goes wrong.

**It does not refute the conclusion.** Showing that an argument for Q fails is not showing Q is false — that is the [fallacy fallacy](../tangents/the-fallacy-fallacy.md). A bad argument for a true conclusion is common and unremarkable.

**The vault's own position makes this concrete.** Chris is a theist. The target has never been God:

> *"I have never taken the position that logic being constructed disproves God, it was always the opposite of the themes presented.. 'logic proves God exists' that I take issue with."*

So parity here is aimed at a *proof*, not at a belief. It says: **this argument does not establish what it claims to establish.** That is the whole claim, and stating its limits out loud is what keeps it honest — and, tactically, what stops the exchange collapsing into a fight about theism that was never the disagreement.

**It does not license "therefore all foundations are equal."** The regress page is explicit that the trilemma does not say all systems are equal, only that all grounding takes one of three forms. Parity establishes a *tie at the foundation*, which is precisely why the argument must then be had somewhere else.

**And it is not a universal solvent — using it as one makes you the thing you are criticising.** [Force Doctrine](../morality/force-doctrine.md) diagnoses the opposite error in Wilson: *"every move is the selective application of a universal solvent… he wins only by acid-washing the opponent's side of the table and wiping his own clean."* A leveler applied to one side of the table is the same offence with the polarity reversed. **If you deploy parity, you have accepted that you are inside it too.**

## The Collection Step: Door, Not Room

The move is only half an argument. Conceding the tie is where most people stop, and stopping there is a draw offered and never collected.

> **Concede the leveler, then collect:** *"Agreed — neither of us gets a neutral exemption. So we're back to arguing whose position is structurally better. Let's. That's the argument your framing was built to skip."*

This is [Force Doctrine](../morality/force-doctrine.md)'s formulation — *the leveler is the door, not the room* — and it lands exactly on **Move 2** of [The Regress Problem](./the-regress-problem.md): once no foundation is self-justifying, what remains to evaluate is the **structure** built on it, by coherence, predictive power, and observed outcomes. Peano's axioms are unprovable and bridges still stand.

So the full sequence is four beats:

1. **Concede 1–3 fully and early.** Yes, my foundation is not self-justifying. This costs nothing and removes the opponent's best material.
2. **Apply the demand symmetrically.** What accounts for yours?
3. **Name the exit they take** — brute/self-attesting (parity) or something-else (relocation).
4. **Walk through the door.** Whose structure tracks reality better? That is a real argument, it is winnable, and it is the one the transcendental framing existed to avoid.

## The Legitimate Escape — And Why The Vault Endorses One

Parity is not unanswerable, and pretending otherwise is the over-deployment error. There is a real third option: **relocate the grounding relation so that neither horn applies.**

The vault takes this seriously in the moral case and endorses it. Aquinas took Euthyphro's second horn and made it structural: murder is wrong because of what rational creatures *are*, and God is the **Author of the natures, not the author of the duties**. As [Force Doctrine](../morality/force-doctrine.md) puts it, *"natural law is constitutive realism with a theistic Author bolted on"* — and this correction is *more* Christian than the fiat version, not less.

Note what happened there. Horn 2 was accepted, and the uniqueness claim was dropped in exchange for keeping the conclusion. That is the honest outcome of parity: **the argument is surrendered, the belief survives.** An opponent who does this has not been refuted; they have been moved off a bad argument onto a better position, and the disagreement afterward is an ordinary one about structure. That is a good result and should be treated as one.

The same escape is available for logic — logic as constitutive of reasoning rather than decreed. That is [Move 1](./the-regress-problem.md) of the regress page, the performative escape, and it is the vault's own exit. **Which means the vault's foundation is not brute either**; it claims a different *kind* of justification. Anyone running parity should expect that reply and be able to say why their version of it works.

## A Fourth Option, Not a Fourth Horn

The question left open above — whether the performative escape defeats Agrippa's trilemma or coexists with it — is what separates the vault from the Objectivist critics who run the same instrument. Chris's read:

> *"I suppose I think that performative grounding is another horn. Though I am not sure it can be used everywhere. the term 'self-evident' seems to not carry much.. **was the 5th postulate 'self-evident'?** 'validated in use' is better, this is the performative part."*

That is the answer, and the P5 question is what makes it precise.

### "Self-evident" is not a credential — it is a feeling with a track record

The Objectivist route needs the axioms to be **self-evident**. Test that credential against the one case the vault has worked in full ([Formal Systems and Approximation](../logic-and-math/formal-systems-and-approximation.md)): **the parallel postulate was the paradigm case of a self-evident truth for two thousand years, and it was wrong.** Kant staked his epistemology on it. Saccheri derived hyperbolic geometry, found no contradiction, and rejected it as *"repugnant to the nature of the straight line"* — substituting intuitive repugnance for a proof, and publishing it as one.

So self-evidence has been falsified as a foundation-credential by the clearest available case. Anything resting on it inherits that record.

### The two credentials come apart — and P5 is the discriminator

The move that does the work is separating two things that get conflated:

| Credential | Claim | P5 | Non-contradiction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Self-evident** | I can see directly that it is true | **claimed, for 2,000 years — and false** | claimed |
| **Validated in use** | you cannot coherently operate without it; denial deploys it | **never held, and demonstrably not so** | **holds** |

The second row is the decisive one. **P5 was never validated in use and could not be — Euclid proved his first 28 propositions without it**, and an entire consistent geometry exists in which it fails. Non-contradiction is the opposite: the denial requires the thing denied.

**So the objection that kills self-evidence leaves the performative escape standing.** That is the strongest available defence of Move 1, and it arrives from the very test that looked like a threat to it.

### The operability test, and what it sorts

The credential yields a usable test — **can you operate without it?** Run it on the three laws:

| | Can you operate without it? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity (A is A)** | nothing to deny — it asserts nothing | tautology |
| **Non-contradiction** | **no** — denial deploys it | constitutive; performatively grounded |
| **Excluded middle** | **yes** — intuitionistic mathematics, Coq, Lean, shipping verified software | an empirical bet, not a precondition |
| **The parallel postulate** | **yes** — 28 propositions, plus hyperbolic geometry | not even a candidate |

**Note what happened: the operability test independently sorts the three laws exactly as [the hub's unbundling](../logic-and-math/the-absoluteness-claim.md) does** — one tautology, one bookkeeping rule that genuinely cannot be abandoned, one empirical bet. Two unrelated methods, the same partition. By the vault's own standard that convergence is the evidence, not the argument.

It also shows the scope limit Chris flagged (*"I am not sure it can be used everywhere"*) is real and narrow. The escape reaches non-contradiction, one's own existence as a reasoner, and agency-as-effort. It does **not** reach excluded middle, the parallel postulate, specific moral norms, or God. **That narrowness is what makes it credible** — a foundation-generator that validated everything would validate nothing.

### Why it is not a fourth *horn*

Chris's instinct is right that there is a fourth option; "horn" is the one word to avoid, and the reason is exactly where the Objectivists go wrong.

Agrippa's three horns describe how a **chain of propositional justification** can terminate: brute assertion, infinite regress, or circle. The performative escape **is not a chain.** It does not justify P by deriving P from something else; it shows that asserting ¬P deploys P. It is not another exit from the taxonomy — it is outside the taxonomy's subject matter.

> **Which is precisely why it does not refute the trilemma.** The trilemma remains a true description of propositional justification. The escape shows that *not everything requires propositional justification.* Those are compatible claims about different questions.

The Objectivists in the [Void Podcast critique](../../debates/malpass-dyer-tag-logic.md#the-critic-video--objectivists-and-a-three-way-fight) slide from *"there is something the trilemma does not cover"* to *"the trilemma is self-refuting."* That is the same **scope failure** this page warns about in its own instrument: a valid tool pushed past its domain. Calling the escape a "horn" concedes the frame that makes the slide look reasonable — it puts the escape inside the taxonomy, where its existence then reads as the taxonomy being incomplete and therefore broken.

**So: the vault is not at parity with a self-attesting foundation, and it does not need the trilemma to be false.** It needs the trilemma to be exactly what it is — a diagnostic about chains — plus a small, well-fenced set of things that are not chains at all.

## Arbitrary Origin, Real Obligation

The other half of Chris's contribution, and it answers a different objection: *"morals are just preferences unless grounded in God."*

> **Chris:** *"a person doesnt have to burn themselves over and over in order to realize putting thier hand in a fire is bad.. likewise, things can be 'abitrary', but still have moral wieght. In the US, people drive on the right, in England, they drive on the left. The choice is arbitray. But it is clear to say that when in the US, it is good to drive on the right, and it is wrong to drive on the left.. while in England this is reversed.. arbitray, but still real. Some may say these are not moral claims, but I think that is a misunderstanding of morality."*

Full treatment as a specimen on [Constructed ≠ Arbitrary](../constructed-not-arbitrary.md#the-driving-specimen--arbitrary-content-real-obligation). Why it belongs here: **it is the same structural point as the operability test, one level down.** "Validated in use" and "arbitrary in content" are not opposites — the driving convention is both at once, which is why treating *arbitrary* and *binding* as exclusive is the error the whole cluster keeps running into. The choice of side is elective; the requirement to coordinate is constitutive; and once the convention is established, violating it endangers people who reasonably relied on it, which is an ordinary moral wrong of the same shape as breaking a promise.

## Specimens

The debates this thesis was assembled from. Each contributes something the others don't.

- **[Rights Don't Exist, Only Force (Wilson vs 5)](../../debates/wilson-rights-dont-exist-only-force.md)** and **[Force Doctrine](../morality/force-doctrine.md)** — the origin of the *door-not-room* formulation, and the diagnosis of the mirror error (the universal solvent applied to one side of the table only).
- **[Christians Have as Much Right to Rule as Secularists](../../debates/wilson-christians-right-to-rule.md)** — the cleanest field test: five graded opponents, and **only the one who concedes the symmetry and walks through the door survives.** Evidence that beat 4 is the load-bearing one.
- **[Is God the Necessary Precondition for Knowledge? (Nicks vs Allen)](../../debates/god-precondition-knowledge-nicks-allen.md)** `:77` — the regularity-trap boomerang: *"if pragmatic justification is insufficient for regularity, it's insufficient for your belief in God."* Parity applied to a TAG conclusion.
- **[Malpass vs Dyer — TAG and the Laws of Logic](../../debates/malpass-dyer-tag-logic.md)** — the hardest case, and the one that shows the move's ceiling. Dyer runs explicit **worldview holism** (*"there's no starting point that's neutral or arbitrary"*) and **openly accepts circularity at the foundation**. Against an opponent who concedes parity in advance, beats 1–3 are free and the entire contest is beat 4. This specimen is why the collection step is not optional.
- **[The Void Podcast critique of Dyer](../../debates/malpass-dyer-tag-logic.md#the-critic-video--objectivists-and-a-three-way-fight)** — the move arrived at **independently, by Objectivists**, against Dyer's embrace of foundational circularity: *“the circularity is a good thing on the … paradigmatic level of axioms … **it's good for me, but not for thee**.”* Evidence the move is a natural response to the argument shape rather than a vault invention. Also the clearest case of the **scope failure** this page warns about: they pair a correct parity charge with the claim that Agrippa's trilemma is *self-refuting* — running the leveler while denying it reaches their own foundation, which is acid-washing one side of the table.
- **[Agrippa's Trilemma (Wilson vs Whatever)](../../debates/agrippa-axioms-wilson-whatever.md)** — the trilemma itself in live use, and the mistranslations between foundationalist and Agrippa-user.
- **[The Consistency Defeater](../logic-and-math/the-consistency-defeater.md)** — parity applied not to a conclusion but to the **inference engine**: consistency is a precondition of the proof, cannot be established from inside, and the opponent's own criterion then convicts his own argument.

## Open Questions

1. ~~**Is the constitutive escape genuinely different in kind, or is it parity's third horn wearing better clothes?**~~ **LARGELY ANSWERED 2026-09-10** — see [A Fourth Option, Not a Fourth Horn](#a-fourth-option-not-a-fourth-horn) below. Residue: the answer turns on the *operability test*, and the test's edge cases are unmapped (induction and the reliability of memory are the hard ones — arguably you cannot operate without them, but their denial is not obviously self-defeating in the way non-contradiction's is).
2. **Does parity work against an opponent who concedes it up front?** Dyer does. Presuppositionalism *affirms* foundational circularity and claims the Christian circle is vindicated by "the impossibility of the contrary." Against that, beats 1–3 are already granted — so the move reduces entirely to beat 4, and the interesting question becomes whether "impossibility of the contrary" survives the existence of *built, consistent* contraries.
3. **When is a tu quoque legitimate and when is it a dodge?** [Force Doctrine](../morality/force-doctrine.md) says *"a tu quoque is a draw"* and treats that as the goal. But tu quoque is normally a fallacy. The distinction being relied on: parity attacks the *criterion* the opponent introduced, not the opponent. Worth stating rigorously — it is the difference between this move and mere whataboutism.
4. **Does the door/room framing let both sides claim victory?** Both parties can announce they have entered the room and are winning on structure. Is there a way to make beat 4 *adjudicable*, or does it always devolve to the [weighting problem](./weighting-problem.md)?
