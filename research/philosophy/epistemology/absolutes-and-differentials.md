# Absolutes and Differentials — Frame Selection as the Political Crux
> Chris's thesis: a great many political disagreements are not disputes about facts or values but about **which frame the facts get stated in** — and each side selects the frame that favors it. One argues the *absolute* (how are things?), the other the *differential* (how do they compare, or which way are they moving?). Both can be stating truths, which is exactly why the argument never resolves and why it generates strife rather than policy.

**Links:** [The Weighting Problem](./weighting-problem.md) (the parent — this is its *selection* step weaponized in rhetoric), [The Scope Confusion Thesis](../morality/scope-confusion.md) (the sibling — same "the disagreement isn't where you think it is" shape, different dimension), [The Load-Bearing Word](../../debates/the-load-bearing-word.md), [Reading Outcome Statistics](./reading-outcome-statistics.md), [The Fallacy Fallacy](../tangents/the-fallacy-fallacy.md)

## The pattern

Take any quantity with a political charge — income, crime, emissions, deaths, capability. There are at
least two true things to say about it:

| Frame | The question it asks | What it licenses |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute** | What is the level? Is the problem being fixed? | A **task** — something to do |
| **Differential** | Compared to whom? Moving which way? | A **blame assignment** — someone to hold responsible |

Both frames can be simultaneously correct on the same data. Real median income rose *and* the gap
widened. Suicide rates are flat *and* the level is terrible. That is not a paradox; they are answers
to different questions.

**The failure is not that one frame is wrong. It is that the frame gets selected — usually
silently, usually motivated — and then the selection does the persuasive work while appearing to be a
factual claim.** Each side hears the other citing true numbers to reach an obviously false
conclusion, and infers bad faith. The strife is a *byproduct of the frame mismatch*, and it can run
indefinitely because neither side is lying.

### Why the differential frame is the strife-generating one

Chris's specific claim, and the reason this is not merely a symmetric "both sides" observation:

> "The framing the discussion to look at differentials instead of absolutes is a big problem and
> creates unnecessary strife."

A differential has a *counterparty* built into it. To state a gap is to name someone on the other side
of it. So the differential frame generates an enemy as a structural matter, independent of whether
the underlying numbers are bad — which is why class warfare is a differential argument **by
construction**, and why grievance politics reaches for differentials first. The absolute frame asks
whether the thing is being fixed, which has a policy answer and no villain.

The corollary is a live rhetorical warning, not just an analytical note: reframing a real grievance
from absolute to differential does not make it more defensible, even though it often makes it more
*rhetorically effective*. It changes what the grievance licenses.

## The second axis — one legible criterion standing in for a multivariate reason

Chris's Gulf-war examples add a distinct failure in the same family. Here the compression is not
level-vs-gap but **many variables → one stateable variable**:

> "'Weapons of mass destruction' — a partial truth, as it is a broad claim, but only 1 part of a
> multivariate reason. We see this today in the gulf: 'Iran cannot build a bomb,' and yet the war
> still continues."

A multivariate justification gets compressed to a single legible criterion for public consumption.
This is [The Weighting Problem](./weighting-problem.md) step 2 (selection) performed in public and
then denied — one attribute is assigned effectively all the weight, and the composite judgment is
presented as if it *were* that attribute. The compression then fails in **both** directions:

- **Criterion falsified → whole justification reads as fraud.** No stockpiles found, so the entire
  multivariate case retroactively becomes a lie, even to the extent other variables were real.
- **Criterion satisfied → the action continues anyway, exposing the proxy.** If the stated criterion
  is met and the policy does not change, the criterion was never the actual driver. The public
  argument was conducted over a proxy the whole time.

The second is the more diagnostic of the two, because it is the case where nobody can claim the
criterion was merely mistaken. *(The current Gulf specifics are live and contested; the point here is
the structural one Chris is drawing, not an adjudication of the present conflict — see
[symmetric grounding](../../../CLAUDE.md).)*

**Why it belongs on this page:** both axes are the same disease — *the argument is conducted over a
selected proxy rather than the actual question, and the selection is invisible.* Absolute-vs-
differential selects a **frame**; WMD-vs-multivariate selects a **criterion**. In both, whoever
controls the selection has largely settled the dispute before any evidence is examined.

### War as the limit case

Chris's reason for reaching for war examples rather than domestic-policy ones:

> "Wars tend to give simple reasons for every complicated matter."

That is the compression at its most extreme: the highest-stakes, most thoroughly multivariate
decisions a state makes are the ones handed to the public in a single sentence. War justification is
[*stated reason ≠ cause*](./reading-outcome-statistics.md) — a **bucket on a form that sounds like a
mechanism** — operating at civilizational scale. Three distinct failure shapes, all of them Chris's:

**1. Trigger ≠ cause.**
> "WW1 started because the archduke was assassinated — sure, countries mobilized and went to war
> shortly after that. But it wasn't the only reason, just a trigger."

The temporal link is real and that is exactly what makes it persuasive. But a trigger *releases*
accumulated pressure; it is not the pressure. Alliance structures, mobilization timetables, naval
rivalry, and colonial competition were all loaded before Sarajevo. The trigger selected the **moment
and the pretext**, not the outcome. This is the same shape as the vault's conservation-of-imbalance
reasoning in [All of the Above](../../../notes/fiscal-pressure-must-leak-somehow.md): when pressure
must route somewhere, the visible precipitating event names *which channel opened*, and mistaking it
for the cause means treating a horn-selection as the explanation.

**2. The breadth dodge — a claim wide enough to be technically satisfied and substantively false.**
> "When the US went into Iraq, did they find any weaponry that someone can classify as 'WMD'? I bet
> this for sure. But generally this was a lie, and we started it for many, many alternate reasons."

This is why "partial truth" is the precise description rather than a hedge. A criterion defined
broadly enough is nearly guaranteed a technically-qualifying instance, so it can be *defended* after
the fact — while the impression it was deployed to create (an active, strategically-significant
program justifying invasion) was false. The breadth is doing two jobs at once: **persuasive power up
front** (everyone hears the strong reading) and **retreat cover afterward** (the weak reading is
literally true). Compare the *inflation* move in
[The Load-Bearing Word](../../debates/the-load-bearing-word.md) — widening a term toward vacuity —
except here the widening is built in at the definition stage precisely so the retreat is available
later. **[Motte-and-bailey](../tangents/the-fallacy-fallacy.md) with the motte pre-registered.**

**3. The same shape recurs, which is the actual evidence for the pattern.** US entry into WW1 — *"was
it really just over sinking a liner?"* One compressed justification is an anecdote; the recurrence
across independent cases is what makes this structural rather than a claim about any particular war.

### Pearl Harbor as the archduke of WW2

Chris's sharpest formulation, and it does all the work the conspiracy framing was reaching for
**without requiring anyone's intent**:

> "It's ok to leave out the conspiracy — I just see Pearl Harbor as the archduke of WW2."

The parallel is exact on every structural point. The pressure was fully loaded years in advance; the
event selected the **moment and the pretext**; and it is nonetheless taught as *the cause*. What was
already loaded in the Pacific by late 1941:

- **The oil embargo and asset freeze** (July–August 1941, following Japan's move into southern French
  Indochina) — the decisive one. It put Japan on a **use-it-or-lose-it clock**: reserves sufficient
  for roughly two years, so either abandon the China position or seize the Dutch East Indies for oil.
  Seizing them meant war with the US regardless. Preceded by the 1940 Export Control Act and the
  scrap-metal embargo.
- **US material support for China** since 1937 — Lend-Lease aid, credits, and the covertly-sanctioned
  American Volunteer Group (the Flying Tigers), recruited and equipped in 1941 with government
  blessing while the US was formally neutral. This is Chris's *"fighting by proxy"*, and it is
  literally that.
- **The ABCD encirclement** (America, Britain, China, Dutch) and the Hull Note of 26 November 1941.
- **The southern-resource-area problem** — Japanese planning toward the Dutch East Indies and the
  threat that posed to Australia and Allied lines of communication.

**Chris's read here is mainstream historiography, not a fringe position.** The standard account of the
strike's logic is exactly his: the embargo created a clock, the clock forced a choice, and the choice
entailed war with the US — so the Combined Fleet struck first to buy operational time. *"We were going
to war whether we liked it or not, and this was the main impetus to strike first at Pearl."* Note the
direction: the pressure explains **the Japanese decision to strike**, which is what the archduke
parallel needs. It requires no claim about American foreknowledge at all.

### "Allowed to happen" — two claims that must not be merged

This is the distinction the page originally collapsed, and getting it right is what makes the guard
usable rather than a blanket veto. **"It was allowed to happen" names two very different theses:**

| | **Weak — provocation posture** | **Strong — specific foreknowledge** |
|---|---|---|
| **Claim** | The administration wanted war, faced isolationist opposition, and deliberately pursued policies that made a Japanese first strike likely — welcoming it as the thing that would unify opinion | Specific advance knowledge of the *Pearl Harbor attack*, withheld so the fleet would be hit |
| **Requires** | Intent about **war in general** | Intent about **one operation** |
| **Evidence** | Documented in writing and in policy | Requires a suppressed warning nobody has produced |
| **Status** | Defensible; a live historiographical position | Rejected by mainstream historiography |
| **Guard verdict** | **Survives** — it meets the "independently specifiable and independently evidenced" bar | **Bites** — this is the substitution the guard forbids |

**Chris's formulation lands on the weak version:**

> "The politicians did it in such a way to gain sympathy for a cause they already wanted."

*Did it in such a way* is a claim about **policy shaping**, not about a withheld warning — and policy
shaping is exactly the kind of intent that leaves a paper trail. The embargo chain, Lend-Lease, Plan
Dog's Germany-first commitment, and the escalating undeclared naval confrontation in the Atlantic are
all documented policy. So is the posture itself: Stimson's diary for 25 November 1941 records the
question as *how we should maneuver them into the position of firing the first shot without allowing
too much danger to ourselves.* (Its interpretation is genuinely contested — it is plausibly read as
anticipating a strike on Allied possessions rather than on Hawaii — but the **posture** it records is
not in doubt.)

**So a correction to this page as first written:** the guard's escape clause is *"unless separately
evidenced,"* and the weak version **satisfies it.** My original framing implied any "allowed to
happen" reading re-commits the single-criterion error. That is true of the *strong* version and not
of the *weak* one, which stays multivariate — it says the pressure was deliberately built, not that
one hidden decision explains the outcome. Chris is right that it is the better read; it is also
compatible with the archduke framing rather than an alternative to it. **The archduke parallel and the
provocation posture are the same claim seen from two ends:** pressure was loaded on purpose, and the
trigger — whenever and wherever it came — would supply the pretext.

**Naval detail, refined.** Chris: *"the BBs hit were WW1-era ships, not modernized ones."* Half right,
and the correction cuts both ways. They were WWI-era **hulls**, but most had received substantial
interwar reconstruction (machinery, deck armor, anti-aircraft). The genuinely modern fast
battleships — *North Carolina* and *Washington*, commissioned 1941 — were in the Atlantic, so the
Pacific battle line was indeed the older force. Two facts that matter more for the bait question:
**six of the eight were repaired and returned to service**, so the loss was far less permanent than
it looked on 8 December; and **"obsolete" is hindsight** — in 1941 the battleship was still widely
taken to be the capital ship, and the battle line's destruction was received as catastrophe, not a
cheap trade. An inference resting on valuing those losses the way *we* value them is running the
scoreboard backward into intent. The carrier absences likewise have documented mundane causes
(aircraft ferry runs to Wake and Midway; one in refit stateside). None of this touches the weak
version, which never needed the ships to be bait.

### How the single criterion gets manufactured — a cost/durability ladder

Chris's closing observation opens a taxonomy the page needs:

> "WMD might be the 'poor man's' way of manipulating the public vs. allowing Pearl to happen."

If the public requires one sentence for a multivariate decision, there is more than one way to supply
it, and they differ sharply in **what they cost** and **how long they survive contact with reality**:

| Tier | Method | Cost | Durability | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Fabricate or inflate** | Assert a criterion (WMD) | Cheap, fast, unilateral | **Low** — falsifiable in principle and in fact | Collapses when checked; poisons the well for later true claims |
| **2. Provoke** | Shape conditions until the adversary supplies the criterion (embargo → first strike) | High — slow, patient, needs an adversary who will act, risks real losses | **Very high** — self-authenticating | Rarely fails; the cost is paid in advance and in blood |
| **3. Argue the actual case** | Present the multivariate reason honestly | Highest *political* cost — will not compress into a sentence | Total | Usually not attempted; loses to Tiers 1–2 in the attention market |

**The asymmetry that makes "poor man's" exactly the right phrase:** Tier 2 buys something Tier 1 can
never buy — a justification that **cannot be debunked, because at the surface level it is simply
true.** Japan really did attack Pearl Harbor. You can contest *why* it happened; you cannot contest
*whether*. Tier 1 has no such cover, which is why the WMD claim survived roughly two years and the
Pearl Harbor casus belli has survived eighty. Tier 1 is what you reach for when you lack the time,
the patience, or an adversary who will oblige.

This also explains the **breadth dodge** above as a Tier-1 refinement: defining the criterion broadly
is how you buy a *fraction* of Tier 2's truth cover on a Tier-1 budget — a weak reading that stays
literally true, pre-installed beneath the strong reading that does the persuading.

**The uncomfortable corollary, and the reason this ladder belongs on this page rather than in a
politics note:** the ranking is by *durability of the justification*, not by *honesty*, and the two
run in opposite directions at the top. Tier 3 is the honest option and the weakest competitor. A
public that will only accept one-sentence reasons is a public that has selected for Tiers 1 and 2 —
so the compression this page diagnoses is not merely a rhetorical vice practiced on audiences, it is
partly **demanded by** them.

### The guard — pattern does not establish intent

This is the load-bearing constraint, and it is Chris's own, stated in the same breath as the examples:

> "If these are really true or not, we will probably never know. We don't know the real minds of the
> leaders involved, and often this happens in a chaotic way that it might not even be intentional."

Three separate limits, each sufficient on its own:

1. **Minds are not recoverable.** Intent is the one variable no document settles. Even candid private
   records show what someone was willing to write down.
2. **Chaos mimics design.** A process with no coordinating author can produce exactly the signature of
   one — institutional momentum, bureaucratic self-protection, and motivated reasoning at scale all
   leave the same fingerprints as a plan. *It might not even be intentional* is not a softening; it is
   a genuinely distinct causal hypothesis that fits the same evidence.
3. **Innocent explanations are individually available.** Each Pearl Harbor item has one — the radar
   contact was read as expected B-17s. Chris grants this directly (*"can be due to something else"*),
   and mainstream historiography rejects deliberate-foreknowledge accounts. Convergence of
   suggestive items is not additive proof when each is independently explicable.

**So the claim that survives is the weaker and far more defensible one: the stated reason
*underdetermines* the decision.** That is fully supported and requires no theory of anyone's mind.
The claim that does *not* survive is "the real reason was X" — which needs the intent the first three
limits deny you.

> **Guard, in the idiom of [force-doctrine](../morality/force-doctrine.md)'s:** the alternate reasons
> must be **independently specifiable and independently evidenced**, or "the stated reason is never
> the real reason" collapses into unfalsifiable conspiracy — the mirror image of vacuous historicism.
> A pattern that explains every case equally well explains no case in particular. Note this is the
> same vacuity blade the vault uses in
> [Variance Is Not Luck](../../economics/variance-is-not-luck.md) (*if everything is luck, nothing
> is luck*) and against relative poverty metrics — **turned here on our own tool.**

The honest output is therefore a *subtraction*, not a substitution: the single stated criterion is
shown insufficient, the decision is restored to its multivariate character, and the question of which
factors actually dominated is left open unless separately evidenced. That is less satisfying than a
hidden-real-reason story, and it is the part that is actually defensible.

**The sharpest reason to stop at subtraction — the substitution can re-commit the original error.** A
hidden-real-reason story *of the single-hidden-decision kind* replaces one simple cause with a
different simple cause, collapsing a multivariate situation into a single legible criterion all over
again, sign flipped. In that form, **conspiracy framing is single-criterion compression wearing the
costume of sophistication** — the disease presenting as the diagnosis.

**But the guard is a standard, not a veto — and this is where it is easiest to misapply.** The escape
clause is *"unless separately evidenced,"* and some substitutions clear it. The test is whether the
alternative is **independently specifiable and independently evidenced**, and whether it **stays
multivariate**. A claim that policy was deliberately shaped to make conflict likely is evidenced by
policy documents and leaves the situation multivariate; a claim that one concealed decision produced
the outcome needs a document nobody has and re-compresses the explanation. The first survives; the
second does not. See
[the weak/strong split at Pearl Harbor](#allowed-to-happen--two-claims-that-must-not-be-merged), above
— the worked case, and the one that corrected an over-broad first draft of this guard.

## Where this attaches — the lossy culture → politics arrow

Chris's placement of the whole hub:

> "This was the 'lossy' arrow of culture → politics. We see this even today where an event is used to
> champion a cause that really isn't what the cause is about — and it seems lying about it no longer
> matters. *'Hands up, don't shoot!'*"

This is the same mechanism as
[Technology → Culture → Politics](../dynamics/technology-culture-politics.md)'s **Hop 2**, where *the
absorbed form drops the qualifier and keeps the permission* — applied to **justifications** rather
than doctrines. A multivariate grievance does not reach politics as stated; it reaches it *as
absorbed*, and the absorbed version is a single legible criterion. The qualifier is, as that page puts
it, precisely the part that costs something to keep. **Nobody has to intend it — coarsening is what
transmission does.**

**The specimen.** After the August 2014 shooting of Michael Brown in Ferguson, *"hands up, don't
shoot"* became the movement's slogan. In March 2015 the DOJ issued **two** reports:

- On the shooting itself — the evidence did **not** support the claim that Brown had his hands up in
  surrender; no charges against Officer Wilson.
- On the **Ferguson Police Department** — a documented pattern of unconstitutional policing and
  revenue-driven municipal enforcement falling disproportionately on Black residents.

**That pairing is the entire thesis of this page in one case.** The multivariate grievance was real
and was substantiated *by the same department in the same month*; it was compressed into one legible
criterion that did not hold at that incident. And the compression was **actively costly to the cause**
— it made the documented systemic findings hostage to a specific claim that could be, and was,
checked. The strongest available evidence was the part that got dropped, because it does not fit on a
sign.

**Note the political symmetry, which is why this belongs here and not in a partisan note.** WMD and
*"hands up, don't shoot"* are the same structural move executed by opposite coalitions. The pattern is
a property of how justification transmits to publics, not of a faction —
[symmetric grounding](../../../CLAUDE.md) applies.

**The observation that modifies the ladder:** *"it seems lying about it no longer matters."* The
cost/durability table above assumes falsification is expensive — that Tier 1 is cheap but fragile, and
that is what made provocation worth its enormous price. If a falsified criterion no longer damages the
cause it was minted for, **Tier 1 gets cheap *and* durable, and the ladder's discipline collapses.**
Provocation was only ever worth paying for because fabrication could be checked; remove the check and
nobody needs the patient method. Whether this is a real secular shift or the familiar illusion of
recency is left open below — but if real, it is the most consequential claim on this page, because
every other mechanism here is downstream of whether being caught still costs anything.

## Specimens already in the vault

This axis was hit repeatedly before it was named — which is the argument for its generality.

- **[Is Masculinity in Crisis?](../../debates/wordwar-masculinity-crisis-owlish-garcia.md)** — the
  cleanest specimen. Owlish argues the **derivative** (rates are flat → no crisis), Garcia the
  **level** (the absolute state is bad → crisis). Both legitimate senses; they never collide, and
  that page already carries the general diagnostic: *"check whether one is arguing the derivative and
  the other the absolute value. Usually neither has noticed."*
- **[Poverty as a Sign of Exploitation](../../debates/poverty-exploitation-prep.md)** — carries the
  decisive argument against relative metrics: **relative poverty is definitionally permanent** —
  triple everyone's real income and the rate does not move, so *a metric that can never reach zero
  cannot be a sign of anything.* Also the Marxist **relative immiseration** reading, which explicitly
  permits real wages to rise while exploitation rises — a differential frame chosen precisely because
  the absolute one stopped cooperating.
- **[The Productivity–Pay Gap](../../economics/productivity-pay-gap.md)** — the slide from "labor's
  share fell" (a differential) to "workers aren't paid for what they produce" (an absolute claim about
  the wage relation). Two different claims; only one is supported.
- **[Lyn Alden / trade deficits](../../economics/lyn-alden-trade-deficit-analysis.md)** — the vault's
  own framing correction: the affordability crisis is a **relative-prices and distribution** story,
  *not* an income-decline story. Confusing "asset prices outpaced income" with "real income fell" is
  named there as the most common mistake on the topic.
- **[Freitas & Christian on the reserve currency](../../debates/freitas-vance-reserve-currency.md)**
  (2026-08-17) — where Chris stated the thesis. The populist grievance is real in absolute terms, but
  framing it as a differential is the on-ramp by which the DSA converts a monetary problem into
  revolutionary policy. Freitas's counter is the absolute frame as parable: *"your enemy is not the
  person who knows how to swim — the enemy is the people that keep making the waters difficult."*
- **[Measuring Inflation](../../economics/measuring-inflation.md)** — the upstream mechanic: "real"
  quantities are nominal series divided by a *chosen* index, so basket selection sits above nearly
  every one of these disputes while being the least-audited step.

## The diagnostic

When a political argument will not resolve despite both sides citing accurate figures, ask in order:

1. **Is one side stating a level and the other a gap or a trend?** If so, the dispute is not factual.
2. **Which frame does each side's conclusion require?** If the frame tracks the side rather than the
   subject, the selection is motivated.
3. **Is a single stated criterion carrying a multivariate justification?** If so, ask what happens to
   the policy when the criterion is *satisfied*. If nothing changes, it was a proxy.
4. **Is the criterion a trigger or a cause?** A trigger releases accumulated pressure and selects the
   moment; it does not supply the pressure. Ask what was already loaded.
5. **Is the criterion broad enough to be technically satisfied while substantively false?** If a weak
   reading is pre-registered inside a strong-sounding claim, expect the retreat to it later.
6. **Stop at subtraction.** Having shown the stated reason insufficient, do *not* substitute a
   confident alternative — that requires intent you cannot access (see the guard above).
7. **Only then argue the numbers.** Arguing them first is how these run for decades.

## Open questions

1. **Is the absolute frame always the better one?** Chris's position is that it is the problem-fixing
   frame. But differentials are genuinely the right tool for some questions — positional goods, market
   power, and anything where relative standing *is* the causal variable. Where is the boundary?
2. **Relationship to [The Load-Bearing Word](../../debates/the-load-bearing-word.md)** — that page's
   *degree* family (a word needing a failure condition) overlaps this heavily. Is frame-selection a
   fifth family there, or is the load-bearing-word analysis a special case of this?
3. **Does this subsume or merely neighbor [Scope Confusion](../morality/scope-confusion.md)?** Both say
   "the disagreement is not where the participants think it is." Kept as siblings for now.
4. **Has falsification actually stopped costing anything?** Chris's *"it seems lying about it no longer
   matters."* The whole cost/durability ladder is built on the assumption that a checkable criterion is
   a fragile one. Testable in principle: compare the fate of the cause after WMD (1–2 years to
   collapse, and it damaged later claims) against *"hands up, don't shoot"* (falsified within months,
   slogan persisted). Confounds to clear before believing it: the two differ in *who* was falsified
   (a government vs. a movement), in whether an institution had to defend the claim under oath, and in
   whether the underlying multivariate case survived the falsification — Ferguson's did, Iraq's largely
   did not. **The recency illusion is the null hypothesis here and has not been ruled out.**
5. **Page size.** At ~4.5k words this hub is past the vault's lean-page guidance. Natural split if it
   grows: move the war material (limit case, Pearl Harbor, the manufacture ladder, the guard) to its own
   page and leave the hub pointing at it.
