# LiquidZulu — *Anarcho-Capitalism: Responding to the Critics*
> A 116-minute video essay that is less a rebuttal of critics than a **guided tour of LZ's whole system** with the critics as pegs: anarchism as the rejection of *legal authoritarianism*, law as the branch that answers scarcity-conflict, Hoppe's argument-from-argument rebuilt on Randian rather than Kantian ground, a break with Rand on "politics," and a late-added attack on lifeboat ethics. Strong where it is deductive; thin exactly where the vault has already run the experiment.

## How to read this page

LZ entered philosophy through Peikoff and leans Randian, then rejects Rand's politics because Rand rejects anarchy. Chris's stance is the same shape from the other side — *"I also take lots of parts of Objectivism but I do not agree with it in whole"* — so this is not a hostile review. It is an introduction to how LZ thinks, with the vault's verdict at each station. Timestamps are into the raw transcript. Passages marked **(vault)** are our analysis, not LZ's claims; passages marked **(Chris)** are Chris's reads from the discussion on 2026-09-21.

## 1. "Anarchy" = rejection of legal authoritarianism (0:00–4:47, 32:05–36:34)

**LZ:** anarchism is any philosophy that rejects *legal authoritarianism* — the thesis that actions are justified because a preferred authority says so. Crusoe (first use) vs Friday (the village elder said I'm stick-keeper). "Anarchism doesn't mean no laws … it means the law is objective, inherent in nature" — hence *natural law*. The state is Rothbard's monopoly on force **plus legal positivism**, i.e. a monopoly on the *justification* of force (17:30). The "law of the jungle" and the ancoms are *not* anarchists, because might-makes-right is itself an appeal to authority (32:05); legal positivism frozen onto "law" produces the false alternative *politicians' arbitrary law vs bandits' arbitrary law* (34:07).

**(Chris) — the weakest claim; drop it.** The common tongue of "anarchy" is *lawlessness*, not "without state"; the technical sense is modern and has not caught on. Every critique LZ answers in this section could be removed by using different language. This is a thorn in most ancaps' side.

**(vault) — and it is self-inflicted by his own epistemology.** LZ cannot take Chris's exit, because §7 of this video commits him to definitions being *objectively correct or incorrect on epistemic grounds* (16:37). If the correct definition of "anarchy" is fixed by the requirements of man's conceptual faculty, he is obliged to fight the word rather than route around it. The concept-realism is what turns a vocabulary problem into a philosophical one.

## 2. The correct problem: scarcity → conflict → NAP (3:40, 86:38–88:41)

**LZ:** law tackles *scarcity*; a good is scarce iff men can come into conflict over its use, where conflict = mutually exclusive actions. Aggression = *initiation of conflict*. So the NAP is the starting point of all legal analysis, and "capitalism implies anarchism and anarchism implies capitalism" (33:05). At 86:38 he restates it as the *right question*: "we live in a world of scarce means, so men can come into conflict — how should you deal with conflicts?" Three answers: jungle, mixed, NAP.

**(vault) — this is the vault's own derivation, independently confirmed.** [Self-Ownership and Property Rights](../morality/legal-theory/self-ownership-and-property-rights.md) already replaces the self-ownership chain with exactly this: scarcity → conflict → resolution norm → first use. LZ never routes through self-ownership either. Two independent arrivals at the same root is evidence the root is load-bearing.

**(Chris) — but not all law is about scarcity.** Personal autonomy is a second root. A punch in the face violates the NAP, but the ancap move is to say you *own yourself* and an assault *damages your property* — tying everything back to property law. *"I don't think the NAP is only property — but ask Zulu, and he will make the argument above."* The vault's self-ownership page half-agrees: it says **bodies are not property** (no transfer, sale, abandonment) *but* still grounds bodily autonomy as "the first application of conflict avoidance — your body is the first scarce resource." So there is a live question inside the vault, not only against LZ: **is bodily autonomy a case of scarcity-conflict (vault page, LZ), or a distinct root (Chris, 2026-09-21)?** Registered under Open Questions below.

## 3. Hoppe rebuilt on Rand, not Kant (19:35–32:05, 93:45–95:44)

**LZ vs TIK on Kant→Mises:** he *agrees* Kant let "mysticism" into the Austrian school, rejects that a priori reasoning is any foundation for praxeology, and proposes **"epilology"** — the science of free will, containing praxeology (general statements about the man-made) and thymology (particular man-made facts). The real distinction is *conceptual vs perceptual*, not *a priori vs a posteriori* (22:02). Free will is defended by retortion: the determinist "cannot validate any belief he holds" (23:09). Then the genealogy: Rothbard's 1957 letter to Rand ("you introduced me to the whole field of natural rights … Aristotelian epistemology"), Branden's biography, and Rothbard's own gloss that the action axiom is "a law of reality rather than a law of thought, and hence 'empirical' rather than 'a priori'" (31:10). Conclusion: "anarcho-capitalism and the Rothbardian wing owe their fundamental philosophy to Ayn Rand — not Immanuel Kant."

**His argument-from-argument (94:46):** argumentation is a conflict-free interaction; to argue for aggression is to seek the other's consent while denying that consent is required — a practical contradiction. Two exits: stop arguing (become "an animal-beast governed by whim") or drop the claim. It is preceded by a separate anti-jungle argument (88:41): both disputants presuppose the *ownership/possession* distinction, so a "mere possessor" ethic is unassertable; and whim-ethics rest on whim-epistemology, which rests on the **primacy of consciousness** (91:38). Class-based mixed law is "arbitrary particularisation" of an ethic that, if it came from the nature of man, would be universal (93:45).

**(vault)** This is the lineage page's Hoppe with the transcendental vocabulary stripped out and the Randian vocabulary (primacy of existence, frozen abstraction, package deal) put in. Two notes. (a) The **liberty-right vs claim-right** objection recorded on the lineage page lands on LZ's version unchanged: "seeking consent while arguing" establishes at most that the arguer *permits* the other's exclusive control during the argument, not that anyone has a duty to respect it afterwards; and the Murphy–Callahan scope limitation (body parts needed to argue; only during the debate; not third parties) is exactly what bites in §5. (b) His anti-jungle move — *both disputants presuppose ownership ≠ possession* — is the stronger and less-discussed argument, and it is closer to the vault's [performative grounding](../morality/is-ought-and-performative-grounding.md) than the Hoppe version is: it grounds the *concept of a rights-claim* rather than a specific rights-content. It still over-derives when he goes from "there is a fact of the matter about who should control it" to "and first use is that fact."

## 4. The Rand arc — praise first, then the break (25:33, 58:24–86:38)

Chris's observation: it is fun to hear him praising Rand in the first half only to criticise her in the second. The structure is deliberate: he needs Objectivism to be the *precondition* of ancap (Peikoff's philosophy of history, 55:25 — "there must first be an acceptance of Objectivism") before he can say Rand got her own politics wrong.

**The break (83:32–86:38):** "politics is not a valid field of philosophy — law is." Peikoff's problem-statement ("what is right or wrong in terms of what the government does") is the **fallacy of the frozen abstraction** — it grafts "there should be a government" onto the question, the same error Rand herself catches in *"what will be done about the poor?"* (the collectivist premise is smuggled into the question). "Society" cannot act; a branch of philosophy asking what society should do is "murderology." The valid question is the scarcity-conflict one of §2.

**Open Objectivism, LZ's variant (67:00–83:32):** Objectivism is neither "whatever Rand believed" (closed) nor "true philosophy" (Hicks — a frozen abstraction; Biddle's objection stands) but **the philosophy of the primacy of existence** — genus *philosophy*, differentia *explicit application of the primacy of existence to every issue*. Against Ghate's Darwin/Einstein analogy: "Darwin's theories" close at his death, *evolution* does not; "Objectivism" names the theory, not the person's belief-set. He concedes Rand would have been a closed Objectivist and replies "'Ayn Rand said so' isn't an argument."

**(vault)** A genuine third position for [Objectivism Boundary §5](../morality/objectivism-boundary.md#5-closed-vs-open-system--a-partial-difference-stated-carefully), and the cleanest statement of the open side on file. Its cost: once Objectivism = "primacy of existence applied consistently," every dispute becomes a dispute about who is applying it consistently — which is what the rest of this video is.

## 5. The lifeboat, the asteroid, and death as a standard (95:44–109:21)

Added after editing began; LZ credits Gee (Proheretics) and Binswanger. Against "the NAP doesn't apply in emergencies / metaphysically abnormal circumstances," four moves:

1. **"How the hell did we get here?"** Ethics does not answer a snapshot; the immoral act is usually the negligence that produced the emergency. The asteroid-and-laser case is *Atlas Shrugged* with the sign flipped — the framing asks you to side with "the idiot masses" against the man who foresaw it (97:40).
2. **Death is not an action, so it cannot be a standard.** "Value is that which man acts to gain and/or keep — you cannot gain or keep death." Survival-as-standard reifies a zero; the same error as Cartesian doubt (taking error as prior to truth). "Death is the end of action" (100:39–101:48).
3. **Context ≠ danger.** Ethics goes out of context *iff* you can no longer choose; "we are not outside the context of ethics just because things are really scary" (102:49).
4. **Principles cannot be dropped "just this once"** (Binswanger): ethical principles are the apparatus by which values are identified; drop them and you cannot identify the value you are dropping them for (103:48). Then Hugh Glass as the empirical counterexample to "survival was impossible without aggression" (107:11).

**(Chris)** On first hearing this sounded like babble; on reflection the life/death distinction is where the theory springs leaks. It breaks down at the **edges of a life — conception/birth and death/inheritance** — which are the cases where it does not match reality. And LZ is doing more than the Rand fix: he is pulling in the **action axiom** to refute the idea that we only care about mere survival.

**(vault) — what he has actually done, and what it costs.** Chris's second read is right and it matters for the boundary page. Rand grounds the conditional in *life*; the vault grounds it in *action* ([Objectivism Boundary §1](../morality/objectivism-boundary.md#1-the-base-life-vs-action)). LZ's "death is not an action; value is what one *acts* to gain or keep" is an **action-base** argument wearing Rand's vocabulary — which puts him nearer the vault than Rand on the base, and supplies an answer-attempt to the boundary page's open question *"what are the norms of an agent pursuing death?"*: on LZ there is no such agent — pursuing non-existence is not a pursuit, so the case is not degenerate, it is empty. That is a real answer and it should be tested, not adopted (recorded on the boundary page).

The cost is precisely Chris's edges. An action-base and an argumentation-base both index rights to *an actor*. Then:

- **Before agency (conception → birth → early childhood).** The fetus and the infant neither act in the praxeological sense nor argue. On LZ's own grounds nothing derives a right for them; the Murphy–Callahan third-party objection already on the lineage page is not a quibble here, it is the whole case. This video does not touch it.
- **At death (death → inheritance).** LZ: "at the moment of death, there is no 'one' to speak of." Then at that instant the estate has no owner. Either title transferred *before* death by a contract executed in life (the Rothbard/Kinsella route — a will as a conditional title transfer), or the estate is abandoned and open to homesteading. Both are answers, but both mean the theory needs a bridge across a moment where its own axiom — an actor — is absent; and "a dead man's will binding the living" has the shape of the authority's-say-so that §1 was built to exclude. Not addressed in the video.

These are the two questions to put to him (hub → *Questions for LZ*).

## 6. Private defense, and the sore spot (36:34–55:25, 109:21–116:19)

**LZ:** Hoppe's *Private Production of Defense*, closely followed — the Hobbesian sovereign S over A and B is "a rights-protecting violator of rights, a contradiction in terms"; a world state would be "the last surviving protection racket"; the calculation problem stops even an angelic sovereign from knowing how much security to produce (41:34). Positive case: defense as an *insurable* risk (contracts bind the insured to peaceful conduct; you cannot insure what you control), collateral-damage liability pushes toward precision, premiums sort neighbourhoods, insurers already use private arbitration. Against state invasion: no casus belli against unorganised free people; the best-armed population on earth; Vietnam as proof that tonnage does not beat guerrillas (53:19); brain drain; insurers running intelligence, surgical strikes and "orbital strikes."

**Against Rand's Smith/Jones (113:19):** "what would actually happen is that neither firm wants to go to random war, so they would investigate who the aggressor is and seek damages" — plus the "war of the wallets" parody. Against "we need an arbiter of last resort" (47:37): the more independent the judges, the better; a monopoly judiciary "must necessarily tend towards a systematic perversion of justice." Law is discovered, not produced; competing arbitrators "appeal to reality to discover objective legal principles."

**(Chris)** He handles the standard militaries-and-police objection pretty well, but it stays a sore spot. On the final arbiter: *"I agree more with Rand than LZ. We have run this experiment out in reality many, many times, and 'rationality' usually does not prevail."* Rand's *honest disagreement among rational men* is the part that usually gets glossed over, and it is glossed by **two assumptions that dismiss the problem rather than accept it**: (1) *war between two REAs is expensive, so they will negotiate instead*; (2) *there IS a truth of the matter, and it can be discovered.* Neither is an argument that the problem resolves — both are assertions that it does, and history says it does not always resolve cleanly. Rand's own label, **"competing governments,"** is the accurate description of what is being asked for: *"we had this in the past; it didn't always go as smoothly as they think it will."*

**(Chris) — the refusal to describe ancapistan.** At 35:50 LZ declines to say what the market would produce — *"it is not possible for me to explain the specific bureaucratic structure that would obtain on the market … just as it wouldn't be possible to explain what the restaurant industry would look like before one had developed"* — then offers only "likely or possible features." Chris: this is weak. It is fair that he cannot predict the future or know which bugs need working out, but he *does* have a picture (the course will show it) and declines to commit to it **to avoid direct confrontation**. The "trust me bro" posture does not sell.

**(vault) — this is the collision, and LZ's reply is option 2.** Chris's two assumptions map onto the vault's pages exactly: (1) is the *trade-always-dominates-conflict* premise that [Government Formation](../morality/legal-theory/government-formation.md#trade-vs-conflict--not-always-what-libertarians-claim) already rejects (conquest is rational when the asymmetry is large enough — Rome did not trade with Gaul); (2) is the rationalist-discovery premise, and the honest version of "law is discovered" — the Hayekian one below — predicts *disagreement*, not convergence, because the information does not exist until the case is adjudicated. And the restaurant analogy has a defect of its own: nobody needed to predict the restaurant industry because a bad restaurant fails *locally*; a bad rights-enforcement equilibrium fails as a war. The asymmetry of the failure mode is what makes "I can't predict it" a non-answer here and a fine answer for restaurants. [Government Formation](../morality/legal-theory/government-formation.md#why-the-ancap-model-fails--the-jurisdiction-problem) gives two REAs with conflicting judgments three options: a shared meta-arbiter (the state with extra steps), negotiation (works at low stakes), or force. "They would investigate and seek damages" *is* negotiation, asserted to hold at all stakes — the page's claim is that it fails at existential ones, and the city-state record (poleis, Italian communes, the Hansa: federation, conquest, or absorption) is the experiment Chris means. Two further seams: (a) his defense-as-insurance premise meets the vault's [insurance page](../../economics/insurance.md) — insurance has one legitimate job, hedging catastrophic low-probability events, and routine crime is neither; (b) his "discovery" is *rationalist* — judges appeal to reality and read off the principle — whereas [Common Law as a Discovery Procedure](../morality/legal-theory/common-law-as-discovery.md) is *Hayekian*: adjudication *generates* the information at the dispute and precedent aggregates it. Both say "discovered not produced," but they disagree about where the knowledge comes from, and only one of them explains why honest judges disagree.

**Symmetric-grounding note (vault).** He quotes TIK, Rand and Peikoff at length, which is to his credit. But Rand's actual objection — *objective law needs an arbiter for honest disagreement even among fully rational men* — is answered by assertion ("never substantiated"); Peikoff's Iran clip (52:09) is used as guilt-by-association for Randian politics generally; and the Vietnam rebuttal answers "tonnage" when the Objectivist claim was political restraint on *targets*. The lifeboat section is his strongest original work; the defense section is his most borrowed.

## 7. Definitions, nominalism, and the rejection of "pragmatism" (4:47–19:35)

> ⚠ **On one question the standing is reversed (2026-09-23).** This page holds TIK only as the *target* of LZ's attacks. But TIK's own [ancap video](../tik-anarcho-capitalism.md) raises the **market-for-a-state** objection — *"as soon as one person wants a state, that need is met by the entrepreneur"* — which is the vault's own [division-of-labour thesis](../morality/legal-theory/government-formation.md) arrived at independently, i.e. the *praxeological* case for the state made with the ancaps' own apparatus. **Chris's verdict:** *"I don't think LZ responded well to all of these, but on this subject, he [TIK] stands close to me."* See also the [choice-distance gradient](../morality/legal-theory/government-formation.md#the-choice-distance-gradient--a-second-independent-limit-on-market-provision), the mechanism TIK does not supply.

**LZ vs TIK on "state":** TIK's *public = large hierarchy* is a **package deal** (bigness + collectivism); the correct criterion is *collective vs individual*, since "group ownership is a contradiction in terms" that arises "out of pure Platonism" (9:24). Then the theory behind it: "definitions are not and cannot be arbitrary labels … the words one uses must conform to the requirements of man's conceptual faculty" (9:24); package deals "are not valid concepts and cannot be knowledge." TIK's appeal to historical usage is **nominalism**, traced through Hobbes — materialism → sensualism → concepts as arbitrary conventions → no definition can be true or false → no general principles → every dispute is semantic (10:59–16:37). "Definitions can be objectively correct or incorrect on epistemic grounds."

**(Chris)** He is a bit Platonic here, and there is a rejection of empiricism the vault goes against. More broadly: LZ is a theorist and tends to reject empirics because of it; "pragmatism" and the like are slurs in his world, and that makes the theory weaker.

**(vault) — be fair to him first, then say where it fails.** LZ would reject "Platonic" — he is an Aristotelian realist about essences and explicitly calls *group ownership* the Platonic error; and he would say he accepts empiricism "properly understood" (the action axiom as a law of reality, per Rothbard at 31:10) and rejects only *modern* empiricism, i.e. nominalism-sensualism. Granted. The vault's disagreement is then precise: on [Where Concepts Live](../epistemology/where-concepts-live.md) a concept is a trained detector and the sameness between two minds' concepts lives in the world they were trained on — there is no essence for a definition to be *correct about* apart from the job the concept does. And on [Constructed ≠ Arbitrary](../constructed-not-arbitrary.md), *that is enough*: a construction with a job is evaluable, and "socialism = when the government does stuff" beats "puppydogs and rainbows" because it isolates the cause and predicts, not because it matches a Form. Notice that LZ's own criterion — "the requirements of man's conceptual faculty" — is a **functional** criterion. Read functionally it is the vault's view and he has no quarrel with us; read as correspondence to an essence it is the realism Chris is calling Platonic. He wants the second while arguing with the first, and the word he uses for the first is "pragmatism."

## Verdict — strengths and weaknesses, for the hub

**Strengths.** Clean problem-statement (§2, independently matches the vault's); the ownership/possession retortion (§3b); the frozen-abstraction diagnosis of Rand's "politics" (§4), which is a fair hit; the lifeboat section (§5), his best original work and really an action-base argument; consistently quoting opponents in full.

**Weaknesses.** Fights the word "anarchy" because his epistemology forbids the exit (§1/§7); over-derives from argumentation (§3) with the lineage page's objections untouched; has nothing for the two edges of a life (§5); answers the jurisdiction problem with negotiation-by-assertion against a historical record he does not engage (§6); and treats "pragmatism" as a slur while running on a functional criterion (§7).

## Open Questions

- **Is bodily autonomy a case of scarcity-conflict or a second root?** Chris (2026-09-21) says the NAP is not only property; the vault's own self-ownership page says bodily autonomy is the *first application* of conflict avoidance. One of these needs to give, or the disagreement needs to be shown verbal.
- **Does LZ's "death is not an action" survive the edges?** What does his system say about (a) the rights of the not-yet-acting and (b) title at the instant of death? Both are questions for him (hub).
- **What does LZ actually think ancapistan looks like?** He refuses to commit on this page (35:50) but has a picture; the course is where to find it and hold him to it.
- **Does the ownership/possession retortion (88:41) do more work than the argument-from-argument?** It looks like the modest, vault-shaped version of the move. If so it should be added to the lineage page as its own entry rather than as a Hoppe footnote.
