# LiquidZulu Course, Ch. 4 — *Contract Theory*
> The title-transfer theory (Rothbard/Evers/Kinsella): a contract is not a promise but a transfer of title, enforceable only where default would be *theft*; no such thing as "breach"; fraud is theft-by-trick; fractional-reserve banking is fraud; there is no debtors' prison because a defaulted loan stole nothing; voluntary slavery is impossible because a body cannot be *abandoned* while its actor lives — and a will is a title transfer conditioned on death. **The chapter is his best.** On slavery he does *not* hand-wave: he argues, and the argument is the vault's own slavery test, with the vault's own bundle point conceded in his words ("ownership does not imply the right to sell"). The costs show at the edges he keeps: a person whose will is destroyed becomes "legally analogous to a corpse" and *ownable*; an intestate estate is "ceded to nature" for a homestead race; and the will-on-death violates his own no-gap transfer rule. Hub question 2 now has his answer, and it is the edge Chris predicted.

## 1. Title transfer (§ *The Title-Transfer Theory of Contracts*)

**LZ:** property is antecedent to contract. Ownership can end by **abandonment** (Crusoe leaves the stick to rot — *"owning a thing involves actually possessing it along with the intent to direct the use of it; if you lose this intent you no longer own it"*, and abandonment must be objective and ascertainable) or by **transfer**, which is simultaneous abandonment-and-homesteading with *no gap* (else Xury could swoop in). A contract is enforceable iff defaulting would be **aggression** — Crusoe keeping the stick after agreeing is theft, because his agreement is evidence he relinquished it and "he cannot deny this without contradiction." Mere promises (the fiancée who does not marry) are unenforceable. Conditional transfers are valid; the paper is only *evidence*; *"there isn't really such a thing as 'breaching' a contract"* — a no-show magician has not aggressed; if you want him to show, bake in a penalty transfer, and only withholding the *penalty* is theft. Smith/Jones: if the buyer has the gold, title moved at acceptance and the gold is what he stole; if not, the condition failed and the car is.

**(vault) — clean, and it is Chris's side of the mens rea debate.** "No breach, only the penalty you contracted for" is exactly Chris's Alice-and-Bob answer: *both cases owe restitution; penalties deter without examining intent.* Agreement. Two seams inside the section. (a) *Ownership requires "the intent to direct"* — intent is back in the foundations, and it is not intersubjectively ascertainable; he patches with "abandonment must be objective," which is the right patch and makes the intent clause idle. (b) The **no-gap rule** for transfers — the transferee must take up control at the same instant — is stated here as a necessary condition; hold it for §5, where a will transfers title at the instant of death to an heir who is not there.

## 2. Fraud, and fractional reserves (§ *Fraud*)

**LZ:** fraud = **theft-by-trick** — the blunted spear sold as razor-sharp is no contract, because Crusoe "did not have the intention of transferring title to a sharp spear," so the shells were stolen. **FRB:** a 100-ounce deposit with 100 certificates is a warehouse; lending 20 ounces (or issuing 120 certificates against 100) creates more titles than gold, which "implies that people are being deceived," so it is fraud; free-banking defenders misunderstand that property precedes contract; a *disclosed* fractional-reserve arrangement is a "casino game," not fraudulent — *"but representing this casino game as a bank is. A bank is a money-warehouse."*

**(vault) — two findings, one on each half.** (a) Fraud is defined by *deception* and detected by *intent* ("did not have the intention…") — that is Claude's side of the mens rea debate, the side Chris rejected: *the penalty makes fraud expensive regardless of whether you can prove it was fraud.* So within one chapter LZ holds Chris's rule for breach and the opposite rule for fraud, and the difference between the two is exactly an intent inquiry. (b) On FRB his own definition delivers the **vault's** conclusion and he refuses it. If fraud is deception, then a *disclosed* fractional reserve deceives no one — which is the [business-cycles page](../../economics/business-cycles.md)'s position: the Rothbardian "inherently fraudulent" claim is wrong as stated; the fix is an honest contract that says *your funds will be invested, you share returns, there is risk of loss*. LZ concedes this is not fraud, then recovers "fraud" by **stipulating the meaning of the word "bank"** ("a bank is a money-warehouse"). That is the definitional realism of the video's §7: a functional criterion (was anyone deceived?) overridden by an essence (what a bank *is*). Once the depositor has read the contract, the word on the door is not a deception.

## 3. Debtors' prison (§ *Debtors Prison*)

**LZ:** a defaulted loan is not theft. Not of the 1000 ounces (title passed to B at the contract — "if it was not transferred there would be no loan"); not *retroactively* (a property theory must assign a definite owner *at all times* — "Schrödinger's property" is expelled); not of the 1050 (they do not exist — "you cannot contract to transfer money that doesn't exist," so future-dated transfers are **aleatory**, with an implied *unless impossible*). If B has the sum and withholds it, *that* is theft. Vapourised-B has not robbed A from beyond the grave. Lenders protect themselves with interest clauses that transfer title the moment funds exist; "it is not the job of law to make sure that A makes a profit" — no debtors' prison.

**(vault) — good, and it commits him to two principles that bind later.** *A definite owner at all times* and *you cannot transfer what does not exist* are exactly the principles §5 has to survive. The vapourised-B case is also his first admission that **death ends the theory's reach** — "if nobody else exists it is nonsensical to speak of anyone being criminal towards A" — which is what the boundary page's edge question is about.

## 4. Voluntary slavery (§ *Voluntary Slavery*)

**LZ:** Block: *if I own it I can sell it; if I can't sell it I don't own it* ("full monte" alienability). Rothbard: the will is inalienable — a man can sell labour, not "the capitalised future value" of himself; submission that stays voluntary is not slavery, and submission enforced against a changed mind is not voluntary. Blockean replies: a lobotomy (or a future pill) *could* subdue the will, so the objection is technological, not principled; and the contract concerns the *body*, not the will. LZ's answer: (i) the argument-from-argument shows *just* possession of another's body is a contradiction, so a master cannot *justly* possess a slave; Block's K9 dog is disanalogous because the dog cannot act or argue and is "in the same legal category as a machine-gun"; (ii) **transfer requires abandonment, and you cannot abandon your body without making yourself brain-dead** — so title to a living body cannot pass; (iii) consent is **revocable**: the girlfriend who agreed to sex tomorrow and changes her mind — prior agreement does not license the act, "clearly … rape"; likewise a promise of obedience transfers nothing; (iv) Rothbard's will-inalienability, restated: *"demolition of the will would allow a body to be owned by another, but only because this demolition of will would render the individual analogous to a corpse."* Then legal vs catallactic ownership: labour is not *owned* ("what would it mean to exclude someone from your labour?"); economists mean control, lawyers mean just control; *"it is simply not the case that ownership implies selling or that selling implies ownership. I can sell a bitcoin, or an idea, or my labour, and yet I do not own any of these things … owning something does not imply the right to sell it."*

**(vault) — Chris's hand-wave worry: answered, not waved — and the answer is the vault's.** Argument (ii) *is* the [slavery test](../morality/legal-theory/self-ownership-and-property-rights.md#the-slavery-test): *slavery is wrong because you can't transfer exclusive bodily control; a slave contract is void because it promises what can't be delivered.* Argument (iii) is the vault's consent-vs-title distinction in his own example. And the closing paragraph concedes the [bodies-are-not-property](../morality/legal-theory/self-ownership-and-property-rights.md#bodies-are-not-property) bundle point outright — ownership is a bundle, bodies have the exclusive-control stick and not the transfer stick. Where the vault says *therefore it isn't property, it's the agent that owns property*, LZ says *therefore ownership doesn't imply saleability* and keeps the word. Same fact, one label apart — and the label is now doing no work he can name.

**(vault) — but the label's cost is in his own text, twice.** First, the **corpse clause**: a person whose will has been demolished is "legally analogous to a corpse" and *can* be owned by another — he calls the lobotomy route "not per se illegitimate." On a theory where title is the objective link between actor and body, everyone who is not an actor — the comatose, the profoundly demented, the anencephalic, and (ch. 5 will have to say) the not-yet-acting — has no link and is either ownable or "ceded to nature." That is the edge Chris predicted from the video's lifeboat section (*the theory breaks at conception/birth and death*), now explicit at the end of life and for the disabled. Second, the girlfriend case shows the body is governed by **revocable consent** and property by **irrevocable title** — he has drawn the second-root distinction Chris asked for, in his own example, and kept one word for both sides of it.

## 5. The last will (§ *The Last Will and Testament as a Contract*)

**LZ:** a severed arm is alienable (no link remains); so is the whole body at death. A will is a conditional transfer whose condition is the owner's death — *"the instant the condition is met Crusoe's body becomes alienable just as surely as a stick,"* and "third parties are not in contradiction by claiming to own him" because he no longer has the potential to argue. *"Should a man die who has no inheritors his property — including his body — becomes instantly ceded to nature, ready for someone to homestead it,"* so a man who wants his corpse treated a certain way must transfer title to family or a funeral home.

**(vault) — hub question 2, answered, at a price.** The answer: a will is a title transfer conditioned on death; intestacy is abandonment; no dead man binds the living, because the transfer completes *at* the instant of death. Three costs, all from his own rules. (a) **The no-gap rule (§1)**: a transfer requires the transferee to *take up control* at the same instant the transferor relinquishes it, or it is abandonment and "Xury could swoop in." At the instant of death the heir has taken up nothing. By §1 the will is an abandonment with a preferred homesteader, and the estate is a race. (b) **"A definite owner at all times" (§3)**: at the instant of death either the decedent still owns (but has no link) or the heir does (but has no control) — the Schrödinger's-property he expelled. (c) **Intestacy = homestead race**: the family home, the business, the body, "instantly ceded to nature" for whoever is fastest — Chris's "practical issues" at their sharpest, and a result no working legal system has ever adopted. The chapter also confirms **the corpse is property** that must be titled to be protected.

## Verdict

**What holds.** Title transfer as the theory of contract; *no breach, only the transfers you agreed to* (Chris's position); aleatory contracts and the end of debtors' prison; the slavery test, argued from abandonment and from revocable consent; the concession that ownership does not imply saleability.

**What does not.** Intent returns twice — as a condition of ownership (§1) and as the definition of fraud (§2) — against the intent-blind rule he holds for breach. Disclosed FRB is cleared by his own definition of fraud and re-convicted by stipulating what "bank" means (§2). The corpse clause makes the will-less *ownable* and the not-yet-acting undefined (§4). The will violates the no-gap rule and the definite-owner-at-all-times rule; intestacy is a race (§5).

**One-line read for the hub:** *his best chapter — the slavery argument is the vault's own — and the edges of a life are now in his text: destroy the will and you are a corpse; die intestate and your estate is a race.*
