# LiquidZulu Course, Ch. 3 — *Homesteading and Property Rights*
> The content chapter: the NAP finally says *who* the non-aggressor is — the first director. Then borders (objective, intersubjectively ascertainable), stalemates, the anti-Georgist reductio, self-ownership from the "mouthpiece" argument and Rothbard's trilemma, the impossibility of group ownership, the donut problem answered with de Jasay's *let exclusion stand*, a switch of definitions (ownership = just *direction*), and a coda against IP. **The finding:** every derivation in the chapter runs on a **false dichotomy** — first-comer *or* whoever-takes; labour-mixing *or* verbal decree; self-ownership *or* communism *or* castes; one owner *or* contradiction. The vault reaches most of the same conclusions **comparatively** ("least arbitrary"); LZ reaches them **apodictically** ("only non-contradictory"), and the second is what he cannot actually deliver. Plus one bullet bitten hard: enclosing land makes you owner of the *unused interior*, which is exclusion-as-ownership — the very thing the chapter's first argument denies.

## 1. Homesteading from the NAP (§ *Derivation from the Non-Aggression Principle*)

**LZ:** *ownership is assigned to the initial possessor/director of any external good* — the prior/later distinction. Crusoe and Friday presuppose that ownership ≠ direction (each says he *justly* directs). If B could own by taking from A, then C could own by taking from B, so *whoever controls, owns* — which collapses ownership into direction, contradicting what both presupposed. *"Therefore … initial direction is the only just direction."* Kinsella: *"the very idea of ownership implies that only libertarian-style ownership is justifiable."*

**(vault) — the retortion is good and the conclusion does not follow from it.** The premise establishes that *some* rule other than "the current controller owns" must hold — agreed, and it is the modest ownership/possession move the video review praised. But "not the current controller" is not "the first controller." Need-based assignment, allotment by lot, use-it-or-lose-it, Georgist use-with-rent, and collective assignment **all** distinguish ownership from possession. The argument's only reason for landing on *first* is that the alternative it considers is *taking*. That is a **false dichotomy**, and it is the pattern of the whole chapter. The vault's [self-ownership page](../morality/legal-theory/self-ownership-and-property-rights.md#the-better-derivation-conflict-avoidance) makes the *same* derivation and lands on first use — but as *"the least arbitrary resolution norm"*, after naming the alternatives. Comparative, defeasible, honest. LZ's version claims apodicticity it has not earned.

## 2. Borders, stalemates, Georgism (§ *Criteria for Property Borders*, *Homestead Stalemates*, *Against the Georgist Anti-Homestead Ethic*)

**LZ:** borders must be *objective* (or the rule "A may punch B but not vice versa" could be a property rule) and *intersubjectively ascertainable* (Crusoe shouting "I own the moon" homesteads nothing; the astronaut cannot perceive the claim) — *"communication lies at the very root of law."* Hoppe: only two ways to acquire the unowned, doing something with it or verbal decree, and decree cannot avoid conflicts. **Stalemate:** simultaneous grabs are *not* a conflict; neither action has begun "until the other backs off"; whoever *forcefully* excludes the other is the aggressor and forfeits; otherwise the less stubborn cedes. **Georgism:** to argue one must homestead at least standing room, so the anti-homestead ethic self-refutes; Hoppe: a latecomer ethic requires the consent of all posterity, hence "implies advocating death."

**(vault) — three notes.** (a) "Intersubjectively ascertainable" is the right criterion and it is **conventional**: whether a cairn, a fence, a blaze on a tree or a registry entry *counts* is fixed by the practice of the community, not the armchair — [constructed ≠ arbitrary](../constructed-not-arbitrary.md), and one more instance of discovery-as-generation admitted in the example. (b) The stalemate rule makes title depend on the *other party's* persistence: "the more stubborn man" wins, and "forcefully excludes" is undefined where both have a hand on the stick. That is a might-contest with a peace veneer, and it is also the first place **Chris's "initiation" question** bites — two hands, one stick, who initiated? Not answered here; carried to ch. 6. (c) The Georgist reductio proves that an arguer must *use* some scarce means — a liberty to stand somewhere — not that *permanent, transferable title* follows from standing there. Georgism as actually held permits possession and use and socialises *rent*; nothing in the standing-room argument touches it. Hoppe's "consent of all posterity" is a straw Georgist — no Georgist proposes consent, they propose a rule. Symmetric-grounding failure: the opponent is not steelmanned.

**(Chris, carried)** The punching example again treats a bodily rule as a *property-border* rule. The chapter never argues that assault is a property matter; it assumes it in an illustration.

## 3. Self-ownership (§ *Self-Ownership*)

**LZ:** bodies are "an entirely different type of object" — not found in nature awaiting a soul; "necessarily linked to an actor," and *that objective link* is what imbues ownership; homesteading is merely one way of demonstrating a link. The **mouthpiece argument**: for A to argue with B, A must presuppose B owns himself, else A is arguing with B's owner C. **Direct control beats indirect**: an enslaver controls only through coercion, and the slave's direct link is the superior one; a might-based assignment "authorises conflicts." Hoppe: the enslaver presupposes his own self-ownership in the act. Then **Rothbard's trilemma** — total self-ownership, universal co-ownership ("communist" — nobody may act without everyone's permission, and asking is an act), or partial ownership of one group by another ("particularistic … sub-humans ruled by humans"; not derivable from the nature of the entities; all humans *qua* acting beings have *the potential to argue*, so no group has a different logic).

**(vault) — the fact is the vault's; the label is not.** "Objective link" = *exclusive direct control* — the brute fact the [self-ownership page](../morality/legal-theory/self-ownership-and-property-rights.md#bodily-autonomy-grounded-independently) grounds autonomy on. Agreed, and "direct beats indirect" is the slavery test in different words. The disagreement is the word *ownership*. The mouthpiece argument shows B is an **independent decision-maker** — agency — and agency is not title: it is the control ≠ ownership objection from the lineage page, and LZ himself has just said bodies are "an entirely different type of object," then files them under the same concept anyway. That is Chris's ch. 1 worry in its purest form: **the label is chosen for tidiness ("ownership = just direction," §6), and the danger — that whatever is *owned* can be sold, pledged, or forfeited — is deferred to ch. 4 rather than faced.** Rothbard's trilemma is a false *trichotomy*: guardianship, self-ownership-with-duties, and every mixed regime in actual law are absent — and **ch. 5 will need one of them for children**, which will contradict "universal." Note also the word *potential*: universality is secured by "the potential to argue," which is exactly the move hub question 1 asks about (is potential not the intrinsicism he rejects?). He has now used it.

## 4. The impossibility of group ownership (§ *On the Impossibility of Group Ownership*)

**LZ:** if {A…Z} co-own a stick and A and B conflict, one is the just victor and the other therefore does not own it — contradicting co-ownership. Voting does not help (the loser did not own it; with two members every vote splits). Roderick Long's village path (labour mixed collectively) fails because labour-mixing is the wrong theory — Kinsella: creation is neither necessary nor sufficient, *occupation* is; Long's gift-to-the-public fails because contract theory follows property theory and cannot create contradictory titles; the polycentrist judge does not resolve it either; companies are "a web of contracts" with *one* nominal owner and conditional title transfers — "nowhere … is any property owned by multiple people."

**(vault) — the proof equivocates on "own."** It assumes that to own is to *win every use-dispute*. Co-ownership is a **bundle with internal rules** — use rotas, votes, buy-out, exit — and holding a share means holding a defined position in those rules, not a guarantee of victory. His own company paragraph concedes the point: a nominal single owner bound by conditional-transfer contracts to the shareholders' voted goals *is* co-ownership, re-described so the ledger shows one name. The institution is identical; only the bookkeeping changed. Against Long: the villagers *are* the first occupiers of the path — first possession by several — and "several cannot first-possess" is precisely what is at issue, so the reply is circular. The vault has **shared ownership** queued on the [legal-theory README](../morality/legal-theory/README.md#upcoming) and on the mens rea page's open questions; this chapter is the strongest ancap statement of the *no* answer and should be the foil there.

## 5. The donut, forestalling, and *let exclusion stand* (§ *The Blockean Proviso*)

**LZ:** Crusoe homesteads a ring 𝔸 around unowned 𝔹; Friday may enter 𝔹 but not 𝔸, and cannot reach 𝔹 without 𝔸 — Crusoe is *forestalling*, acting as owner of what he does not own. Block's camp answers with a proviso (no donuts; Dominiak: Crusoe₁₀₀, who seals the ring, is the criminal). LZ prefers Kinsella-via-de Jasay: property *is* exclusion; *"Crusoe in excluding others from … 𝔸 thereby becomes its owner"*, and the act of exclusion **is** his initial direction of *both the ring and the hole* — so Crusoe owns 𝔸 and 𝔹 the moment the fence closes, unless someone already occupies 𝔹 or ℂ, in which case the fence claims owned land and *is* forestalling. Aside: a property right in a gun is a right to *exclude* others from it, not a right to use it however — "who owns the gun isn't relevant to whether the shooting is just."

**(vault) — the bullet he bites contradicts §1 and §2.** Owning the unused interior by enclosure is **ownership by exclusion** — the ability to keep others out — which is exactly the *whoever-controls-owns* rule §1 called a contradiction. And the interior has no "objective, intersubjectively ascertainable" border of *use* — nothing was "done with" it, which §2's Hoppe quote made the criterion against decree. The fence is a decree with posts. The vault's [hierarchy-of-ownership](../../evolutionary-capitalist/hierarchy-of-ownership.md) assessment already put the point in one line: *"the only absolute is scarcity — exclusion is one response to it"*; LZ has promoted the response to the definition. The gun aside, meanwhile, is *correct* and cuts against him: if ownership is the right to **exclude** (de Jasay) and not the right to **direct**, then the chapter's chosen definition (§6) is wrong by its own best paragraph.

## 6. Direction vs possession (§ *Direction vs Possession: What is Ownership?*)

**LZ:** "just possession" fails the sofa case (the invited friend justly possesses the sofa; may you ask him to leave?). Rather than antecedent/descendant rights (workable, "a little bit tedious"), he redefines **ownership = just direction**, because it will later let *the right to retribute* and *the right of guardianship* be treated as **property rights** — "scarce, so our property theory has to cover them, but they aren't physical."

**(vault) — this is the sentence to remember for chapter 5.** Guardianship over a child is to be a *property right* in the direction of the child — declared here, in a terminological footnote, for convenience. That is the body-as-property danger Chris named, extended from one's own body to someone else's. The definitional switch also leaves the chapter with **two incompatible definitions** in play: de Jasay's *exclusion* (§5) and LZ's *direction* (§6). Antecedent/descendant rights would have handled the sofa without either; "tedious" is not a reason.

## 7. Intellectual property (§ *On the Impossibility of Intellectual Property Rights*)

**LZ:** ideas are not scarce (Friday can spearfish with a different stick and the same idea), so no property in them; IP = a criminal threat via the state; a consistent IP ethic needs permission to ask permission, ad infinitum → "the death of humanity."

**(vault)** On the scarcity criterion, agreed, and this is the standard Kinsella result. The regress is rhetoric — patents claim specific embodiments, not "ideas" — but Chris has set IP aside as too specific and nothing here changes the ledger.

## Verdict

**What holds.** Ownership ≠ possession as a presupposition of any dispute (§1); *intersubjectively ascertainable* as the border criterion (§2); exclusive direct control as the fact under "self-ownership," and direct-beats-indirect as the slavery test (§3); the gun aside — rights are exclusion rights (§5); IP on the scarcity criterion (§7).

**What does not.** First-comer is reached by excluding only *taking* (§1); Hoppe's "labour or decree" excludes every rule in between (§2); the stalemate rule rewards stubbornness and leaves "initiation" undefined (§2); Georgism is straw-manned (§2); the mouthpiece argument yields agency, not title, and the trilemma omits every mixed regime, including the one ch. 5 will need (§3); group ownership is "impossible" only under a definition his own company example abandons (§4); enclosure-owns-the-interior is ownership-by-exclusion, denied in §1 and contradicting §2 (§5); two definitions of ownership in one chapter, the worse one chosen so that guardianship can be property (§6).

**One-line read for the hub:** *the vault says first use is the least arbitrary rule and exclusion is one response to scarcity; LZ says first use is the only non-contradictory rule and exclusion is ownership — and his own donut and company examples side with the vault.*
